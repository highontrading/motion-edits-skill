# v31 — "5,127 prototypes": James Dyson persistence story (B/W photo-documentary with italic-serif word captions)

**Source creator handle:** earn.edits · **Format:** 720×1280, 9:16, 30 fps container, 39.4 s. The graphics are animated **on 12 fps**: distinct-frame intervals alternate 2/3 frames (196× and 161×), the same cadence as v05 · **Shots:** 16 real (17 detected; the 0.1 s "shot 3" at 4.47 is the white middle of a cloud transition, and 25.37 is a background dim plus overlay, not a cut) · **Avg shot:** ~2.5 s (median ~2.2 s) · **Music BPM:** ~108 (soft cinematic bed) · **VO:** yes, English, calm, measured male narrator

**Style tags:** documentary-archival, cutout-photo, 2.5d-parallax, footage-led, word-by-word-captions, serif-editorial, paper-texture, grain, vignette, slow-push, documentary-vo, cta-endcard, selective-colour-accent *(new)*, zoom-settle *(new)*

**One-line essence:** a quiet "biography poster" look. Monochrome AI-style photo scenes of the subject, each entering with a soft 110–118% → 100% zoom settle, carry small lowercase italic-serif captions synced word by word. One yellow accent (the vacuum, the newspaper headline, the CTA ring) is the only colour the eye gets, and it lands on the key nouns.

## 1. Concept & script structure
- **Genre:** founder or persistence micro-biography (a "failure count → struggle → result → moral → CTA" structure). It is the same creator as v05, but in a calm "prestige documentary" register instead of loud collage.
- **Beats:**
  - 0.0–5.9 **Hook:** "He failed 5,126 times before becoming a billionaire." A big number plus a paradox. The numbers sit in a sky → factory → cloud → money-office chain.
  - 6.0–10.3 **Problem / origin:** tired of vacuums losing suction, so he built his own (living room → patent drawings).
  - 10.8–19.8 **Struggle:** failed again and again and didn't stop. In total "5,127 prototypes" (workshop → hands writing the sentence on a notepad).
  - 20.1–22.3 **Contrast line:** "Most people would've quit long before that." The video goes empty: words on blank paper, no image.
  - 22.6–28.4 **Payoff:** a billion-dollar company that changed the industry (colour hero portrait → newspaper front page).
  - 28.0–34.3 **Moral:** success is about refusing to quit one attempt too soon. This switches to **real footage** of Dyson.
  - 34.6–39.4 **CTA:** "Editors, grab the After Effects project file on earnedits.com" (desk flat-lay endcard).
- **Hook mechanics (first 1.5 s):** a saturated blue sky (the only fully saturated shot until 22.5 s). A small plane flies in from the right towing a white banner that reads **he failed**, while the same words appear as italic-serif captions word by word above the plane ("he" at ~0.1 s, "he failed" at ~0.6 s). At 1.10 s a hard cut to **black-and-white** Dyson with "5,126 times" shows the colour drop on the number.
- **Script → visual rule:** each sentence gets one staged "photo" scene. The caption shows only the 1–3 words currently being spoken and never the full sentence. Literal props carry the noun (vacuum, patent drawings, motor, notepad, newspaper). The emotional reset line ("Most people would've quit…") gets **no image at all**.

## 2. Visual language
- **Art direction:** photo-real, staged, AI-generated-looking B/W scenes of the subject (factory exterior, money office, 80s living room, workshop, top-down desk). These are followed by a colour corporate portrait and then real interview and stage footage. Graphics are minimal and the imagery does the work. It is "documentary-archival" with a fashion-editorial caption layer.
- **Backgrounds & textures:** every image has fine film grain, white dust specks and a faint halftone/dot screen in the shadows (visible in key_shot05 and the footage in key_shot16), plus a soft dark vignette. One pure-texture scene uses warm cream paper (`#edd8ab` centre → `#b1a994` corners) with a radial light falloff. The CTA sits on light-grey grid paper.
- **Colour:**
  - 0–22.5 s is near-monochrome (sat ≈ 0–4 on a 0–255 scale) with luma swinging per scene: factory ~116, office ~50, living room ~57, workshop ~54 (with a slight brown and orange tint, sparks `#c46a2a`-ish), desk ~90.
  - **Single accent = signal yellow** `#d2b30d`/`#c9af26`. It is the vacuum cleaner (selective colour, 7.4 s), the folder in the factory shot, the newspaper headline highlight bar and the CTA ellipse ring. Yellow always marks "the thing".
  - Colour is reintroduced at the payoff ("Today, Dyson is a billion-dollar company", 22.5 s: sat jumps 4 → 36), and the real footage keeps natural, slightly warm colour (sat 54–92). Grade logic: **past = B/W, present/real = colour**.
- **Typography:**
  - **Caption face:** a high-contrast italic text serif (Editorial New Italic / Instrument Serif Italic / Lora Italic class), regular weight, **all lowercase** even for names ("today dyson", "james dyson"), tight tracking (≈ −2 to −3%). Size is small and elegant: cap height ≈ 3% of frame height (~38 px at 1280), and a 2–3-word caption spans 30–50% of frame width ("of vacuum" ≈ 30%, "would've quit" ≈ 49%). White `#f2f2f2` with a soft dark drop shadow (≈ 0 2px 8px rgba(0,0,0,.5)) over images. On the paper scene it is near-black `#1f1808` with a warm, blurred, offset shadow (≈ 4px 6px 10px, tinted `#c9a96a` at ~40%), so the type seems to float above the paper.
  - **Footage caption variant (28.5–34.8 s):** the same italic serif, white, inside a **tight black rectangle** (`#111` at ~90%, padding ≈ 4–6 px, no radius). This is a highlight-box subtitle that keeps legibility on busy, bright footage.
  - **Big number:** "5,127" in the same italic serif at ~25% frame width (≈ 3× caption size), white, placed below the notepad. Numbers get their own beat.
  - **Diegetic type:** the VO sentence is written on the torn notepad in a serif italic "handwriting" (Times Italic-like), with a hand-drawn strikethrough over "In total". The plane banner uses a bold grotesk (Helvetica/Arial Bold), black on white cloth. The newspaper has a blackletter masthead "The Daily News" and a condensed bold sans headline (Bebas Neue / Oswald Bold) "JAMES DYSON CHANGES THE WAY WE CLEAN FOREVER" on a yellow highlighter bar.
  - **CTA type:** "EDITORS" in a heavy italic display serif, ALL CAPS (Playfair Display Black Italic / DM Serif Display Italic), black, ~45% frame width, ringed by two thin yellow ellipses (an orbit or halo). It is then replaced by a black plaque badge with a 6 px grey bevelled border and drop shadow containing "grab the project file / on earnedits.com" in white bold italic serif, two lines, centred.
- **Layout & composition:** the subject is centred and full-height. **Captions sit at y ≈ 45–55% across the subject's chest**, centred, never in a lower third. The exceptions are the desk scene (caption under the notepad at y ≈ 78%) and the newspaper (caption above the paper, rotated with it at ≈ −5°). There is large negative space above the subject's head.
- **Assets:** cut-out plane with banner, AI-generated B/W portraits and sets, falling banknotes (particle props), patent line drawings as stacked paper sheets, a torn spiral-notepad page with hands and a fountain pen, a blueprint sheet, a fake newspaper front page, a real HQ photo with passing cars, real stage and interview footage, and a flat-lay of a MacBook, keyboard and iPhone.
- **Finishing / FX:** grain + dust + halftone on everything (including the footage, which unifies AI stills and real clips), vignette, and a soft shadow under each paper sheet. There is no glow, no chromatic aberration and no motion-blur trails except during the cloud move.

## 4. Motion & timing analysis
- **Pacing:** ~0.41 cuts/s. Shots run 0.9–4.6 s, median 2.2 s, which is **half the cut rate of v05**. The video breathes: long holds with slow settles, and the words carry the rhythm instead of the cuts. The fastest section is the footage montage (0.9–2.0 s shots). The slowest are the contrast line (2.7 s of static paper) and the CTA (4.6 s).
- **Animation on 12 fps:** all designed motion (plane, settles, paper drops, the yellow flicker) updates every 2–3 frames of the 30 fps file, which gives a subtly "printed / stop-motion" feel. The real footage plays at full rate.
- **Easing signature: the zoom-settle.** Almost every still enters already moving: it starts at ≈ 110–118% scale and decays to 100% with an exponential ease-out. Measured per-step zoom: −5.8, −3.7, −2.7, −2.0, −1.6, −1.2, −0.9 (14.43 s). Each 12 fps step is ~0.7× the previous one (≈ 0.87× per 30 fps frame, time constant ≈ 7 frames). 90% of the move is done in ~15 frames and the tail runs ~30–40 frames. Same shape at 6.0 s (Σ −23), 22.5 s (Σ −11), 34.8 s (Σ −13) and 1.1 s (Σ −11). Direction is almost always **zoom-out** (content shrinks into place). The office (5.0 s) and workshop (Σ +5.6) use a slow push-in instead.
- **Ease-in for exits:** the tilt into the clouds accelerates (pan_y per step 2.7 → 3.5 → 4.6 → 5.6 → 7.1 → 8.5 → 10.3 → 11.8 over 0.6 s), and the arrival on the other side decelerates (11.5 → 7.0 → 4.0 → 2.8 → 1.8 → 0.7 over 0.4 s). This is a classic in-out S-curve split across a white frame.
- **Entrance / exit vocabulary:**
  - zoom-settle in (30–40 frames).
  - Slide-in of the subject from off-frame (living room, ~0.9 s ease-out with ±1.5° rotational wobble).
  - Paper drop-and-stack (each sheet ~5–8 frames, slight rotation, shadow).
  - Spin-and-unfurl newspaper fly-in (~15 frames, small rotational overshoot).
  - Plaque badge pop (scale settle ~10 frames).
  - Caption word fade-on (~3 frames, no movement) and chunk clear (instant).
  - **Selective-colour flicker** (3 on/off flashes at 12 fps, ~0.5 s, then solid).
  - Scene dim-down (luma −60% over 5 frames) to push a scene back behind an overlay.
- **Camera:** a virtual camera on stills, made of zoom settles, slow pushes, one big vertical tilt through clouds and a punch-in/punch-out pair on the desk. The 2.5D separation of figure and background is shown during the factory zoom-out. There are no whip pans and no shakes.
- **Secondary motion:** falling banknotes, sparks from the motor, cars passing behind the hero portrait, the hand writing, the yellow rings orbiting the CTA word, and grain/dust flicker everywhere. **Every still has one small live element.**
- **Sync:**
  - Captions are word-synchronous. On the static paper scene, change frames vs Whisper word starts: most −0.06, people −0.07, quit +0.01, long +0.07, before ≈ +0.0, that +0.15 s. Captions appear within ±0.1 s of the word and are grouped into 1–3-word chunks that clear at clause boundaries.
  - **Cuts follow the VO sentence starts:** 5.97 vs "James" 6.02 (−0.05), 10.83 vs "It" 10.82 (0.0), 19.83 vs "Most" 20.06 (−0.23), 22.50 vs "Today" 22.60 (−0.10), 31.33 vs "It's" 31.48 (−0.15), 34.77 vs "Editors" 34.62 (+0.15). Cuts usually lead the sentence by 0.05–0.25 s.
  - **Cuts are also beat-snapped:** 11 of 15 cuts fall within ±0.16 s of a detected beat (1.10/1.18, 5.97/5.90, 9.37/9.47, 14.43/14.47, 22.50/22.62, 25.37/25.45, 30.43/30.35, 31.33/31.46, 34.77/34.81…).
  - The yellow flicker starts 0.08 s before "vacuum" (7.48) and goes solid on "cleaners" (7.78–7.90). The big "5,127" lands near the spoken number (Whisper 15.34; on screen ≈ 15.2).

## 5. Transitions catalogue
1. **Cloud tilt-through (vertical whiteout)** at 3.37–4.6 s. Frame by frame (cuts_01): the camera tilts up off the subject (accelerating), clouds fill the frame to near-white (luma 214) and hold for ~5 frames. The next scene is revealed **under** the clouds, which keep travelling upward and out (decelerating), so the motion is continuous through the cut. Total ~1.2 s / 36 frames. Rebuild: scene A translateY 0 → +60% with `Easing.in(Easing.cubic)` over 27 frames. A full-frame cloud PNG layer translates from below to cover by frame 27, holds 5 frames and continues upward over 12 frames with `Easing.out(Easing.cubic)` while scene B sits behind it. Swap A → B while covered.
2. **Hard cut on the sentence + beat** (most cuts). The incoming still always arrives with the zoom-settle, so a hard cut never feels static.
3. **Blur punch-in** at 16.53–16.67 s: 2–3 frames of soft blur on the wide shot, then a jump to a ~1.4× tighter framing with a sharp image. Rebuild: `filter: blur(6px)` ramp over 3 frames, then scale jump 1 → 1.4 at the cut.
4. **Punch-out with rotation** at 18.27 s: a one-frame zoom-out (−6.9) plus −1.6° rotation, followed by a continuous pull-back while new paper slides in.
5. **Dim-and-overlay** at 25.2–25.43 s: the background is darkened to ~40% luma over 5 frames (luma 89 → 66 → 35) and desaturated, then the newspaper spins in on top. This is a "scene within scene" without a cut. Rebuild: a black overlay at opacity 0 → 0.6 over 5 frames with `saturate(0.4)`.
6. **Caption carry-over across a footage cut** (31.33 s): the subtitle layer is independent of the clip edits and holds through the cut for 3 frames before swapping.
7. **Soft flash dissolve** between two footage clips (31.0–31.3 s): 2–3 frames of brightening/mix.

## 6. Sound design
- A soft, warm cinematic music bed at ~108 BPM (piano or pads feel; low RMS 0.13) sits under a calm VO. The music never competes with the voice.
- Strong onsets cluster at scene changes and reveals (1.28, 5.90, 9.47-ish, 14.19, 15.56 for the "5,127" number, 18.27 for the punch-out, 20.04, 22.62, 30.35, 37.29), which suggests soft whooshes on cuts and a hit on the big number. Paper and newspaper movements likely carry paper-rustle SFX.
- Silence or space: VO gaps of ~0.6 s before "James Dyson" (5.4 → 6.0) and before "In total" (13.4 → 14.1). Both gaps are filled by a scene entrance settle, not by text.

## 7. What makes it high-end (the transferable lessons)
1. **Monochrome + one accent colour.** Keep the whole story B/W and reserve a single signal yellow for the object that matters (product, headline, CTA ring). The eye goes exactly where the script points.
2. **Grade as timeline.** B/W = past/struggle, colour = present/success. Switch to colour on the payoff sentence.
3. **Small, elegant captions.** Lowercase italic serif at ~3% frame height, centred on the subject's chest, 1–3 words at a time. Restraint reads as premium. Big type is reserved for one number.
4. **Every still enters with a zoom-settle** (≈115% → 100%, exponential ease-out, ~1 s). The cuts can be plain because the image is always "landing".
5. **Give every still one live element**: falling cash, sparks, passing cars, a writing hand, orbiting rings.
6. **An empty frame for the emotional line.** Text on blank paper with no image makes the contrast sentence hit hardest.
7. **Unify AI stills and real footage** with the same grain, dust and halftone overlay and the same caption font. Switch captions to a black highlight box only when the background gets busy.
8. **Animate graphics on 12 fps** for a tactile, printed feel, while real footage stays at full frame rate.
9. **Motion that crosses the cut** (the cloud tilt-through) for the hook-to-story transition.

## 8. Remotion rebuild recipe
- **Composition:** `1080×1920`, `30 fps`, duration = VO + ~0.4 s. Global `<PosterizeTime fps={12}>` wrapper for graphic layers: `const f = Math.floor(frame / 2.5) * 2.5` (footage layers excluded).
- **Layer stack per scene (bottom → top):**
  1. `<StillPlate src grade="bw|colour">` (CSS `filter: grayscale(1) contrast(1.05)`).
  2. `<SelectiveColour mask>`: the coloured prop as a separate PNG above the B/W plate.
  3. `<LiveElement>` (particles, cars, sparks).
  4. `<Vignette strength={0.35}>`.
  5. `<GrainDust opacity={0.12}>` + `<HalftoneScreen opacity={0.06} cell={3}>`.
  6. `<SerifCaption>`.
- **Components:**
  - `ZoomSettle`: scale `interpolate(f, [0, 36], [1.15, 1], {easing: Easing.bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1), extrapolateRight: 'clamp'})`. An equivalent overdamped spring is `spring({frame, fps, config: {damping: 9, stiffness: 20, mass: 1}})` (time constant ≈ 7 frames). Apply it to every still at frame 0.
  - `SerifCaption`: takes the Whisper words and groups them into chunks of ≤3 words, breaking at punctuation. Each word: opacity 0 → 1 over 3 frames, starting at the word start − 2 frames. The chunk clears instantly at the next chunk start. Style: `font-family: 'Instrument Serif'; font-style: italic; text-transform: lowercase; font-size: 54px` (at 1080 w), `letter-spacing: -0.02em`, `color: #f2f2f2`, `text-shadow: 0 2px 10px rgba(0,0,0,.55)`. Position `top: 50%`. Variant `boxed`: `background: rgba(10,10,10,.9); padding: 2px 8px`. Variant `onPaper`: `color: #1f1808; text-shadow: 6px 8px 12px rgba(160,120,60,.45)`.
  - `CloudTiltThrough` (see §5.1): scene A `translateY` with `Easing.in(Easing.cubic)` over 27 frames. Cloud layer cover → hold 5 frames → reveal with `Easing.out(Easing.cubic)` over 12 frames.
  - `SelectiveColourFlicker`: coloured layer opacity `[1,0,1,0,1,0,1]` stepped every 2.5 frames from the keyword start − 2 frames, then solid.
  - `DimForOverlay`: black overlay 0 → 0.6 over 5 frames + `saturate(0.4)` on the BG.
  - `NewspaperFlyIn`: from `translate(-40%, 30%) rotate(-60deg) scale(0.6)` to `rotate(-5deg) scale(1)` with `spring({damping: 11, stiffness: 120, mass: 0.8})` (small overshoot), plus a fake unfurl using `perspective(800px) rotateY(40deg → 0)` over 12 frames.
  - `PaperStackDrop`: N sheets, each starting 4 frames apart, from `scale(1.12) rotate(±6deg) opacity(0)` to `scale(1) rotate(±2deg)` over 6 frames with `Easing.out(Easing.quad)`. The shadow grows from 0 to `0 8px 20px rgba(0,0,0,.4)`.
  - `OrbitRingTitle`: two SVG ellipses (`rx = 0.6·textWidth`, `ry = 0.18·textWidth`, stroke `#d2b30d` 2 px) rotating at ±1.5°/frame around a heavy italic-serif word.
  - `PlaqueBadge`: `background: #1b1b1b; border: 6px solid #8a8a8a; box-shadow: inset 0 0 0 2px #555, 0 10px 24px rgba(0,0,0,.35)`. Pop in with scale 1.08 → 1 over 10 frames.
- **Tricky bits:**
  - Separating figure and background for the 2.5D zoom: cut out the subject (rembg/SAM) and scale the BG plate 1.18 → 1 while the subject goes 1.12 → 1.
  - Passing cars: a short car PNG translating across a mid-plane layer between the BG and the cut-out subject.

## 9. Variation ideas
1. **Founder/brand origin stories** (e.g. "Airbnb was rejected 7 times"): B/W staged scenes, with the product in the accent colour (Airbnb coral instead of yellow), the newspaper front page as proof and a colour switch on "today".
2. **Personal finance or habit moral** ("Compounding rewards whoever doesn't quit"): replace the portraits with objects (coin jar, calendar), keep the empty-paper moment for the moral line and render the big number as the hero.
3. **User-footage edit (athlete or creator recap):** grade the user's old clips B/W with grain, keep the italic-serif chunk captions, and switch to colour plus black-box captions when the recent clips start. Add zoom-settles to every photo and a selective-colour flicker on the user's brand item.
