# v11 — "Your brain has a glitch": the Scarcity Effect (12 fps collage explainer with zoom-punch cuts)

**Source creator handle:** earn.edits · **Format:** 720×1280, 9:16, 29.97 fps container, but **every visual change, including the live footage, is stepped at 12 fps** (new image every 2 or 3 frames, alternating; 146×2-frame and 144×3-frame gaps measured), 38.3 s · **Shots:** 16 real (19 detected; detector cuts 8–12 inside the Supreme footage are a mask recolour and stepped-footage jumps, and it missed a punch-in at 8.78 s) · **Avg shot:** ~2.4 s (median ~2.1 s; four 0.17–0.83 s logo flash cards pull the average down) · **Music BPM:** ~108 (bed under VO) · **VO:** yes, English, calm-confident narrator

**Style tags:** collage-explainer, cutout-photo, halftone, grain, vignette, kinetic-type, 3d-extrude-type, stacked-type, word-by-word-captions, typewriter, hand-drawn-doodle, ui-mockup, device-mockup, notification-ui, footage-led (one segment), zoom-through, slow-push, documentary-vo, cta-endcard (proposed: on-twos-12fps, zoom-punch-cut, tracked-face-mask, logo-color-flash)

**One-line essence:** every VO clause gets its own textured photo-collage set, and every set is joined to the next by the same "accelerate-out / decelerate-in" zoom punch, all stepped at 12 fps. The video feels hand-animated and punchy, yet each cut lands within ±0.08 s of the first syllable of its clause.

## 1. Concept & script structure
- **Genre:** marketing-psychology micro-explainer (same series grammar as v05: hook → named principle → definition → 3 brand proofs → reframe → moral → CTA).
- **Beats:**
  - 0.0–1.3 **Hook:** "Your brain has a glitch." A shocked man's scalp flips open and his brain pops out.
  - 1.8–5.8 **Setup:** marketers found it and have used it on you ever since.
  - 6.4–8.1 **Named principle:** "This is the scarcity effect." It arrives as a fake macOS dialog box at 17% of runtime.
  - 8.7–14.2 **Definition chain:** limited → valuable → urgent. Two parallel clauses, the second set on black.
  - 14.7–24.9 **Proof ×3, accelerating:** Amazon (2.1 s), Airbnb (4.1 s), Supreme (4.2 s split into footage + 4 flash cards).
  - 25.1–28.4 **Reframe:** "The product isn't rare, the feeling is engineered."
  - 28.4–32.8 **Moral:** on black, in three text beats.
  - 33.0–38.3 **CTA:** "Editors get the project file… earnedits.com" over a phone mockup.
- **Hook mechanics (first 1.5 s):** frame 0 shows only a tuft of hair at the bottom of a flat yellow wall. At 0.10 s the man **pops up from below frame and overshoots large** (zoom per 12 fps step: +1.9, +0.5, then −4.2, −4.4, −3.4, −2.6 … settling by 1.43 s). His face is motion-blurred for ~3 steps. "YOUR / BRAIN / HAS / A GLITCH" rise in with blur from 0.23 s. At 0.47–0.93 s the **top of his head hinges open like a lid** and a cut-out brain pops up out of the skull. Two floating white UI cards (">>>" and "Glitch") sit behind him. That is four things moving in under a second, on a saturated yellow that stops the thumb.
- **Script → visual rule:** one set per clause. The clause's **noun becomes a real-object prop** (brain, notebook, iMac, locker poster, plane window + Airbnb passport, Supreme footage, notepad). Its **key phrase becomes heavy type laid into the set's surface**: printed onto paper, behind a held card, or inside a UI notification. Abstract "thought" lines (valuable → urgent, the moral) go on **black**.

## 4. Motion & timing analysis
- **Pacing:** 0.42 cuts/s. Shots run 2–5 s through the explainer and are broken by a 0.17–0.27 s logo-flash burst at 23.8–24.4 s (the fastest moment, on "of thirty thousand"). Text changes inside a shot keep the visual change rate near 1 event per 0.5 s.
- **The 12 fps step:** every change (zooms, text, even the live footage) updates on an alternating 2/3-frame cadence = 12 fps. Motion reads as deliberately hand-made and punchy, and motion blur is baked into each step to stop it looking choppy.
- **Easing signature (measured per 12 fps step):**
  - **Entrances** are exponential ease-out: the first step is the biggest, and each later step is ×0.80–0.88 of the one before. They settle in 10–14 steps (0.8–1.2 s), e.g. shot 3 zoom +1.88, 1.50, 1.26, 1.14, 0.98, 0.92, 0.81, 0.76 …
  - **Exits** are exponential ease-in into a hard cut: 4–6 steps (0.3–0.5 s) growing ×1.5–1.7 per step, e.g. 11.34–11.78 s: −0.37, −0.65, −1.02, −1.53, −2.41, −6.61.
  - **No overshoot on camera moves.** The overshoot lives in the props (man popping up, brain popping out).
- **Entrance / exit vocabulary:**
  - pop-up-from-bottom with scale overshoot (man, 3 steps up then 10 steps settle)
  - hinge-open lid (scalp, ~5 steps)
  - word blur-in (opacity 0.4 → 1 + blur → 0 over 2–3 steps)
  - typewriter per character (WhatsApp, ~11 chars/s)
  - hand-drawn ellipse write-on (~5–6 steps / 0.45 s)
  - horizontal slide with smear (in: 3 steps decel; out: 2–3 steps accel)
  - fade-up from dark (gold block, 0.5 s)
  - object carried across the cut (brain, shot 1 → 2)
  - flat background colour swap behind a fixed logo (5–8 frames per colour)
- **Camera:** every set has a virtual camera. It is a slow decaying push-in or pull-back after the entry, never static (mean flow 0.3–0.7 even on "still" shots). There is one deliberate **S-curve re-frame** to land on the circled keyword (shot 14). The footage segment keeps its own handheld bob.
- **Secondary motion:** sunburst rotation; magnifier drifting and tracking the typing caret; floating translucent dialog duplicates with depth blur; smoke plume drifting on the CTA; site screen recording scrolling inside the phone.
- **Sync:**
  - **Cuts are word-locked.** Measured offset from the cut to the first word of the clause: −0.01 (Marketers), +0.01 (they've), +0.02 (This), +0.08 (When), −0.08 (Amazon), −0.04 (Airbnb), +0.02 (Supreme), +0.06 (product), −0.03 (Editors). Median |offset| ≈ 0.03 s, so the picture changes on the syllable.
  - Beats are a secondary lock: 14.78, 16.92, 21.02 and 28.86 also sit within ±0.01 s of a beat. At 28.86 the cut follows the beat, not the word "They" (28.36), so the moral starts on a downbeat.
  - On-screen words appear within ±0.15 s of the spoken word in most sets. Exceptions: the hook (text trails by ~0.2 s while the man arrives) and the Airbnb card (the 3-line block resolves 0.3–0.9 s ahead of the VO).

## 5. Transitions catalogue
1. **Zoom-punch cut** (the signature; 1.77, 3.77, 6.34, 11.78, 16.92 s). The outgoing shot accelerates its scale for 4–6 steps (0.3–0.5 s, ×1.6 per step), then a hard cut; the incoming shot opens mid-motion and decelerates (×0.85 per step) for ~1 s. Rebuild: on the last 12 frames of the scene, `scale = 1 - 0.12 * easeInExpo(t)`. On the first 30 frames of the next scene, `scale = 1.08 → 1.0` with `Easing.out(Easing.exp)`. Add directional blur proportional to velocity.
2. **Object carry-over** (1.77 s): the brain is the last thing to move in shot 1 and the first and only object in shot 2, on the same screen position. Rebuild: keep the prop in a persistent layer above the scene switch.
3. **Punch-in cut** (8.78 s): the same iMac set, cut ~15% closer when the UI layer swaps (dialogs → WhatsApp banner).
4. **Slide-out to the right with smear** (14.68–14.75 s): the text line translates ~+40% of frame width in 2–3 steps with a horizontal motion blur, and the cut hits mid-motion.
5. **Logo colour-flash sequence** (23.76–25.26 s): the logo stays fixed while the background swaps yellow → blue → white → black every 5–8 frames, ending on an inverted (white-on-black) logo held for 0.83 s.
6. **Hard cut to black statement card** (11.78, 28.86 s): resets the eye before abstract sentences.
7. **Fade-up from dark** (28.9–29.4 s): the gold type ramps from ~20% to 100% over ~0.5 s.

## 6. Sound design
A soft, mid-tempo music bed (~108 BPM, steady 0.55 s beat) under clear VO with no long pauses (max ~0.6 s gap, e.g. 1.26–1.76 s). Strong onsets cluster on visual events, which points to SFX on them: the dialog pop-ins (6.43, 6.78, 6.99, 7.20 s, a rapid 4-hit cluster = UI pops), the zoom-punch cuts (14.77, 21.0, 25.05/25.29 s), the Supreme flash cards (24.17 s) and the black-card moral (28.86 s). The WhatsApp typing almost certainly carries keyboard ticks. The music bed keeps running under the CTA.

## 7. What makes it high-end (the transferable lessons)
1. **One transition grammar for the whole piece.** Accelerate out of every shot, decelerate into the next. A single consistent move makes 16 unrelated photo sets feel like one camera.
2. **Step the animation at 12 fps** with baked motion blur. It reads as handcrafted collage, hides compositing seams, and makes moves punchier than smooth 30 fps.
3. **Type lives inside the set:** printed on the notebook at its perspective, multiplied into a poster, sandwiched between the hand and the sky, typed into a notification. The words feel like they belong to the world.
4. **Cuts on the first syllable** (median 0.03 s). Text appears on its word. The beat is used only to place the big "moral" cut.
5. **Black statement cards every ~10 s** with a coloured keyword and a hand-drawn ellipse give rhythm and a breather between dense collages.
6. **Brand proof via authentic artefacts:** real UI patterns (macOS modal, WhatsApp banner), real footage with deliberately "found" YouTube captions, and the real Supreme box logo, then a burst of colour flashes on the big number.
7. **A tracked graphic mask on footage** (the balaclava) turns stock or found footage into a branded and anonymised character with one overlay.
8. **Every set gets a two-colour accent rule** and one prop. Nothing generic, nothing flat.

## 8. Remotion rebuild recipe
- **Composition:** `1080×1920`, `fps={30}`, duration = VO + 0.3 s. Render at 30 but quantise animation time to 12 fps: `const f12 = Math.floor(frame / 2.5) * 2.5;` and feed `f12` into every `interpolate`/`spring`. For footage, use `<OffthreadVideo>` with `playbackRate` normal but hold frames with `startFrom` + a stepped `frame` (or pre-convert the clip to 12 fps with ffmpeg `-r 12` and then `-r 30`).
- **Scene stack (bottom → top):** `<SetPlate src="desk|lockers|grid" />` (flat-lay photo) → `<Cutout src halftone />` props (CSS `mask-image` with a radial-dot SVG pattern at ~3 px pitch, or a pre-processed PNG) → `<SurfaceType text rotate={-12} blend="multiply" color="#1f3b33">` → foreground occluder (hand/card PNG) → `<GrainOverlay opacity={0.08} />` → `<GateVignette radius={40} />` (inset box-shadow `0 0 60px 20px #000` with rounded corners).
- **Components:**
  - `ZoomPunchOut({durationInFrames: 12})`: `scale = interpolate(t, [0,1], [1, 0.82], {easing: Easing.in(Easing.exp)})`; `filter: blur(${v*0.6}px)`.
  - `ZoomSettleIn({durationInFrames: 30, from: 1.12})`: `Easing.out(Easing.exp)`, or a decaying multiplier `0.85^step`.
  - `PopUpFromBottom` (hook): `spring({frame: f12, fps: 30, config: {damping: 11, stiffness: 140, mass: 0.8}})` for translateY 60% → 0 and scale 1.35 → 1.0.
  - `HingeLid` (scalp): `rotate` −0 → −35° around the lid's left edge over 6 frames at 12 fps, with brain `translateY` −15% and a `spring` overshoot.
  - `WordBlurIn` (each word: opacity 0.4 → 1, `blur(8px)` → 0 over 3 steps, start = word.start − 0.03 s).
  - `TypewriterBanner` (chars/sec derived from the VO span; caret position exported for the `Magnifier` to follow, lens = circle clip of a 1.4× scaled copy of the layer underneath).
  - `EllipseWriteOn` (SVG path, `strokeDashoffset` 100% → 0 over 14 frames, `Easing.out(Easing.quad)`, red `#e0201a`, 4 px, slightly open loop).
  - `SlideSmear` (translateX ±40% in 6 frames, SVG `feGaussianBlur stdDeviation="20 0"`).
  - `LogoColorFlash({colors: ['#eace46','#2c5c97','#ededed','#070707'], frames: [5,8,7,25]})`.
  - `TrackedMask` (PNG balaclava with holes; position/scale/rotation keyframes from a tracker JSON, e.g. exported from Mocha/AE or computed with MediaPipe face landmarks).
  - `YouTubeCaption` (Source Sans 600, 40 px at 1080 w, `background: rgba(0,0,0,.55)`, padding 4/10 px).
- **3D extrude type:** 6–8 stacked `text-shadow: 1px 1px 0 #8a8a8a, 2px 2px 0 #7a7a7a …` plus a soft drop `0 8px 12px rgba(0,0,0,.35)`.
