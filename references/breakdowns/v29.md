# v29 — "Her beauty vs. her brain": Hedy Lamarr and frequency hopping (archival-collage mini-doc on a CRT/print finish)

**Source creator handle:** earn.edits · **Format:** 720×1280, 9:16, 30 fps file, but **animated on 3s** (picture updates every 3rd frame ≈ 10 fps for most of the video; 551 of 960 frames are exact duplicates), 32.0 s · **Shots:** 22 real (24 detected; 5.00 s is the router's signal rings expanding, and 15.07 s is the crumple continuing a 3-frame pre-roll) · **Avg shot:** ~1.45 s (median ~1.3 s) · **Music BPM:** ~89 (soft bed, beat period 0.67 s) · **VO:** yes, English, calm male documentary narrator

**Style tags:** collage-explainer, cutout-photo, documentary-archival, footage-led, serif-editorial, typewriter, label-badge, scanlines, chromatic-aberration, halftone, grain, depth-of-field, hand-drawn-doodle, counter-ticker, frame-in-frame, cursor-ui, search-bar-cta, documentary-vo, cta-endcard, stop-motion, posterized-frame-rate, defocus-transition, selective-color

**One-line essence:** a quiet "untold history" story told in pale paper-grey and black, where each clause gets one isolated image or a single italic-serif word, and the whole thing runs through a CRT-scanline, halftone and RGB-fringe filter and is posterised to 10 fps, so cut-outs, archival footage and UI look like one analogue print.

## 1. Concept & script structure
- **Genre:** micro-biography / "forgotten genius" history explainer with an ironic-reversal structure (bookended antithesis: beauty → brain).
- **Beats:**
  - 0.0–1.6 **Hook:** "Everyone remembered her beauty." A black void with one small word, then a glamorous 1950s cut-out labelled "her beauty".
  - 1.6–5.7 **Twist:** almost no one realised she helped create the technology behind your Wi-Fi (crowd → patent typing → router).
  - 5.7–9.9 **Context:** WWII, actress Hedy Lamarr, who "wasn't just making films" (archival footage, glamour still, then one word per beat on blank paper).
  - 9.9–15.0 **Invention:** a communication system that stops the enemy jamming torpedoes (radio tower, sailors, torpedo).
  - 15.0–16.8 **Rejection:** the military ignored it (the drawing crumpled into a bin).
  - 16.8–23.3 **Vindication:** years later it became the foundation for Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and GPS (year counter, chalkboard, single words, badges).
  - 23.3–27.5 **Moral / bookend:** the world remembered her beauty, but history remembers her brain (colour film stills → gilt-framed portrait with caption).
  - 27.5–32.0 **CTA:** "Editors, grab the After Effects project file on earnedits.com" (editor silhouette → AEP icon click → website search bar).
- **Hook mechanics (first 1.5 s):** 0.00–0.67 s is near-black (luma 11) with a faint grid texture and a tiny chromatic-fringed sans word "EVERYONE" (~35% frame width) whose last letters rise into place, so the eye has to lean in. At 0.67 s a hard cut to a bright paper-grey frame (luma 11 → 138 in 1 frame) shows the cut-out woman on a red carpet. The jump from dark to light is the scroll-stopper, and a second cut at 1.07 s punches in on her face with a defocus-to-sharp.
- **Script → visual rule:** one clause = one image or one word. Nouns become literal props (router, radio tower, torpedo, bin, chalkboard, picture frame). Abstract connective phrases ("wasn't just making films", "became the foundation for technologies") get **no picture at all**, only one italic-serif word at a time on empty paper. The edit alternates "busy collage frame" with "empty paper + one word" as its breathing rhythm.

## 2. Visual language
- **Art direction:** documentary collage. Cut-out B/W photos (pin-up woman, crowd of men), real archival footage (colourised WWII troops and sailors, Technicolor film stills of Lamarr), simple line illustrations (lattice radio tower, router, torpedo), real artefacts (the 1942 frequency-hopping patent drawing with the Kiesler-Markey/Antheil signatures), and UI pieces (text-selection handles, AE file icon, hand cursor, website search bar). Each element sits alone in a lot of empty space.
- **Backgrounds & textures:**
  - Default ground: flat **light paper-grey `#bfbebe`–`#c2c1c1`** (shots 2–4, 9, 14, 16, 20). It reads as a CRT showing white.
  - Dark ground: near-black `#070707`–`#0b0b0b` with a faint 1-px grid and dust specks (hook, badges), and a deep teal-black `#051416` for the router scene.
  - Beige patent paper `#bcb1a3` (shot 5), a plaster wall with a framed chalkboard (shot 15), and a saturated blue ocean/sky `#25567f → #4282b5` gradient with soft vertical pink light streaks (torpedo).
  - **Global finish on every frame:** fine horizontal **scanlines** (~1 line per 3–4 px at 720 px), **halftone dot** texture visible in footage and on the green bin, **RGB chromatic fringing** of 1–2 px on all edges (text, tower lines, badges), light grain and dust specks. This one filter unifies cut-outs, footage and UI.
- **Colour:** mostly monochrome (paper grey + black). Colour appears only on meaning: red carpet and red signal arcs (`#d96a6a`-ish salmon-red with stripes), torpedo turning **red** when it is "jammed", the **green bin `#0a7a35`/`#075025`** as the only colour in a B/W war room (selective colour), **signal-blue badges `#247db5`/`#2a8fd6`**, and the gilt frame gold `#91703c`/`#c9a33a`. The final colour film (teal `#0e5c66`, skin, jewels) is the warmest moment and is saved for the "beauty" bookend.
- **Typography:**
  - **Primary: high-contrast italic serif**, condensed and editorial (Instrument Serif Italic / Editorial New Italic / Playfair Display Italic-like), in sentence or title case: "her beauty", "almost / no one / realize", "During WW2", "wasn't / Just / Making / Films", "Communication System", "jamming torpedoes", the years, "Became / the / Foundation / Technologies", "history remembers / her for her brain.", "Editors". Colour is near-black `#1e1e22` on paper and off-white `#e8e8ec` on footage. Size is deliberately **small**: single words are 12–25% of frame width (e.g. "Foundation" ≈ 22%), annotation labels ≈ 30%, the two-line moral ≈ 45% wide. Tight tracking (≈ −2%).
  - **Secondary: neo-grotesk caps** (Helvetica Neue Medium / Inter SemiBold-like) for "tech" or "machine" text: "EVERYONE", the typed "SHE HELPED CREATE THE TECHNOLOGY", "BEHIND YOUR WI-FI.". Off-white `#d8d8d8` on black, black on patent paper, around 30–55% of frame width.
  - **Two-tier lockup:** a small italic kicker "designed a" (≈ 12% width) sits directly over a larger "Communication System" (≈ 65% width) in a narrower serif. The second word is first set in 40% grey, then fills to black as the VO reaches it.
  - **Label badge:** "Wi-Fi / Bluetooth / GPS" in bold sans (Arial Rounded / Helvetica Bold-like), white with a dark 2 px drop shadow, on **flat blue rectangles `#2a86c8` with a 2 px white inner stroke and an offset dark shadow**, each ≈ 30–40% of frame width.
  - **Torn-paper label:** "jamming torpedoes" sits on a ragged white paper strip (rough deckle edges), the newspaper-clipping trope.
  - **Emphasis rule:** no colour highlights in the type. Emphasis comes from isolation (one word per frame), from the blue text-selection highlight on "TECHNOLOGY", and from grey-to-black fill on the last word ("System", "brain.", "WI-FI.").
- **Layout & composition:** strictly centre-weighted. Single-word captions sit at the optical centre (y ≈ 50–53%). Annotations hug the subject ("her beauty" + a hand-drawn curved arrow pointing at her face, top-left of the head). Captions over a scene sit at y ≈ 36–40%, above the subject (crowd, torpedo, tower), or directly under a framed picture (y ≈ 68%). Typical negative space is 60–80% of the frame.
- **Assets:** B/W cut-out photos with hard edges, colourised WWII footage, Technicolor film stills, the patent drawing, line-art radio tower, a white consumer router, a torpedo cut-out, a crumpling paper sheet, a green plastic bin, a framed chalkboard, a gilt picture frame, an iOS-style text-selection highlight with blue handles, an Adobe AE ".AEP" file icon, a hand (pointer) cursor, the earnedits.com homepage, Google Drive/Gmail icons.
- **Finishing / FX:** scanlines, chromatic aberration, halftone, grain, **depth-of-field defocus used as a transition device** (blur ramps of 3–6 frames into and out of shots), soft contact shadow under the bin and frames, gentle dark vignette on the dark scenes. Footage is graded slightly faded (lifted blacks ~`#141316`), with low-saturation colourisation.

## 4. Motion & timing analysis
- **Pacing:** 22 shots in 32 s (0.69 cuts/s), median ~1.3 s. Fast in the hook (0.4–0.5 s shots), even 1.0–1.7 s through the story, and longest at the vindication and moral (badges 2.7 s, framed portrait 2.2 s). The calm VO and the many empty-paper frames make it feel slower than its cut rate.
- **Frame-rate signature:** nearly all motion updates **every 3rd frame** (gaps of 3 frames dominate: 183 occurrences, phase-locked). Moves read as stepped and "printed", like stop-motion. Grain and the typing caret occasionally update on 1s.
- **Easing signatures (per 3-frame step):**
  - Camera zoom-outs and lateral settles are **exponential ease-out** with a decay ratio of ~0.6–0.7 per step: shot 13 bin reveal (−6.4 → −1.0 over 10 steps), shot 20 frame reveal (−5.0 → −0.6 over 6 steps), shot 23 CTA slide (−13.1 → −0.7 over 10 steps). That fits `Easing.out(Easing.exp)` or `bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` over 20–30 frames.
  - Push-ins are gentle ease-outs (shot 15: +1.44 peak at step 2, then decaying, net +5.8 over 1 s).
  - Shot 7 is the exception: a **linear constant** zoom-out with roll (≈ −3/step and −2°/step for 13 steps), which reads as a drifting archival "Ken Burns".
  - No overshoot anywhere. No sign flips in the zoom/pan data within shots, except at the router (+3.2 on the frame its rings start). The tone is calm and documentary.
- **Entrance / exit vocabulary:**
  - Per-letter rise (EVERYONE): letters rise from ~30% cap height below, staggered ~1 step.
  - Word fade+unblur (almost/no one/realize; history remembers…): opacity 0 → 1 and blur ~6 px → 0 over ~6 frames, staggered by word timing.
  - Single-word hard swap with a scale-up (wasn't/Just/Making/Films; Became/the/Foundation): each word appears at 70–80% scale and grows to 100% in 2–3 steps (6–9 frames). The previous word is cut, not faded.
  - Typewriter caps + caret (patent) at ~28 chars/s, the italic letter-type (During WW2, Editors) at ~12 chars/s, and the search-bar type at ~30 chars/s.
  - Text-selection highlight: a blue fill grows L→R across the word in 3 steps (9 frames) with handles at both ends.
  - Scrambled-order letter reveal on the torn-paper label (~9 frames).
  - Grey-to-black fill on the final word of a lockup ("System", "brain.", "WI-FI.").
  - Rise-from-bottom props (router, torpedo) take ~6 frames, ease-out.
  - Slide-from-right badges take ~6 frames with motion blur, and so does the tower slide-in.
  - Radial grow of signal arcs: scale 0 → 1 over ~10 frames, with arcs drawn as striped wedges.
  - Stop-motion crumple: 3–4 discrete paper states.
  - Odometer year roll.
  - Exits are almost always hard cuts or **defocus-outs** (3–8 frames). Objects rarely animate out; the torpedo tumble is the exception.
- **Camera:** mostly locked or slow scale moves. Zoom-out reveals carry meaning ("pull back to reveal it's in a bin / in a frame"). One rolling zoom-out on the war footage. One whip-in on the CTA. No shake.
- **Secondary motion:** jewel sparkles on the Lamarr footage, sonar rings, expanding signal rings, the blinking text caret, and the 10-fps jitter itself, which gives a constant low-level "alive" flicker.
- **Sync:**
  - Cuts land **0.0–0.15 s before** the first word of their clause (e.g. cut 5.67 → "During" 5.72; 6.97 → "actress" 7.06; 15.07 → "The" 15.14; 25.27 → "history" 25.30; 23.27 → "The" 23.34).
  - Badges land within 0.06–0.2 s before their word.
  - Single-word and typewriter captions **trail** the spoken word by 0.15–0.35 s ("wasn't" spoken 8.04, shown 8.37; "Just" 8.64 → 8.80; "she" 2.80 → typed 3.17). The VO leads and the type echoes it.
  - Music: the beat is slow (0.67 s period) and cuts sit within ±0.1 s of a beat only about half the time (5.67, 6.97, 8.27, 20.57, 25.27). The edit is **VO-driven, not beat-driven**.

## 5. Transitions catalogue
1. **Dark → light hard cut** (0.67 s): luma 11 → 138 in one frame. A high-contrast scroll-stopper and act break. Rebuild: just cut, no fade.
2. **Punch-in with defocus** (1.07 s): cut to a ~1.8× tighter crop of the same cut-out, arriving at blur ~10 px and sharpening in 3 frames. Rebuild: `filter: blur(interpolate(f,[0,3],[10,0]))` with `scale 1.8` on the same asset.
3. **Defocus-out bridge** (1.47, 4.53, 23.1, 25.0 s): the outgoing shot blurs over 3–8 frames, then cuts (or the incoming shot starts blurred and resolves). It is the house "soft cut" for this calm tone.
4. **Blur-to-zoom-out reveal** (25.27 s): the outgoing close-up blurs, the incoming shot is the same image already framed but oversized and blurred, which then zooms out to ~0.6 scale and sharpens with expo-out over ~20 frames. It reads as "the memory becomes a museum portrait". Rebuild: one continuous `scale` from 2.4 → 1 with blur 14 → 0 on the framed group, and swap the frameless image for the framed composite at the cut frame.
5. **Stop-motion crumple + zoom-out reveal** (14.97–16.0 s): a 3-frame establishing close-up of the drawing, then sequential crumple states while the camera pulls back to reveal a bin, and the ball sinks into it. Rebuild: a sprite sequence of 4 crumple PNGs on 3-frame holds plus a parent scale 3 → 1 (expo out).
6. **In-scene slide-on (no cut)** (9.87 s): the next illustration slides in over the same paper ground, so "Films" and the tower share a set.
7. **Whip-in with exponential settle** (30.17 s): the incoming site screenshot enters laterally at ~52 px/step and decays by ×0.68 per step.
8. **Same-ground word swaps** (8.27–9.87, 18.87–20.57 s): consecutive single words on identical paper, swapped on hard frames. The "cut" is the word change itself.

## 6. Sound design
A soft, slow music bed (~89 BPM, low RMS 0.13). The first strong onset only arrives at 3.4 s, so the hook is nearly VO-only. Likely SFX, based on onset clusters that line up with visual events: keyboard typing under the patent typewriter (3.1–4.3 s) and the CTA search bar (30.3–31.2 s); a soft whoosh for each badge slide (20.6, 21.6, 22.4 s, onsets at 21.9/22.0); a paper crumple at 15.1–15.6 s; a click on the AEP icon (~29.1 s, onset 29.07); and a muted impact at 25.24 (the frame reveal). The VO is continuous with ≤0.6 s pauses; the longest gap (22.74 → 23.34 s) sits before the bookend line and acts as a dramatic breath.

## 7. What makes it high-end (the transferable lessons)
1. **One global "medium" filter** (scanlines + halftone + 1–2 px RGB split + grain + 10-fps posterise) welds cut-outs, archival footage, line art and UI into one coherent object. Apply it last, over everything.
2. **Alternate dense and empty frames.** After every image-heavy shot, give the viewer a blank paper frame with a single small word. Restraint reads as confidence.
3. **Small italic serif, not giant grotesk.** For history and biography, an editorial italic at 12–25% width feels literary and premium. Reserve caps sans for "machine" text (typing, tech words).
4. **Colour only where it means something:** monochrome base, with a single colour per scene for the semantic object (red jammed torpedo, green bin, blue tech badges, the gilt frame).
5. **Pull-back reveals as punchlines:** zoom out to show the drawing is in a bin, or the beauty shot is in a museum frame. The camera move is the joke or the moral.
6. **Defocus is the transition.** Blur ramps of 3–8 frames replace crossfades and fit a calm narrator.
7. **Literal UI metaphors for invisible ideas:** text-selection highlight on "TECHNOLOGY", typing caret, file icon + cursor click.
8. **Bookend the script** (beauty → brain) and repeat the visual motif (Lamarr glamour) at the end, in colour this time.
9. **Expo ease-out with no overshoot** for every move keeps the tone documentary rather than playful.

## 8. Remotion rebuild recipe
- **Composition:** `1080×1920`, `30 fps`, ~960 frames (VO + 0.3 s). Wrap the whole tree in a `<PosterizeTime fps={10}>` (use `const f = Math.floor(frame / 3) * 3` and pass it down through context; keep the caret, grain and sparkles on real frames).
- **Layer stack per scene (bottom → top):** `<PaperGround color="#c0bfbf" | "#080808" | img>` → `<Cutout | OffthreadVideo>` (footage gets `filter: saturate(0.75) contrast(1.05)` + lifted blacks) → `<SignalArcs>` / props → captions → `<CRTFinish>`, which combines a scanline layer (`repeating-linear-gradient(0deg, rgba(0,0,0,.08) 0 1px, transparent 1px 4px)`), a halftone dot pattern on footage (an SVG `<pattern>` circle grid at 5 px, `mix-blend-mode: multiply`, 25% opacity), RGB split (three copies of the frame via an SVG `feColorMatrix` channel-isolate + `feOffset dx=±1.5`, `screen`-blended) and `<Grain opacity={0.07}>`.
- **Components:**
  - `<SerifWord text at={wordStart+6}>`: Instrument Serif Italic, 64–90 px at 1080 w, `#1e1e22`. Entrance: scale 0.75 → 1 and opacity 0 → 1 over 6 frames, `Easing.out(Easing.cubic)`, posterised. Hard cut out when the next word starts.
  - `<WordFadeLine words>`: each word gets opacity 0 → 1 and `blur(6px) → 0` over 6 frames at its whisper timestamp; the last word holds at 40% grey for 9 frames, then goes to full.
  - `<LetterRise text>`: per letter `translateY(0.3em → 0)`, 2-frame stagger, opacity 0 → 1 over 3 frames (EVERYONE).
  - `<Typewriter text cps={28} caret>`: caret blink 15 frames on/off; the parent `translateX` follows the text width beyond 70% of the frame.
  - `<SelectionHighlight target="TECHNOLOGY" frames={9}>`: the fill `#9cc3dd` width animates 0 → 100%, with 2 handles (a 3 px line + a 10 px dot in `#1e88d0`) at the ends.
  - `<RiseIn from="bottom">` for props: `translateY(110% → 0)` over 6 frames, `bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1)`.
  - `<BadgeStack items={['Wi-Fi','Bluetooth','GPS']} at={[...wordStarts]-4}>`: each badge `translateX(120% → 0)` over 6 frames, with an SVG horizontal blur `stdDeviation="12 0"` falling to 0. Style: `background:#2a86c8; outline:3px solid #fff; outline-offset:-6px; box-shadow:6px 6px 0 #0b2233`; text is bold sans white with a `2px 2px 0 #0b2233` shadow.
  - `<SignalArcs>`: SVG wedge arcs (striped via a `repeating-radial-gradient` mask), scale 0 → 1 over 10 frames. For the router, concentric rings expand with `scale` 0.6 → 1.6 and opacity 0.5 → 0, looping every 20 frames.
  - `<OdometerYears from={1940} to={1990} steps={[1940,1945,1965,1970,1985,1990]}>`: a vertical list with `translateY` stepping, each step 3–6 frames, accelerating; outgoing opacity 0.4.
  - `<CrumpleReveal frames={['flat','crumple1','crumple2','ball']}>`: 3-frame holds; parent `scale` 3 → 1 over 30 frames with `Easing.out(Easing.exp)`; the ball then drops `translateY` 0 → 30% over 12 frames into a `<Bin>` mask (clip-path at the bin rim).
  - `<FrameReveal>`: the gilt frame + image group goes `scale 2.4 → 1`, blur 14 → 0 over 20 frames (expo out); the caption follows with `<WordFadeLine>`.
  - `<DefocusTransition frames={6}>`: outgoing blur 0 → 12 px; incoming optional blur 10 → 0 over 3 frames.
  - `<CursorClick path to icon at>`: a hand cursor travels a bezier path over ~20 frames; on click the icon scales 1 → 0.9 → 1 over 6 frames.
  - `<SearchBarCTA text="earnedits.com" cps={30}>` over a blurred screenshot (`blur(6px)`, brightness 0.6), entering with `translateX(25% → 0)` over 30 frames, `Easing.out(Easing.exp)`.
- **Tricky effects:** the halftone over footage is best done by rendering the footage to a canvas and thresholding into dots via a WebGL shader (`@remotion/three` or a custom `<Canvas>` shader). A cheap alternative is a multiply-blended dot pattern plus `contrast(1.2)`. Selective colour (the green bin in a B/W room) means desaturating the background plate and leaving the cut-out in colour.

## 9. Variation ideas
1. **Founder/science micro-biographies** ("The nurse who invented the ER triage tag"): same CRT/halftone finish, archival footage, one-word serif beats, and a gilt-frame-and-caption ending. Change the semantic colour per story (e.g. medical red cross).
2. **Product-origin stories for SaaS** ("Why the Slack logo is a hashtag"): keep the empty-paper word beats and the selection highlight on the product noun, swap badges for product-feature chips in brand colour, and end with the search-bar CTA to the product URL.
3. **Personal-finance myths** ("Everyone thinks rent is wasted money"): a dark → light hook cut, B/W cut-out people, one green or red colour for the money object (green bin → green cash), an odometer ticker for figures, and a framed "receipt" reveal as the moral.
