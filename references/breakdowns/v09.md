## 1. Concept & script structure
- **Genre:** marketing-psychology micro-documentary (the creator's recurring format: "brand paradox → reveal what they really sell → principle → moral → CTA").
- **Beats:**
  - 0.0–4.5 **Hook (paradox):** the product tastes bad, everyone knows it, nobody argues about it.
  - 4.5–8.1 **Stakes:** "…and somehow" it became a $50B empire (big number on a nostalgic XP desktop).
  - 8.1–13.5 **Turn:** it's not what's inside the can, it's what the can means; they never sold energy.
  - 13.5–20.7 **Evidence list:** identity, speed, risk, adrenaline (type on the brand flag) → F1, cliff diving, space jump (real footage, 3 clips in 2.4 s).
  - 20.7–30.4 **Principle:** they engineered culture; competitors copied caffeine/sugar/packaging but "nobody could copy the feeling".
  - 30.4–35.4 **Universal law:** people buy the version of themselves a product represents (shopping basket).
  - 35.4–40.8 **Moral:** never marketed a drink, marketed a lifestyle (footage) → "And that changes everything" (black card).
  - 40.8–46.0 **CTA:** "EDITOR / get the project files…" → "EARNEDITS.COM".
- **Hook mechanics (first 1.5 s):** frame 0 is a blown-out white glare with a ghosted, rotated can. Over 0.1–0.6 s the can pulls back out of the glare (scale down + rotation settle + exposure falling: mean luma 195 → 167), and green hills appear beneath. By 0.7 s the red word "TASTES…" slides in from the left **behind** the can, and by 0.93 s a second line in navy follows. Three layers move against each other in the first second: the glare, the product, and the type sandwiched behind it.
- **Script → visual rule:** each sentence gets a new register. **Claims** get a collage poster with a literal prop (can, toy F1 car, basket of status items). **Abstract pivots** ("And somehow", "because of what the can means", "Every competitor copied…", "And that changes everything") get a black or navy card with only type. **Proof** gets real footage with small subtitles. Nouns in a list get their own framing (SPEED / RISK / ADRENALINE; CAFFEINE / SUGAR / PACKAGING).

## 4. Motion & timing analysis
- **Pacing:** 0.59 cuts/s overall, but in three gears. Poster scenes run 2.2–5.0 s. The list sections run fast: the flag reframes 0.7–0.9 s, the footage burst 0.34–1.2 s (5 cuts in 3.3 s at 35.4–38.9). Statement cards hold 1.0–2.3 s. The CTA is the longest shot (5.2 s).
- **Frame cadence:** all animation, including camera moves on footage, updates on 2s and 3s (~12 fps), so even smooth eases look slightly stop-motion. In a Remotion rebuild, quantise time with `Math.floor(frame / 2.5) * 2.5` (for 30 fps output) on graphic layers.
- **Easing signatures (measured on per-frame flow):**
  - *Hero entrances* (can, basket, phone): short ramp of 3–4 steps (~0.3 s) to peak velocity, then a long exponential decay of 1.2–1.5 s. **No sign flip anywhere, so no overshoot.** Equivalent: `Easing.bezier(0.33, 0, 0.12, 1)` over 36–57 frames @30.
  - *Canvas reframe snaps* (flag 14.78 / 15.48 / 16.35): **accelerate → 1-frame snap → decelerate**. Ease-in over ~0.5 s (velocity ×10, from 0.13 to 2.1 analysis px), a single blurred frame (flow 21–29 ≈ 85–115 px/frame at 720 w), then ease-out over 0.4–0.5 s (half-life ~2 steps).
  - *Camera tilt in the basket shot:* symmetric ease-in-out, 1.2 s (`Easing.inOut(Easing.sin)`).
  - *Ring travel:* ease-in-out moves of 0.3–0.6 s between 0.4–0.5 s holds.
  - *Type:* focus-pull reveals of 4–7 steps (0.35–0.7 s), ease-out; whole blocks keep a slow 3–5% scale-down while they hold.
- **Entrance / exit vocabulary:** glare pull-back (overexposure → normal), slide-behind-object type, paint-on vertical type revealed by a passing object, focus-pull word-by-word, scanner-bar wipe, RGB pixel-block materialise, typed text field with horizontal scroll, blurred chat bubble resolve, decelerating reframe snap, rise-from-bottom with rotation settle, snap-up exit with blur (CTA). Exits are almost always hard cuts; the content does not animate out.
- **Camera:** every graphic scene has a virtual camera: slow pull-backs (shot 1 net_zoom −24, shot 4 −19), drift (XP desktop −140 px), a 3-region reframe on one canvas (flag), ring travel and a tilt (basket ~25°). Footage is mostly left as shot.
- **Secondary motion:** condensation droplets on the can, slight float on the phone, a constant slow scale on held type, flicker of film-gate scratches on the last black card.
- **Sync:** cuts are locked to **VO word onsets, not the beat**: median |cut − word start| = 0.046 s (21 of 27 within 0.10 s), versus a median of 0.13 s to the nearest beat with a 0.67 s beat period (no better than chance). Cuts land 0–0.07 s *after* the word starts. The first word of each statement card appears with its spoken word. The rest of the phrase builds at ~0.23 s/word, so the last word often lands 0.3–0.7 s before it is spoken (INGREDIENTS on screen at 24.29 s vs spoken 24.98 s), which leaves reading time.

## 5. Transitions catalogue
1. **Hard cut on the word** (most cuts): no transition FX; the cut itself is the beat. Cut frame = word onset + 0–2 frames.
2. **Canvas reframe snap** (14.78, 15.48, 16.35 s): one large layout (flag with logo + 3 keywords) is shown as three crops plus a wide. Frame by frame: 7 frames of accelerating push → 1 frame of directional/zoom blur → the new crop already ~85% settled → 10–14 frames of deceleration. Rebuild: render the master canvas at 2–3× frame size and animate a camera transform between region keyframes with `Easing.in(Easing.cubic)` (15 frames) + `Easing.out(Easing.cubic)` (14 frames), swapping at peak velocity; add `filter: blur()` scaled by velocity.
3. **Cut to black statement card** (4.50, 23.52, 38.94 s): a hard cut to pure black; the first word appears at 30–40% grey and is blurred.
4. **Glare reveal** (0.0–0.6 s): opening from an overexposed white bloom. Rebuild: `brightness(2.2) blur(12px)` → `brightness(1) blur(0)` over 18 frames on the hero layer, plus a white radial-gradient overlay fading out.
5. **Light-streak flash** inside footage (17.42 s): 2 steps of diagonal white light bars over the F1 clip, used as an in-clip accent rather than a scene change. Rebuild with an additive (`mix-blend-mode: screen`) light-leak PNG translated diagonally across 4 frames.
6. **Glitch materialise** (8.41 s): the can appears as RGB-split pixel blocks for 2 steps before resolving. Rebuild: 3–4 offset copies with `clip-path` slices and hue-rotated R/G/B, randomised per step.
7. **Snap-up swap** (42.88 s CTA): the current line moves up ~25% with vertical blur in 1–2 steps while the URL slides in from the right with blur and settles in ~0.5 s.

## 6. Sound design
- A mellow ~89 BPM bed under a calm, unhurried narrator. Unlike the fast reads in v05, there are 0.2–0.6 s pauses between sentences, and the cuts sit in those pauses.
- Strong onsets coincide with cuts within ±0.05 s at 2.27, 4.50, 9.94, 11.95, 14.78, 16.35, 20.75, 25.86, 38.94 and 40.77 s, which suggests a whoosh or soft impact SFX on most scene changes. Dense onsets in 30.7–32.4 s match the items dropping into the basket (likely small drop/pop SFX).
- Likely typing SFX under the XP text field (5.5–6.8 s) and the CTA subline.

## 7. What makes it high-end (the transferable lessons)
1. **Three registers, strictly assigned:** collage poster = claim, black type card = pivot or abstraction, real footage = proof. The viewer subconsciously learns the grammar.
2. **Sandwich the keyword behind the hero object.** The occlusion adds depth for free and makes cheap renders feel art-directed (can, basket rim).
3. **One master canvas, many crops.** For a list of 3 words, design one composed poster and snap the camera between regions. It reads as 4 shots but stays visually coherent.
4. **Accelerate into the cut, decelerate out.** Every camera move has an ease-in tail before the snap and a long ease-out after it. No linear moves, no overshoot.
5. **Step the animation at 12 fps** and put a halftone + grain screen over everything, so stock, AI renders and footage share one printed texture.
6. **Pull the palette from the subject's brand** (Red Bull navy/red/gold) and keep footage desaturated so the graphics own the colour.
7. **Cut on the word, not the beat**, with median offset ~0.05 s after the onset. Build multi-word statements slightly ahead of the VO so the full line holds for reading.
8. **Nostalgic UI as a metaphor layer** (XP windows for "empire", iMessage for "they engineered it") adds a second reading without extra script.

## 8. Remotion rebuild recipe
- **Composition:** `1080×1920`, `30 fps`, duration = VO + ~0.4 s. Wrap graphic layers in a `<Stepped fps={12}>` helper that passes `Math.floor(frame / 2.5) * 2.5` to children (footage stays at 30).
- **Typical poster scene stack (bottom → top):** `<SetBackground src blur={depth}>` → `<KeywordStack lines colors behind>` → `<CutoutHero src shadow>` → optional `<ForegroundDepth blur>` → `<HalftoneOverlay dot={3} opacity={0.12}>` → `<Grain opacity={0.07}>` → `<Vignette strength={0.35}>`.
- **Components:**
  - `GlarePullBack({durationInFrames: 57})`: scale `1.35→1`, rotate `-8°→0`, `brightness 2.2→1`, `blur 14→0`, eased with `Easing.bezier(0.33,0,0.12,1)`.
  - `KeywordBehind({lines, delayPerLine: 7})`: each line `translateX(-40%)→0` with `blur(10px)→0` over 8 frames, z-index below the hero.
  - `FocusPullWord({word, startFrame})`: `opacity 0.3→1`, `filter: blur(8px)→0`, `scale 1.06→1`, 10–20 frames ease-out. A `StatementCard` maps VO word timestamps to `startFrame = round(word.s*30) - 1` and caps the gap to the next word at 7 frames, so the phrase finishes early.
  - `CanvasReframe({regions:[{x,y,scale,at}], snapBlur: 24})`: interpolates a camera transform between regions with `Easing.in(cubic)` for 15 frames before `at` and `Easing.out(cubic)` for 14 frames after, applying `blur(snapBlur * normalisedVelocity)`.
  - `RingTour({labels, radius, stops})`: SVG circle stroke `#c98a5a` 3 px; badges positioned on the circumference; camera rotate/translate between stops with `Easing.inOut(Easing.cubic)` (12–18 frames), then a final zoom-out (`scale 2.4→1`, 30 frames).
  - `ChatThread({messages})`: iPhone frame rises (`translateY 40%→0`, `rotate 3°→0`, 33 frames, ease-out). Each bubble enters with `blur(10px)→0` + `opacity` over 12 frames at word onset − 6 frames.
  - `XPWindow({title:'Form1', clip})` plus `ScrollingTextField({text, cps: 18})`.
  - `GlitchMaterialise({src, frames: 5})`: RGB-split slices.
  - `FootageSubtitle({chunks})`: 1–3 word chunks, sentence case, 34 px at 1080 w, at y = 62%; `background: rgba(0,0,0,.35)` only when footage luma is > 150.
- **Tricky bits:** for the paint-on vertical type (shot 2), mask the rotated text with a rectangle whose edge follows the car's tail position each frame (`clipPath` inset driven by the car's `translateY`). For the halftone, use an SVG `<pattern>` of circles in `mix-blend-mode: multiply` at ~12% opacity, or a fragment shader in `@remotion/three` for true luma-driven dot size.

## 9. Variation ideas
1. **Sneaker/streetwear brand story** ("Nike never sold shoes"): the same three registers. The shoe is the cut-out hero on a painted set with the keyword sandwiched behind it, black cards for the pivots, and athlete footage with chunk subtitles; reframe the swoosh poster for "JUST / DO / IT".
2. **Creator-economy explainer** (why a YouTuber's merch outsells their ads): XP or iMessage UI as the metaphor layer, the ring tour for "views, likes, subs → trust", and the basket shot reworked as a cart of merch.
3. **Personal finance** ("People don't buy houses, they buy status"): maroon/cream paper scenes, a cut-out house in a shopping basket, and green-accent statement cards; replace the sports footage with lifestyle B-roll from the user's own clips, subtitled the same way.
