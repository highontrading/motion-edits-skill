## 1. Concept & script structure
- **Topic / genre:** "everyday object history" micro-documentary for a brand-agnostic audience (the barcode / UPC), with an editor-marketplace CTA.
- **Narrative beats:**
  - 0.00–3.52 **Hook:** "Every time you buy something, you probably look at it without thinking." A supermarket POV grab of a red chip bag.
  - 3.94–8.18 **Reframe:** "Those little black lines are one of the reasons modern shopping works…" The bag flips to its barcode on "Those" (4.0 s), then a self-checkout scan.
  - 8.96–12.64 **Before:** prices tracked by hand (a vintage grocer tagging a can).
  - 13.10–18.18 **Turn / date:** "Then, in 1974, a pack of chewing gum became the first product ever scanned." A date card, then a newspaper headline as proof.
  - 18.72–24.18 **Significance:** "It seemed almost insignificant… but suddenly every product could have its own digital identity." Single words between two scanners, then a conveyor of products being scanned.
  - 24.72–32.64 **Payoff list + thesis:** checkout faster / inventory easier / "those little black lines became the language of modern retail" (the callback to the hook phrase is the title line).
  - 33.14–38.40 **CTA:** "Editors, your next client job starts inside this project file, earnedits.com."
- **Hook mechanics (first 1.5 s):** frames 0–6 are a blown-out, defocused white plate (luma 215) that steps down to colour in 2-frame increments (215 → 191 → 160). Frames 9–10 (0.30–0.33 s) are a desaturated, half-inverted flash frame of the same aisle. At 0.37 s a saturated red bag (#c72834) is thrust toward the lens. From 0.43–0.6 s the camera cranes up fast (pan_y −9 to −10.5 per step ≈ 40 px/frame at full res), revealing the shopper from a top-down angle by 1.07 s. So: a white flash, a glitch frame, a hero-colour product and a big camera move all land before the first VO clause ends (1.28 s).
- **Script → visual rule:** every noun phrase gets one AI-generated photoreal scene showing it literally (shopper, checkout, grocer, newspaper, conveyor, Nutella scan). On-screen type appears only on abstract or summary lines ("insignificant", "checkout became faster", "those little black lines / the language of modern retail", the CTA) and is word-synced there.

## 2. Visual language
- **Art direction:** footage-led, built entirely from AI-generated photoreal stills and image-to-video clips (consistent "product photography" lighting, top-down tabletop compositions), plus a few cut-out props with sticker outlines (scanners, barcode label, AE file icon, phone). No flat vector anywhere. The b-roll is on 2s (every other frame is a duplicate in 0–18.4 s and 21.7–24.8 s: 15–17 of every 30 frames have diff < 0.5), while the motion-graphics scenes (18.4–21.7 s, 24.8 s–end) update every frame. This mix makes the AI clips read as deliberately "stepped" rather than glitchy.
- **Backgrounds & textures:** each graphic scene sits on a textured "surface" seen from directly above: rotating radial-dash vortex on black (shot 2), orange cutting-mat grid under soft window-blind shadows (shot 6), dark navy denim (shot 7), dark brown grid with a red cast (shot 8), charcoal felt (shot 9), a white stitched fabric patch on red velvet (shot 10), white/red felt tabletop (shot 11), white halftone paper (shot 12), and a cyan blueprint grid with drifting clouds (CTA). The surface changes on every scene, and the hue changes with it.
- **Colour:**
  - Hero product red `#c72834` / `#b52c2b` carries through the whole first act (Lays bag, laser, scanner glow, red velvet), so red = "the scan".
  - Darks: `#1b191a`, `#2a2829`, `#212025`, `#241c1c`. Warm history browns: `#2e201e`, `#5f3e33`, `#8e5f4e`. Orange grid: `#8e421c` / `#773616`. Conveyor grey: `#333233` / `#4c494a`. Thesis paper white: `#e4e4e7` / `#d5d6d9`. CTA blueprint: `#163f62`, `#111e31`, `#447796`, with cyan type ≈ `#36c6ee`.
  - Rule: one dominant surface hue per scene + one saturated accent (red laser, yellow highlighter `#e8e04a`, cyan CTA type). The grocer shot is warm sepia and is graded darker over its last 0.7 s (luma 85 → 59) to sink into the date card.
- **Typography:**
  - **Serif italic single words** (Playfair Display Italic / Libre Caslon Italic-like), white `#f2ece6`, lowercase, ~30–35% of frame width ("insignificant."), centred at y ≈ 44%. Only one word is on screen at a time. No shadow, just the halftone overlay.
  - **Date numerals:** "1974" in a condensed italic grotesk (Druk Condensed Italic / Bebas Neue italic-like), white, ~22% of width, slightly tilted (~−8°).
  - **Thesis type:** a heavy, wide display sans with soft corners (Bowlby One / Lilita One / Dela Gothic-like), ALL CAPS, charcoal `#2b313a` on white with a 2–3 px blue/orange chromatic offset and a soft drop shadow. Two-line stacks: "THOSE LITTLE / BLACK LINES" ~75% of width at y ≈ 9–13%, and "THE LANGUAGE OF / MODERN RETAIL" ~65% under the barcode. The second line is the same size or larger.
  - **Patch type:** "CHECKOUT BECAME" small (~45% width) over "FASTER" big (~55% width, ~2.2× cap height), black, slightly italic, with a strong soft drop shadow (offset ~6 px down, blur ~8 px, 60% black), so it reads as embossed onto fabric.
  - **CTA type:** "EDITORS" white, ~75% width, same wide display face with an outer glow, then "YOUR NEXT / CLIENT JOB" in cyan at ~0.5× the cap height, stacked tight under it.
  - **Emphasis rule:** at most three words per stack, and the noun (FASTER, LINES, RETAIL, EDITORS) is the biggest or last element.
- **Layout & composition:** top-down symmetric tabletop compositions with the hero object dead centre. Diagonal symmetry for the two scanners (top-right / bottom-left, lasers converging on the centre word). Type sits in the upper third on light scenes (to leave the prop below) and dead centre on dark scenes. The CTA stacks title, icon and phone along the centre axis.
- **Assets:** AI photoreal scenes (supermarket aisle, self-checkout POV, 1970s grocer with Campbell's cans, newspaper "The Washington Post — Pack of Wrigley's Gum Becomes First Product Scanned by Barcode" with a Juicy Fruit photo, conveyor of branded packs), cut-out handheld scanners with a white sticker outline, a barcode label, a glassmorphic inventory card ("INVENTORY · Nutella · 36 In Stock · ✓ Product"), scan-frame corner brackets, an AE "AEP" file icon pushed in by a real hand, an iPhone mockup of the earnedits site, and a search pill.
- **Finishing / FX:** a fine **halftone dot screen** over every frame (visible on all key frames), film grain, heavy vignettes on dark scenes, a **circular spotlight iris** (soft-edged pool of light, outside ~70% black) on the newspaper and the flipped bag, white sticker outlines with a slight RGB fringe on cut-outs, soft contact shadows, red laser cones with additive glow, and a slight barrel "bulge" on the stitched patch.

## 4. Motion & timing analysis
- **Pacing:** 0.34 real cuts/s; shots 1.6–4.1 s, median ~2.8 s. It is slower than the creator's collage pieces because each AI clip carries its own camera move. Pace tightens in the payoff list (2.0, 2.0, 2.65, 1.6 s) and the CTA gets 5.7 s.
- **Easing signatures:**
  - **Settle-zoom** is the house move. Every new scene starts slightly over-scaled and decays out: shot 3 zoom per step −9.9, −5.4, −3.7, −3.0, −2.4, −2.1, −1.9, −1.6 (a ratio of ~0.8 per step, i.e. an exponential ease-out with τ ≈ 6 frames); the CTA −3.0 → −0.4 over 25 f; the phone −3.1 → −0.3 over 25 f; shot 10 −2.0 → −0.55 over 12 f. No overshoot (no sign flip), pure ease-out.
  - Exits use **ease-in momentum**: the arm in shot 3 accelerates over 15 frames (pan_y −1.8, −2.8, −4.5, −6.9, −7.1, −9.4) and the cut happens at peak velocity.
  - The conveyor is a symmetric ease-in-out of ~22 frames per step, 1.0 s period.
  - Type slams (FASTER) are 1-frame impacts with a 3–4 frame settle. Word-by-word pops are ~3 frames. Serif single words just cut/fade (≤ 3 f).
- **Entrance / exit vocabulary:** digit-by-digit build (1974, ~2 f/digit); paper slide-in with rotation (newspaper, 10 f); highlighter sweep (16 f); spotlight-iris close (~6 f, soft edge); push-toward-camera (1.2 s); corner slide-in of cut-outs (8 f); single-word swap; card pop from an object (8 f, scale 0.4 → 1); a hand-pushed icon (10 f); device rise (12 f); search-pill grow + typewriter.
- **Camera:** every AI clip has a built-in move (crane-up, pull-back, push-in). Graphic scenes use slow zoom: net_zoom −56 (vortex), −38 (grocer), +18.6 (1974), −42 (barcode poster incl. slide), −64 (CTA). There are no whip pans. The one lateral move is the 20-frame slide-push at 31.4 s.
- **Secondary motion:** tunnel rotation (~70°/s), slow drift/rotation of the cut-out scanners, laser-cone flicker, drifting clouds in the CTA, auto-scrolling website, and a blinking caret.
- **Frame-rate texture:** the AI b-roll is held on 2s (15 fps). The graphics are at 30. This contrast makes the "documentary" footage feel filmic and the type feel crisp.
- **Sync:**
  - Cuts lead the VO phrase by 0.0–0.3 s: 8.93 vs "Before" 8.96 (−0.03), 13.00 vs "Then" 13.10 (−0.10), 18.43 vs "It" 18.72 (−0.29), 21.73 vs "every" 21.88 (−0.15), 24.80 vs "Check" 24.72 (the flash ramp starts at 24.70, so 0.0), 33.00 vs "Editors" 33.14 (−0.14).
  - The visual punchline lands on the keyword: the bag flips to the barcode at 3.95–4.0 on "Those" (3.94), and the cut to the barcode scan lands on "lines" (4.98 → 5.00).
  - Word-synced type is frame-accurate in the thesis scene (±0.05 s) and lags in the CTA (+0.1 to +0.6 s).
  - Music: cuts are VO-driven but half sit within ±0.12 s of a beat (8.93/8.99, 18.43/18.32, 33.00/33.00, 5.00/4.88). Strong onsets coincide with the bag flip (3.95), the 1974 build (13.14–13.23), the cut at 18.46, the conveyor step (22.27), the circle wipe (26.68) and the CTA cut (33.02), which suggests a whoosh/impact on each.

## 5. Transitions catalogue
1. **White exposure fade-in + inverted flash frame** (0.00–0.37): frames 0–6 overexposed white with heavy defocus, stepping down to colour on 2s; then 2 frames of a desaturated/negative version; then full colour. Rebuild: `brightness(3) blur(12px)` → `brightness(1)` over 7 f with a stepped interpolation, then 2 frames of `invert(0.6) saturate(0.2)`.
2. **Portal / black-hole morph** (1.73–2.13): saturation drops to ~30% over 6 f, a black circle grows from the subject's position to fill the frame in ~4 f, and the next scene (a black tunnel) is already inside it. Rebuild: a radial `clipPath: circle(r)` with r 0 → 120% over 6 f with an ease-in, the next scene inside the circle, and a desat on the outgoing layer.
3. **Spotlight iris** (3.95, 16.6): a soft-edged circular vignette closes from the frame edge to ~70% of the frame width in ~6 f and stays as a pool of light; used to "focus" on a proof object. Rebuild: a radial-gradient overlay `radial-gradient(circle at 50% 50%, transparent 35%, rgba(0,0,0,.85) 55%)` animating the stop from 80% → 35%.
4. **Momentum + defocus cut** (8.43–9.10): the outgoing subject accelerates out of frame (ease-in, 15 f), then a hard cut to 3 frames of the next shot at ~20 px blur, snapping sharp. Rebuild: `filter: blur(20px)` for frames 0–2 of the incoming shot, then 0.
5. **Flash-frame background swap** (15.67): the same object stays locked in position, 2 washed pink-white frames, then a new surface and grade (orange → navy, colour → sepia). Use it for "same fact, new chapter".
6. **Scan flash zoom-through** (24.70–24.83): the scanned object's bracket area blooms to white over 4 frames (luma +120); the next scene emerges from the white, already rotating and scaling down into place. Rebuild: an additive white radial glow scaling up 1 → 6× with opacity 0 → 1 over 4 f; the next scene starts at `scale(1.6) rotate(-25deg)` and springs to `scale(1) rotate(-3deg)`.
7. **Circle wipe from type** (26.63–26.80): a white disc expands from the centre of the keyword in 5 f, revealing a white-floored scene. Rebuild: `clipPath: circle()` 0 → 150% with `Easing.in(Easing.cubic)`.
8. **White panel wipe with hard mask edge** (28.57–28.80): the frame whitens over 7 f while the hand and scanner are cut off by a horizontal mask line, and the barcode label unfolds diagonally on the white. Rebuild: a white solid with `clipPath: inset()` growing upward, plus a diagonal mask on the label.
9. **Slide-push** (31.3–32.0): the outgoing and incoming layouts on the same paper translate left together by one frame width over ~20 f, with an ease-in-out. Rebuild: both in one flex row, `translateX(0 → -100%)`, `Easing.bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1)`.
10. **Hard cut on beat into the CTA** (33.00), followed by a long settle-zoom.

## 7. What makes it high-end (the transferable lessons)
1. **One art-directed tabletop per fact.** Photoreal objects shot top-down on a distinct textured surface (grid mat, denim, felt, velvet, blueprint) make AI imagery feel like a product shoot, not a slideshow.
2. **A single finishing pass unifies mixed sources:** halftone dots + grain + vignette over AI clips, cut-outs and UI alike.
3. **Motivate transitions with the subject's own mechanics:** the scanner's laser flash, scan bracket and spotlight drive the cuts in a barcode video. Pick the "verb" of your topic and make it the transition.
4. **Settle-zoom on every scene start** (start at ~103–110% and decay exponentially over 15–25 frames) keeps static images alive without a visible camera move.
5. **Reserve type for abstraction.** Literal nouns get pictures. Only summary or emotional words become type, and when they do they are huge, word-synced and few (≤ 3 words per stack).
6. **Two type voices:** a whisper-weight serif italic, one word at a time, for reflective lines, and a heavy wide display for facts and thesis.
7. **Proof objects:** a dated newspaper headline with a highlighter sweep timed to the spoken words gives documentary weight.
8. **Callback structure:** the hook phrase ("those little black lines") returns as the thesis title, typed word-for-word in sync.
9. **Stepped 15 fps b-roll vs 30 fps graphics** gives a filmic/digital contrast for free.

## 8. Remotion rebuild recipe
- **Composition:** `1080×1920`, `30 fps`, duration = VO + 0.4 s (≈ 1170 frames). Render AI clips through a `<StepFrames every={2}>` wrapper (use `Math.floor(frame/2)*2` for clip time).
- **Typical scene stack (bottom → top):** `<SurfaceTexture kind="gridMat|denim|felt|velvet|blueprint" tint>` → `<AIClip src stepped>` or `<Cutout src outline="white 6px" shadow>` → `<ScanHUD brackets laser>` → `<KeywordStack>` / `<SerifWord>` → `<SpotlightIris>` → `<HalftoneOverlay dot={3} opacity={0.18}>` → `<GrainOverlay opacity={0.08}>` → `<Vignette strength={0.5}>`.
- **Components:**
  - `SettleZoom`: `scale = 1 + 0.08 * Math.exp(-frame/6)`. Wrap every scene in it.
  - `DigitBuild`: reveal characters at `startFrame + i*2` in random order, white condensed italic, `rotate(-8deg)`.
  - `PaperSlideIn`: from `translateX(110%) rotate(12deg)` to `translateX(0) rotate(-5deg)`, `spring({damping: 16, stiffness: 140, mass: 0.8})` ≈ 10 f, 12 px horizontal blur during the move.
  - `HighlighterSweep`: a yellow rect `#e8e04a` at 70% opacity, `mix-blend-mode: multiply`, `scaleX 0 → 1` from the left over 16 f, triggered at the word timestamp.
  - `SpotlightIris`: radial-gradient stop animated 80% → 35% over 6 f, `Easing.out(Easing.quad)`.
  - `SerifWordSwap`: a list of `{word, start}` from the Whisper JSON; render only the current word, 2-frame opacity in, Playfair Display Italic 64 px @1080 w.
  - `ScanHUD`: four red L-brackets (`#e0313a`, 4 px, 40 px arms), a laser line (a 6 px red bar with a `0 0 16px #ff2a2a` glow) translating Y across the bracket over 10 f, looped once per product.
  - `ConveyorStep`: `x = -slot * stepIndex`, each step `interpolate` with `Easing.inOut(Easing.cubic)` over 22 f, 30 f period.
  - `ScanFlash`: a white radial glow scale 1 → 6, opacity 0 → 1 over 4 f, then cut to the next scene, which starts at `scale(1.6) rotate(-25deg)` and springs `{damping: 20, stiffness: 90}` to `scale(1) rotate(-3deg)` over ~30 f.
  - `StitchedPatch`: a rounded rect, `border: 2px dashed rgba(180,40,60,.5)` inset 14 px, a fabric-noise fill, an SVG `feDisplacementMap` barrel bulge (scale ~20).
  - `SlamWord`: `scale 1.3 → 1` over 3 f + `text-shadow: 0 6px 8px rgba(0,0,0,.6)`.
  - `CircleWipe`: `clipPath: circle(${r}% at 50% 52%)`, r 0 → 150 over 5 f, ease-in.
  - `GlassCard`: `backdrop-filter: blur(12px)`, background `rgba(90,130,170,.45)`, `border-radius: 16px`, 1 px white 30% border, pop `spring({damping: 12, stiffness: 200})` from scale 0.4, anchored at the jar.
  - `SlidePush`: two layouts side by side, `translateX` 0 → −100% over 20 f, `Easing.bezier(0.65,0,0.35,1)`.
  - `PhoneRise` + `SearchPillType`: a phone from `translateY(40%)` with settle-zoom; the pill grows `width 0 → 100%` over 8 f, then types at 2.5 f/char with the caret blinking every 15 f.
- **Tricky bits:**
  - Halftone: an SVG pattern of 3 px dots at 45°, `mix-blend-mode: overlay` at 15–20%.
  - Chromatic offset on thesis type: two extra copies tinted `#2b6cff` and `#ff6a2b` at ±1.5 px, opacity 0.5, `mix-blend-mode: screen`.
  - The inverted flash frame: render the same frame with `filter: invert(.6) saturate(.2) contrast(1.2)` for exactly 2 frames.

## 9. Variation ideas
1. **"History of an everyday object" series** (QR codes, the paperclip, the shipping container): keep the top-down tabletop + halftone finish. Swap the motivated transition for the object's own action (QR → camera-focus snap, container → crane drop).
2. **Fintech / ops SaaS promo** ("how inventory sync works"): the conveyor-scan scene becomes the product demo. Glass inventory cards pop out of real products, and the thesis poster carries the tagline.
3. **Food/health explainer** ("what the numbers on your nutrition label mean"): spotlight iris + highlighter sweeps on label close-ups, serif single words for the reflective beat, a stitched-patch card for each takeaway.
