## 1. Concept & script structure
- **Genre:** brand-strategy micro-lesson ("thesis → case study (Nike) → reveal of the mechanism → moral → recap → CTA"). Creator promo for editing templates (earnedits.com / earnera.co).
- **Beats:**
  - 0.0–2.5 **Hook / thesis:** "Marketing isn't about products. It's about values." (A product, an iPhone, sinks underwater and the word VALUES surfaces with diamonds.)
  - 3.2–9.2 **Setup:** the world is loud and people remember little, so you must stand for one clear thing (globe with ring text → astronauts on the moon → floating iMessage bubbles).
  - 9.4–17.3 **Case study:** Nike just sells shoes, "just a basic product"; but nobody thinks about soles, materials or features (Nike magazine in macOS Photos → "Nike: we sell shoes" notification → browser typewriter → shoe exploded-view diagram).
  - 17.8–19.6 **Turn:** you feel drive, discipline, winning (UI buttons clicked by a cursor, 3D trophy).
  - 19.98–26.7 **Mechanism:** "Why?" Nike never talks about the product; they honour athletes, effort, struggle and greatness (WhatsApp chat, then a 3 s hyper-cut football footage montage).
  - 27.1–34.4 **Moral:** that's who they are / what they stand for / the lesson: people don't remember what you sell, they remember what you believe in (green poster type → white letterbox type → Jordan cut-out → recap panel).
  - 35.1–40.8 **CTA:** "Just for editors", full open AEP file, comment "Link".
- **Hook mechanics (first 1.5 s):** frame 0 is already a rich, moving underwater god-ray background with a continuous push-in (zoom rate rising 0.8 → 1.3/frame). The word "Marketing" sits small and dark (≈30% frame width) as if submerged. At 0.8 s "isn't about" flips in white over it in a mixed-weight lockup, and at 1.33 s the giant "Products" (≈75% width, dark navy, refracted) appears while an iPhone sinks through the type. The hook combines a premium 3D environment, a contradiction in the first sentence, and an object you can track.
- **Script → visual rule:** **one sentence = one world**. The noun of the sentence picks the world (world → globe, remember → astronaut, "you stand for" → phone keypad/messages, "sells shoes" → message notification, "basic product" → a blank browser, features → technical exploded diagram, athletes → real footage). Inside the world, the sentence's words appear as type **when spoken**, usually one word or chunk at a time.

## 4. Motion & timing analysis
- **Pacing:** 0.78 detected cuts/s. Narrative scenes are 1.0–2.9 s (median ≈1.9 s), and every scene carries one full sentence. The footage montage (22.80–27.07) is the deliberate speed burst: **12 cuts in 4.3 s, 3–14 frames each** (0.10, 0.33, 0.43, 0.20, 0.20, 0.27, 0.47, 0.13, 0.10, 0.23, 0.10, 0.43 s), then the edit snaps back to 2–3 s poster holds for the moral. Pace curve: medium → medium → **very fast (emotion)** → slow (moral) → medium (CTA).
- **Cut ↔ VO sync (the core rule):** every narrative cut lands **on the first word of a new sentence**. Measured offsets (cut − word start): +0.07 (People), +0.09 (so), +0.03 (Look), +0.05 (That's), −0.01 (But), −0.04 (you don't), +0.02 (Why), −0.03 (That's), −0.01 (People), +0.01 (They), +0.05 (Just), +0.01 (earnedits). **Median +0.02 s, i.e. the cut is frame-locked to the word**, not leading it. Exception: the hook's VALUES cut lands 0.18 s after the word "values" because the phone-into-lens move needs to finish first.
- **Word-level reveals:** inside scenes, words appear within 0–4 frames of being spoken: DISCIPLINE button 18.93 (VO 18.94), WINNING click 19.47 (19.44), FOR flash 29.33 (29.34), panel 33.13 (They 33.12), NIKE 14.93 (14.84). Typewriter lines run at **1 char/frame (30 cps)**, faster than speech, so a line is fully typed before its sentence ends.
- **Beat relation:** cuts mostly land **0.02–0.19 s before a detected beat** (median ≈ −0.10 s), so the VO is edited to the grid. VO-word priority wins and the beat is a secondary alignment.
- **Easing signatures (from per_frame.csv):**

## 5. Transitions catalogue
1. **Hard cut on the sentence's first word**: the default (≈20 of 28). Combined with a complete change of world, palette and type style, so each cut reads as a "chapter turn".
2. **Letterbox close-in** at 6.67, 17.97 and 29.93: on the cut frame the new scene is full-frame (luma 251). Black bars then slide in from the top and bottom to 255 px each over ~7 frames with an exponential ease-out, and the content scales slightly to fit the 720×770 band. The reverse (letterbox → full-bleed) is always an **instant** hard cut (14.17, 22.80, 35.13). Rebuild: two black `<div>`s, height `interpolate(f,[0,7],[0,255],{easing:Easing.out(Easing.exp)})`; the scene underneath is scaled `1 → 0.98`.

## 7. What makes it high-end (the transferable lessons)
1. **One sentence = one world.** Change the whole environment on every sentence, picking each world by literal association with the sentence's key noun. Novelty every 1–3 s, without random B-roll.
2. **Lock cuts to the first word of the sentence (±1 frame)**, and lock keyword reveals to the spoken word (±4 frames). Visual changes then feel caused by the voice.
3. **A recurring frame system holds chaos together:** an animated letterbox (720×770 window) for UI/talk scenes vs full-bleed for posters/worlds, plus one recurring film-edge border and one brand accent colour (`#8ad260` family). The worlds can be wildly different and still feel like one designer made them.
4. **Real UI recreations as storytelling props:** chat threads, notifications, browsers and buttons. Familiar interfaces deliver a sentence faster than abstract graphics.
5. **Typewriter at 1 char/frame** for "plain truth" statements, and a heavy condensed poster face for "manifesto" statements. Typography changes with the tone of the line.
6. **Use a burst montage for the emotional peak:** 3–14 frame clips, defocused breathers, white and red flash frames, and a single quiet caption line that builds word by word. Then return to slow posters for the moral.
7. **Recap panel before the CTA:** the last scene lists the whole moral as a stack of highlighted lines, so the takeaway is screenshot-able.
8. **Every move eases:** exponential ease-out on bars and panels (≈70% of the travel in 2 frames), ease-in pushes into cuts and ease-out after them, and damped-spring camera orbits. Nothing moves linearly except marquees.
9. **Brand furniture** (site URL at top, barcode, EKG line, film-edge numbers) adds "designed object" detail at almost zero cost.

## 8. Remotion rebuild recipe
- **Composition:** 1080×1920, 30 fps, duration = VO + 0.1 s (≈1230 frames). Scale every px value in this file by 1.5 (the reference is 720 wide). Letterbox bars become 383 px each and the content band becomes 1080×1155.
- **Data:** VO word timestamps JSON → `sentences[]` (start of first word = scene start frame) and `words[]` for in-scene reveals.
- **Typical scene stack (bottom → top):** `<WorldBackground>` (video plate / gradient / paper / UI screenshot) → `<FilmEdgeBorder variant="white|green">` (optional) → props (`<Cutout halftone shadow>`, `<UIBubble>`, `<Device>`) → `<KeywordType>` → `<HighlighterBox>` → `<Cursor>` → `<GrainOverlay opacity={0.06}>` → `<Letterbox open|closed>` → `<FlashOverlay>`.
- **Components and timings:**
  - `<Letterbox mode="close" barPx={383} frames={7} easing={Easing.bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1)} />`. Opening (back to full-bleed) is instant.
  - `<SentenceCut>`: a `<Series>` whose sequence `from` = round(firstWordStart × fps). Do not pre-roll.
  - `<TypewriterLine text cps={30} caret blinkFrames={16} font="Courier Prime Bold">`.
  - `<WordReveal mode="fadeGreyToWhite" perWordFrames={5}>` for footage captions (SF Pro Regular, 3.4% of frame height, y=52%).
  - `<ChunkReplace chunks={["THATS","THATS WHO","THEY ARE"]} at={wordFrames} fadeFrames={2} font="Anton" color="#142803" bg="#8ad260" widthPct={71} />`.
  - `<MarqueeType text repeat={3} speedPxPerFrame={40} blur={8} />` for "PEOPLE DON'T REMEMBER" and the tiled "THAT'S WHAT THEY STAND".
  - `<RingText text="WORLD" radius={0.28*W} rotateDegPerFrame={2} />` (SVG `textPath` on a circle around the globe).
  - `<ChatThread platform="whatsapp|imessage" messages={[{side, words:[{w,t}]}]} wordFadeFrames={4} autoScroll spring={{damping:18, stiffness:160}} />`.
  - `<UIButton label="DISCIPLINE" popSpring={{damping:10, stiffness:220, mass:0.6}} />` + `<Cursor path clickAt>` (scale 1 → 0.85 → 1 over 4 frames on click).
  - `<CalloutPanel side="right" widthPct={70} enterFrames={6} easing={Easing.out(Easing.exp)} lines={recap} lineHighlight="#1d5a14" rotateDeg={-5} />`.
  - `<ZoomThrough outFrames={10} inFrames={6} peakScale={1.35} />` with `Easing.in(Easing.cubic)` and then `Easing.out(Easing.cubic)`.
  - `<LightLeakFlash frames={10} warm="#ffb347" />`, `<InvertFlash frames={2} blur={6} />`, `<ColorFlash color="#c00000" frames={1} />`, `<WhiteFlash frames={3} />` + `<ScalePunch from={1.2} frames={1} />`.
  - `<BurstMontage clips={[...]} durationsFrames={[3,10,13,6,6,8,14,4,3,7,3,13]} />` with a 1-frame white or red flash between selected clips.
  - `<Orbit3D>`: a CSS 3D container (`perspective: 1200px`) rotating Y/Z with `spring({damping: 12, stiffness: 60})` for the iMessage keypad and the orbiting shoes.
  - `<CardCarousel3D cards={siteShots} rotateY={-35} spacing={0.6*W} slideFrames={20} />` + `<ScriptWordmark text="earnedits.com" gradient={["#3ddc97","#1a8f6a"]} widthPct={130} chromaticAberration={2} />`.
- **Tricky effects:**
  - **Halftone on cut-outs:** an SVG pattern of 3 px dots, multiplied over the photo with `mix-blend-mode: multiply` at 40–60%, or a shader in `@remotion/three`.
  - **Displaced "VALUES" water type:** an SVG `feTurbulence` (baseFrequency 0.02, animated seed) + `feDisplacementMap` scale 12 on the text layer.
  - **Stretched "driiivve":** generate the duplicated letters procedurally from the word and scroll them in with a 6 px horizontal motion blur (`feGaussianBlur stdDeviation="6 0"`).
  - **Exploded shoe:** two cut-out layers (upper, sole); the sole translates +60 px y with `spring({damping:14, stiffness:120})`, and the arrows are SVG paths drawn with `strokeDashoffset` over 6 frames.
