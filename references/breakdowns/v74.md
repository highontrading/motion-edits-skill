## 1. Concept & script structure
- **Genre:** branding-principle micro-explainer ("named rule → definition → case study → payoff number → mechanism → call to action to the viewer").
- **Beats:**
  - 0.0–3.37 **Hook:** "This is a branding rule every entrepreneur needs to know." Tiny white caption on black, then a collage of Jobs/Gates/Musk/Zuckerberg polaroids on grid paper.
  - 3.37–6.43 **Name + definition:** "The Purple Cow" (photo of a purple cow among white sheep) → "the Purple Cow rule is simple" (white typographic card).
  - 6.43–12.53 **Rule:** "when every brand goes one way, you go the other way… that's how you stand out / win."
  - 12.53–18.57 **Case study:** "Look at Crocs" → the market chased beauty and perfection (sneaker footage) → Crocs went all in on ugly.
  - 18.57–22.97 **Payoff number:** ugly made them over **$4.5 billion**.
  - 22.97–31.17 **Mechanism:** break the pattern → people notice → they talk → the media talks → you're the only thing people remember.
  - 31.17–43.1 **Viewer turn + close:** "So ask yourself what your brand can do that's truly different… do that, even if it sounds crazy… you don't just get attention, you own the market."
- **Hook mechanics:** for the first 1.37 s the frame is pure black with a single *small* (≈36% frame width) white caption that writes on word by word, and "Rule" gets a green highlighter box. The near-empty frame forces the eye onto the text. At 1.37 an orange-to-white light-leak burn (4 frames) explodes into a busy, colourful collage, so the scroll-stopper is the contrast between minimal and maximal.
- **Script → visual rule:** one clause = one new world. Only the words being spoken are on screen, each word appearing on its own syllable. The emphasis word of each clause is upsized 2–3× and/or restyled (italic heavy, serif, colour, badge). Nouns get literal imagery (cow, Crocs, TVs = media, eye = notice, superhero = remembered).

## 4. Motion & timing analysis
- **Pacing:** ~0.65 real cuts/s; shots 0.33–3.6 s, median ~1.25 s. Fastest stretch: 13.5–16.3 s (shoe swaps every 2–4 frames, plus 3 cuts in 2.8 s). Longest holds are the two "thesis" moments (Superman/PEOPLE 2.4 s, "even if it sounds crazy" 3.6 s).
- **Easing signature, eased through the cut:** the most reusable pattern. Movement **accelerates into the cut and decays after it**, so the cut sits on the velocity peak:
  - Shot 5 exit: flow rises 0.39 → 14.4 px/frame over 20 frames (8.13 → 8.77), roughly exponential (×1.2 per frame), a cubic/expo ease-in.
  - Shot 24: 7-frame ease-in (36.07 → 36.30, flow 0.8 → 15.4), then cut.
  - Shot 5 entrance: peak 5.1 at frame +1, half-life ≈ 6 frames, ~95% settled by frame +40, an expo ease-out (`1 − 2^(−10t)`-like).
  - Shot 23/24 entrances: peak at frame +2, decays to <25% by frame +10–12.
  - Crane in shot 25: symmetric ease-in-out, 8 frames up + 8 frames down (37.67 → 38.03), peak 8 px/frame at 180 px ⇒ ≈32 px/frame at 720 px.
  - No overshoot in camera moves (no sign flips in pan/zoom). Overshoot appears only on small pops ("The Purple cow" card: zoom +2.1 then −0.8 the next frame).
- **Entrance vocabulary (frames at 30 fps):**
  - Word blur-in, the default: opacity 0→1 and blur ~8→0 px over 4–6 frames, starting on the word's VO onset (±2 frames).
  - Heavy payoff word: scale 1.3→1 + blur, 5 frames, with no overshoot.
  - Foreground ghost fly-in: a 4–6× copy of the word, heavily blurred (≈20 px), crossing the frame edge in 8–12 frames while the sharp small copy sits centre.
  - Highlighter box wipe: L→R, 4 frames, ease-out.
  - Underline draw-on: 8 frames.
  - Serif letters popping in random order: ~10 frames for a 12-letter word.
  - Tracking collapse ("other   way"): ~25 frames, ease-out.
  - Defocus→focus card: 8 frames.
  - Torn-edge card reveal: 2 frames.
  - Letter-by-letter giant word behind a subject: ~15 frames for 6 letters.
- **Exits:** mostly none (cut). Otherwise blur-out of 5 frames (4.67 card, WIN card), slide-left with blur of 4 frames, or a glitch collapse of 2–3 frames.
- **Camera:** almost every graphic shot has a slow move: push (+3…+25), pull (−8…−26) or a crane. Dramatic exceptions: the 92° rotate-zoom (shot 5) and the 90° word swing (shot 16). Footage shots are static or handheld.
- **Secondary motion:** HUD counters scramble, dust particles float, doodles boil on 2s/3s, lightning flicker, phone glare, TV static loops, a green bloom pulse on WIN, polaroid page curls.
- **Sync:** cuts are **VO-locked, not beat-locked**. Across 28 cuts the median offset to the nearest word onset is **≈ −0.08 s** (cut slightly before the word): "billion" cut at 21.23 vs word 21.24; "They talk" cut 0.33 s early; "Do that" −0.09 s; "you own" +0.08 s. Offsets against the 112 BPM beat grid are spread ±0.25 s (random within the 0.53 s period), so there is no beat sync. Word reveals land on syllable onsets within ±2 frames. The typos ("your the only", "truely", "becuase") show the captions were typed manually per word.

## 5. Transitions catalogue
1. **Light-leak burn** (1.37–1.50, 28.60–28.77): an orange `#f16c12` glow rises from the bottom edge over 3–4 frames, peaks to near-white (luma 250) for 1–2 frames, and the next shot is revealed as the burn fades. Rebuild: radial gradient orange→yellow→white with `mix-blend-mode: screen`, opacity 0→1 over 4 frames, cut underneath at the peak, fade 1→0 over 3 frames.
2. **Spin-zoom whip** (8.80): the whole layer rotates (−7°/frame) and scales down while speeding up over 20 frames, then hard-cuts to black. Rebuild: `rotate` and `scale` driven by `interpolate(frame, [start, cut], [0,1], {easing: Easing.in(Easing.exp)})`, plus a motion-blur pass.
3. **Sticky-note flip** (3.20–3.37): a green paper square peels from a polaroid and flips toward the lens until it fills ~40% of the frame (5 frames), masking the cut.
4. **Blur-out** (6.27–6.43, 12.37–12.53): the whole card goes 0 → ~12 px Gaussian blur and drops slightly in opacity over 5 frames, then a cut. A soft scene end before a high-contrast new scene.
5. **Card snap** (11.30): a full-bleed page scales to an ~85% inset card on black in 1 frame, settling in 6. Good for "zoom out to see the whole thing" beats.
6. **Teal colour wipe + flash card** (16.17–16.33): a diagonal teal fill sweeps from the bottom-left in 2 frames, a white square card with the last word flashes for 1 frame, then the teal frame becomes the next scene's lighting before it cuts to black.
7. **Glitch collapse / slice displacement** (32.03–32.13, 34.20, 35.55–35.63, 41.83–41.90): type and UI tear into horizontal bars that shift ±30–80 px for 2–3 frames, with grey vertical bands at the frame edges, then a cut.
8. **Zoom-through** (36.07–36.30): a 7-frame accelerating push into a blurred glyph, then a cut to a white page whose content starts already moving (velocity continuity).
9. **Slide-off with blur** (18.40–18.57): the text block translates left ~30% with horizontal blur over 4 frames.
10. **Defocus-in** (12.53): the new card starts at ~15 px blur and focuses over 8 frames, a soft entrance after a hard cut.

## 7. What makes it high-end (the transferable lessons)
1. **Shared chrome, changing worlds.** A thin film-frame/viewfinder overlay (edge print, rules, frame numbers, barcode) on most shots lets every scene use a totally different look and still feel like one piece.
2. **Ease through the cut.** Accelerate into the cut (8–20 frames, expo-in) and start the next shot already moving, decaying over 10–40 frames (expo-out). The cut hides inside the motion peak.
3. **One accent colour per scene** on a black/white base (green, magenta, orange, teal), and **alternate black and white scenes** so every cut is also a luminance hit.
4. **Word-level blur-in reveals** synced to syllables (4–6 frames). This is calmer and more premium than pop-scale, and it reads as "focusing" on each word.
5. **Typographic contrast inside one phrase:** light lead-in plus heavy italic payoff, or grotesk plus Didone serif plus handwriting, with the payoff at ~1.8× size.
6. **Depth with type:** giant blurred foreground copies of the words and text placed behind a rotoscoped subject (PEOPLE behind Superman) make flat type feel 3D.
7. **Literal imagery per noun** (cow, Crocs, TV wall for "media", eye for "notice", superhero for "remember"), drawn from wildly mixed sources (stock, film stills, product shots, UI).
8. **Footage as cards:** inset rounded cards on black let low-quality or off-ratio footage sit in a 9:16 frame and look deliberate.
9. **Cuts land ~0.05–0.1 s before the word**, and the VO gaps carry the transitions.

## 8. Remotion rebuild recipe
- **Composition:** `1080×1920`, `30 fps`, duration = VO + 10 frames black tail.
- **Data:** VO word timestamps JSON → `words[] {text, start, end, emphasis?: 'italic'|'serif'|'condensed'|'badge'|'highlight', accent?: hex}`. Group words into clauses; each clause = one `<Sequence>` starting at `firstWord.start*fps − 3`.
- **Layer stack per scene:** `<SceneBackground variant="black|paper|grid|footage|card-on-black">` → assets (`<Cutout>`, `<FootageCard radius={32} width="92%">`) → `<KineticLine>` words → accent devices (`<HighlightBox>`, `<UnderlineDraw>`, `<LabelBadge>`) → `<FilmFrameOverlay variant="light|dark" edgeText="KODAK PORTRA 400">` → `<Grain opacity={0.06}>` + `<DustParticles>` (dark scenes) → `<CameraRig>` wrapping everything (scale/rotate/translate).
- **Key components:**
  - `BlurInWord`: `opacity: interpolate(f, [0,5], [0,1])`, `filter: blur(${interpolate(f,[0,5],[8,0])}px)`, `translateY: interpolate(f,[0,5],[6,0])`, easing `Easing.out(Easing.cubic)`. Font: Inter Tight / Helvetica Neue 700, `letterSpacing: '-0.04em'`, `lineHeight: 0.88`.
  - `PayoffWord`: 900 italic, scale `spring({damping: 200, stiffness: 300, mass: 0.6})` from 1.3 → 1 + blur 10 → 0 over 5 frames. Optional `glowPulse` (text-shadow 0 0 24px accent, opacity 0→1→0.4 over 10 frames).
  - `ForegroundGhostWord`: the same text at `scale(5)`, `filter: blur(18px)`, `opacity: 0.85`, translating from off-frame (x ±60%) across the edge over 10–12 frames with `Easing.out(Easing.quad)`; z-ordered above the sharp line.
  - `TrackingCollapse`: `letterSpacing` / word gap from 3 em → 0.25 em over 25 frames, `Easing.out(Easing.exp)`.
  - `HighlightBox`: `scaleX` 0→1, `transformOrigin: left`, 4 frames, `#3ddc5a`.
  - `UnderlineDraw`: SVG path `strokeDashoffset` over 8 frames.
  - `CameraRig` with an `easeThroughCut` helper: for the last N frames of a scene apply `progress = Easing.in(Easing.exp)`; for the first M frames of the next scene apply `1 − Easing.out(Easing.exp)` to the same property (rotation, scale or translateY), so the velocities match. Typical N = 8–20, M = 10–40.
  - `LightLeakBurn`: absolutely-positioned radial gradients (`#f16c12` → `#ffd27a` → `#fff`), `mix-blend-mode: screen`, opacity curve [0, 1, 1, 0] over [0, 4, 6, 10] frames; switch the scene at frame 5.
  - `GlitchSlice`: render the outgoing frame into 8–12 horizontal strips with random `translateX(±40–80px)` that changes every frame for 2–3 frames, plus 2 grey vertical bars.
  - `TextBehindSubject`: layer order of background plate → giant word → subject cut-out PNG (pre-rotoscoped matte or a second masked copy of the footage). Reveal letters one by one every 2–3 frames.
  - `HudFrame`: SVG octagon in `#36a60e` with `filter: drop-shadow(0 0 12px #36a60e)`, scale 0.85→1.0 over the shot, plus REC dot blink (15 frames on/off) and a timecode counter.
  - `DigitScramble`: for 4 frames show random digits, then the final value.
- **Footage handling:** inset cards with `borderRadius` and a black surround; the stop-motion shoe swap = a sequence of 2–4-frame clips; hand-drawn doodle overlays as PNG sequences on 2s (`Math.floor(frame/2)` to choose the drawing) with `mix-blend-mode: screen`.
