## 1. Concept & script structure
- **Genre:** object-history micro-documentary ("the surprising origin of an everyday thing"), built on the "ignored for decades, then one moment changed everything" arc.
- **Beats:**
  - 0.0–4.8 **Hook:** "The zipper was invented in 1851. Nobody cared for 70 years." A boot stomps into frame, hands zip it, a hand writes 1851, then a flip-book of years ends on a book cover embossed "70".
  - 4.8–8.2 **Inventor 1:** Elias Howe patented it, then ignored it.
  - 8.2–15.7 **Inventor 2:** another inventor improved it in 1893 at the Chicago World's Fair. "Still nobody cared."
  - 15.7–21.5 **Catalyst:** WWI; the military needed faster gear, so zippers went on uniforms.
  - 21.5–25.9 **Turn:** "Suddenly people noticed. But the moment that changed everything…"
  - 25.9–34.9 **Payoff:** B.F. Goodrich put them on boots in 1923 and named it after its sound: "ZIP".
  - 34.9–40.2 **CTA:** "Editor. Get the After Effects project file on earnedits.com. Download now." (a book opens to a phone mockup and typed pages).
- **Hook mechanics (first 1.5 s):** at frame 0 a leather boot is already stomping down into a top-down shot of a busy workbench (clock, spools, buttons, sewing pattern, a red-zipper boot), with heavy motion blur and three audio onsets at 0.12/0.30/0.58 s. By 0.8 s hands grab the zipper pull. By 1.07 s the camera jumps diagonally toward a ticket where a hand starts writing "1851". So within 1.5 s there are an impact, a tactile action, and the date being written.
- **Script → visual rule:** every noun is a **physical prop on a table** (boot, ticket, flip-book, sewing machine, dossier, rifle, jacket, newspaper, boots, pinned ads, book), and every clause is **typed onto a paper object** in that set. People appear as AI-generated colourised period portraits standing behind desks, never as real footage.

## 4. Motion & timing analysis
- **Pacing:** 14 shots in 40 s (~0.35 cuts/s), median ~3 s. It feels faster than that because something moves or types inside every shot every ~0.3 s: tags, ribbons, particles, page flips, hands. The longest shots (dossier → fair 7.6 s across the dissolve, the newspaper 4.3 s, the CTA 5.3 s) are those where the camera keeps travelling.
- **Frame-rate signature:** animated at ~12 fps (update gaps of 2–3 frames). The **opening hand/boot stop-motion runs at ~3 fps**: holds of 8–10 frames, then 2-frame bursts. This handmade "on 10s" stutter sells the hook as physical. The **AI scenes and camera moves are smoother**: the fair zoom and the whip use 2–3-frame steps throughout.
- **Easing signatures (per update):**
  - Camera push-ins are long, linear-to-ease-out drifts (+28 to +38 net zoom over 2.5–3.5 s) with a small roll (shot 10: −24° over 4.3 s).
  - The **whip-pan** is **ease-in to a peak, then ease-out** (accelerating ×1.3–1.5 per update for 8 updates, peaking at ~60 px/update real, then decaying ×0.75), so it is a symmetric `easeInOutCubic` across ~0.9 s that straddles two compositions.
  - The **dissolve-zoom** at 11.6–12.4 s has the same in-out profile (peak −10.4 at the midpoint).
  - Prop entrances (boots, soldier) are ease-out with a slight wobble overshoot in rotation (≈ ±1.5°, settling in ~0.5 s). Paper tags fade+unblur in ~6 frames with no overshoot.
- **Entrance / exit vocabulary:**
  - Stop-motion action: hands zipping, a boot stomping, a rifle slammed down, a pen writing; 3–6 discrete poses.
  - Page-flip counter: typed years on pages flipping every 4–8 frames with motion smear, closing on a cover number.
  - Rack-focus in (blur 10 px → 0 over ~5 frames) to open shots 3 and 13; defocus out to close shots 2 and 5.
  - Name-tag pop (blur-fade, ~6 frames), then re-position.
  - 3D curling ribbon banner wrapping around an object (~15 frames).
  - Live typewriter (~10 chars/s, matched to VO speed).
  - Particle assemble (scattered specks → letters, ~24 frames).
  - Ember/burn-in paper strip (~12 frames).

## 5. Transitions catalogue
1. **Zoom-punch cut** (2.47–2.53 s): a single-update zoom of +8.3 (≈ 4–5% scale jump) on the last frame before the cut, so the shot "lunges" into the next one. Rebuild: on the last 2 frames, `scale 1 → 1.06` with a 6 px radial/zoom blur, then hard cut.
2. **Defocus → rack-focus in** (4.6–4.9 s, 31.33 s): the outgoing shot blurs over ~5 frames; the incoming shot starts at ~10 px blur and sharpens in ~5 frames, with a slight zoom settle.
3. **Dissolve + ease-in-out zoom** (11.2–12.4 s): a ~0.5 s crossfade from the flat-lay dossier into the AI fair scene while the zoom accelerates into the midpoint and decelerates after (peak ~40 px/update real). The dossier's "1893" reads through the fair gate's "1893" plaques, a loose **match dissolve on the date**.
4. **Smoke / burn wipe** (15.6–16.1 s): the fair scene blurs, then the next scene is revealed through a drifting smoke/char matte that eats from the centre outward, leaving scraps of the old image at the top and bottom edges for 2–3 updates.
5. **Whip-pan between compositions** (18.07–19.1 s): the camera accelerates left across the catalogue backdrop with motion blur and lands on a second cut-out, so two shots read as one move. Rebuild: a long horizontal canvas with both soldiers 1.2 frame-widths apart, `translateX` driven by `Easing.inOut(Easing.cubic)` over 27 frames with a horizontal blur proportional to velocity (max ~24 px).
6. **Beat-locked hard cuts** (8.17, 17.50, 19.73, 21.53, 34.87 s): plain cuts on the beat between strongly different surfaces (portrait → wood, rifle → catalogue, jacket → newspaper).
7. **Prop-led cut** (28.87–28.97 s): a boot slides up into frame one update before the cut, and the next shot continues the boot's entrance with a tilt-up, a motion bridge across the cut.
8. **3D book open as CTA opener** (34.97 s): the cover hinges open like a door, turning the "archive" world into the website reveal.

## 7. What makes it high-end (the transferable lessons)
1. **Build a physical world for the story:** one cohesive tabletop universe (wood, felt, parchment, brass, red string) where every caption is printed on an object in that world. Avoid floating type.
2. **Type in real time at the VO's pace** (~10 chars/s typewriter), so the viewer reads and hears the same word at the same moment.
3. **Numbers become props:** a date written by a hand, a flip-book of years, a "70" embossed on a cover, a red-circled 1923. Each number gets the biggest, most tactile treatment.
4. **Stop-motion hands** (3–12 fps) make collage feel handmade and premium, and they draw the eye to the action.
5. **One accent colour (oxblood red)** for the key word of every line, against an all-sepia world, and one spotlight vignette per scene to direct focus.
6. **Camera always drifting:** slow push-ins with a little roll, plus one big in-out whip or dissolve-zoom per act to reset energy.
7. **Cut on the beat and on the word at the same time.** Pick cut points where a VO clause starts within ±0.1 s of a music beat.
8. **Label things in-scene:** name tags on a chest, a banner around a machine, a ticket stamped "ZIP". Captions double as set dressing.
9. **End the world with a CTA that belongs to it:** the book opens to the website, so the ad never breaks the aesthetic.

## 8. Remotion rebuild recipe
- **Composition:** `1080×1920`, `30 fps`, ~1,210 frames. Posterise animated layers to 12 fps (`f = Math.floor(frame / 2.5) * 2.5`, rounded), and give the stop-motion hand sequences their own 3-fps step (`Math.floor(frame / 10)`).
- **Scene stack (bottom → top):** `<SurfaceTexture type="wood|felt|parchment|stone">` → props as `<Cutout shadow={{x:10,y:14,blur:18,opacity:.45}}>` → `<HandSequence frames={[...]} fps={3}>` → caption carriers (`<PaperTag>`, `<BurntStrip>`, `<MetalPlate>`, `<TicketStamp>`) holding `<TypewriterText>` → `<SpotlightVignette rx="55%" ry="42%" falloff={0.75}>` → `<Grain opacity={0.06}>`.
- **Components & timing:**
  - `<TypewriterText text cps={10} font="Special Elite" color="#151210" accentColor="#a3352b">`: characters appear on VO word timestamps (char index interpolated across each word's start→end), each char with a 2-frame opacity flicker and ±0.5 px baseline jitter for an inked look.
  - `<PageFlipCounter values={[1851,1856,1861,1871,1886,1891,1901,1911]} framesPer={6} endCover="70">`: each flip is a `rotateY(0 → -180deg)` on the right page with `perspective: 900px` over 4 frames plus a 6 px horizontal blur; at the end the cover rotates closed (8 frames).
  - `<NameTag text>`: opacity 0 → 1 and blur 8 → 0 over 6 frames, then `translateY` to its final spot via `spring({damping: 14, stiffness: 160, mass: 0.7})`.

## 9. Variation ideas
1. **Brand-origin stories** ("Why the Post-it almost never existed"): swap the tailor's bench for a 1970s office desk (laminate, carbon paper, typewriter), keep the typed tags and the page-flip year counter, and use brand yellow as the single accent instead of oxblood.
2. **True-crime / mystery explainers:** keep the cork board with red string and push-pins as the main set, burnt-edge evidence strips for captions, stop-motion hands placing photos, and a whip-pan between suspects' cut-outs.
3. **Recipe or food history** ("Ketchup was sold as medicine"): use a kitchen-table flat-lay with recipe cards, handwritten dates on labels, a flip-book of years on a recipe tin, and tomato red as the accent; end with a recipe book opening to the product page.
