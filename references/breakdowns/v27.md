## 1. Concept & script structure
- **Genre:** product/innovation micro-documentary ("what if…" question → product reveal → user proof → human stakes → CTA). 1-sentence-per-scene pacing.
- **Beats:**
  - 0.00–6.72 **Hook (rhetorical question):** next computer revolution won't fit in your hand but in your mouth. Four visuals for one sentence: typed serif question → wall of retro PCs ("Computer Revolution") → hand crossed out → explosion of mouth stickers.
  - 6.73–10.00 **Reveal:** "Meet the MouthPad" by Augmental (the transcript writes "bio-mental", but the on-screen tag reads "Augmental"). A floating product cut-out on a yellow stripe, with a name label and the company logo.
  - 10.00–14.90 **Proof:** real interview footage in an inset rounded card (a user puts the device in his mouth, the Bluetooth "Connected" screen, a laptop with on-screen keyboard). The source footage keeps its own burned-in subtitles.
  - 15.07–22.33 **Stakes / emotional turn:** for people with paralysis it's "not just another gadget" → "a new level of independence" (a marionette on a toy-theatre stage).
  - 22.33–27.87 **CTA:** "Editors, grab the After Effects project file on earnedits.com".
- **Hook mechanics (first 1.5 s):** 3 frames of blank warm-grey paper, then a dark-grey serif italic types "What if the next" word by word with a **yellow text caret**. The line drifts left as it grows so the caret stays near centre. At 1.47 it hard-cuts to black and dozens of beige retro PCs fly in from the camera. Calm editorial type followed by a busy collage gives a "quiet → loud" contrast inside 1.5 s.
- **Script → visual rule:** every clause gets a set. The **noun becomes a literal prop** (computer, hand, mouth, device, patients, puppet), and the **negating or key phrase becomes a handwritten label** that is circled, crossed out or underlined by a marker doodle ("Computer Revolution" X'd out, "didn't fit in your hand" + red X on the palm, "gadget" circled).

## 2. Visual language
- **Art direction:** tabletop collage / photo-set. Each scene looks like a **physical miniature set photographed from above or in front**: real wood desk with a cassette and highlighters, a photo studio with softboxes and vintage film cameras, an engraved paper toy theatre, a grid-paper desk with a ruler and pen. Cut-outs (hand, PCs, mouths, device, patients) have thin white or no edges and soft contact shadows. Live interview footage is only ever shown **inside a rounded "screen" card**, never full-frame.
- **Backgrounds & textures:** warm light-grey paper `#e0dde1`/`#cecbcf` with a strong radial vignette (opener, product, footage cards); black void `#1d1a1d` (PC wall); walnut wood `#684633`/`#473125` (hand); white cyc studio (patients); 19th-century engraved theatre in red/cream; grid paper with kraft sheets (CTA). A fine **halftone dot screen** sits over everything (visible on the hand, the faces and the theatre curtains).
- **Colour:** neutral warm greys and browns with **one accent family: mustard yellow** `#ccb024`/`#ccaf23` (caret, label plates, product stripe, CTA badge). The second accent is **marker red** (~`#d32a2e`: X on the palm, "paralysis", circle on "gadget", "EDITORS"). Blacks are lifted and warm (`#1d1a1d`, `#272238`). Grade: slightly faded, desaturated filmic look (mean luma 60–200 depending on set). Saturation stays low except for the yellow and red.
- **Typography:**
  - **Opening serif:** bookish transitional serif italic (Lora / Libre Baskerville Italic / Georgia Italic class), medium weight, sentence case, dark grey `#3a3638`. Cap height ≈ 18% of frame height, line ≈ 63% of frame width. A **yellow 3 px caret** `#d8bd2a` blinks after the last glyph.
  - **Handwritten marker labels** (main voice): casual print-script hand (Gochi Hand / Patrick Hand / Caveat Brush class), lowercase-led, uneven baseline. Three colour modes: **white on a mustard torn-edge plate** ("Computer Revolution", "didn't fit in your hand"; plate ≈ 37% frame width, 13% height), **black on white or yellow** ("Mouth pad" ≈ 16% W, "Augmental" on a paper tag), and **red on white** ("paralysis" ≈ 9% W with a hand-drawn pointer line). On the CTA it is white on black ("After effect project file").
  - **Chalkboard caption:** white hand-lettered text on a black board with a thin gold `#b59a3c` frame ("it isn't just / another gadget"), ≈ 45% W, typed per letter.
  - **CTA display:** red squarish techno display (Bungee / Audiowide class) with a dark outline, "EDITORS" ≈ 33% W, written on letter by letter. **EARNEDITS.COM** is set in the same family, white with a black outline, on a mustard plate with a dark border (≈ 45% W) across the lower third of the card.
  - **Emphasis rule:** the keyword is never bigger; instead it gets a **doodle** around or over it: white ellipse (Computer Revolution, EDITORS), red ellipse ("Mouth pad", "gadget"), red bracket box ("level"), and an X for negation.
- **Layout & composition:** strict centre-weighted symmetry. The hero object sits in the middle 40% (hand, device, card, puppet). Labels sit just below-left of the hero (hand scene) or above it (product). Footage cards are inset ≈ 12% from each edge with a ~30 px corner radius. Foreground blur props frame the edges: real hands left and right of the desk, film cameras and softboxes in the studio, pen and ruler on the CTA.
- **Assets:** cut-out beige 90s PCs (tiled ~25 copies at random rotations), a mannequin-like cut-out forearm/hand, a pink cassette, red/black highlighters, white-bordered open-mouth stickers (~30 copies), a transparent retainer-style device (rotating product clip), the Augmental round logo (black circle with a white "Λ"), interview footage, AI-looking stock photo of patients in wheelchairs plus a nurse, vintage film cameras on tripods, softboxes, a wooden artist's mannequin on marionette strings, an engraved theatre proscenium, Adobe AE "AEP" file icons, kraft paper, a ruler and a pen.
- **Finishing / FX:** halftone screen (~4 px pitch at 1276 w), film grain, heavy oval vignette with **edge defocus** (corners soften like a vintage lens), mild chromatic fringing on card edges and the pen (blue/orange), deep-DoF blur on foreground props (≈ 15–25 px), soft drop shadows beneath floating objects (the device floats with a detached elliptical shadow ≈ 30% lower).

## 4. Motion & timing analysis
- **Pacing:** 0.43 cuts/s; shots 0.66–5.5 s, median ~1.8 s. The hook is the densest part (4 sets in 6.7 s). The proof section holds longer, calmer footage cards (1.3–2.1 s), and the emotional section uses 2.8–3.2 s shots with continuous camera moves. The CTA is the longest hold (5.5 s).
- **Easing signatures (from per_frame.csv):**
  - **Landing push / settle** after a zoom-through: exponential ease-out, peak in the first 1–2 frames, then ×0.7–0.8 per frame. Studio: 5.43 → 3.74 → 2.63 → 1.98 → 1.59 → 1.23 → 1.00 → 0.80 (f230–237), under 0.3 by f241. Theatre: 3.99 → 2.97 → 2.39 → 1.84… continuing for ~30 frames as a slow push. **No overshoot**: zoom never changes sign during a settle.
  - **Zoom-through exit:** ease-in acceleration over 6–7 frames (0.29 → 0.43 → 0.62 → 0.63 → 1.04 → 1.37 → 3.93), with the cut on the fastest frame. The next shot starts at similar velocity, so speed is continuous across the cut.
  - **Element fly-in (PC wall):** a 16-frame ease-out from −1.64 to 0 with a long tail, cubic-out like.
  - **Punch-in jump cut** (18.07): an instantaneous 1.3× scale with a residual lateral drift that decays over 10 frames (−2.15 → −0.16 px/frame).
  - **Type write-on:** per-letter or per-word, never per-line; no easing (pop-on).
- **Entrance / exit vocabulary:** typewriter with caret (serif); per-letter handwriting write-on (labels, chalkboard, CTA); torn-plate mask wipe L→R (4 frames); doodle stroke draw-on (ellipse 6–8 frames, X stroke 2–3 frames per stroke, arrow 4 frames); sticker scale-pop burst (1-frame stagger, ~4 frames to cover); field drop-away (5 frames); radial clock-wipe logo (8 frames); tag fly-in with rotation (4 frames); card pop up from below (chalkboard, 4 frames); staggered icon pop (2-frame stagger); plate slide-in from left (3 frames).
- **Camera:** almost every set has a camera move: landing push → hold → accelerating push into the next. Net zooms: PCs −14, footage shrink −24.6, studio +14.1, board +11.2, theatre +34.5. No pans or whip pans. All movement is along the z-axis.
- **Secondary motion:** blurred real hands moving at the desk edges, the rotating product turntable, the marionette animating, the blinking caret, live footage motion inside cards. Every hold therefore has something alive in it.
- **Sync:**
  - **Cuts are beat-snapped:** 10 of 11 cuts are within ±0.12 s of a 129-BPM beat (1.47/−0.04, 3.27/−0.05, 6.20/0.00, 6.73/+0.07, 10.00/+0.02, 11.40/0.00, 12.73/−0.09, 15.07/−0.12, 18.07/+0.05, 19.53/+0.10, 22.33/+0.06).
  - **Visual vs VO:** new sets lead their keyword by 0.15–0.3 s ("mouth" sticker field 6.20 vs 6.38; product 6.73 vs "Meet" 7.02; footage 10.00 vs "breakthrough" 10.16; studio 15.07 vs "For" 15.24). Where the beat grid lands later than the word, the cut trails by ≤ 0.19 s ("computer" 1.34 → cut 1.47; "it's" 19.34 → 19.53).

## 5. Transitions catalogue
1. **Hard cut on the beat** (1.47, 3.27, 11.40, 12.73): plain 1-frame cuts, always on a beat, always to a completely different set. Rebuild: `<Sequence>` boundaries snapped to `round(beat*fps)`.
2. **Sticker burst cover** (6.00–6.20): ~30 white-bordered mouth cut-outs scale 0→1 from the frame centre outward with a 1-frame stagger, at random rotations (±15°) and sizes (12–30% W), fully covering the frame in 4 frames. The scene behind is swapped while covered. Rebuild: an array of `<Img>` with `spring({frame: f - i*0.5, config: {damping: 14, stiffness: 260, mass: 0.5}})` scale; order by distance from centre.
3. **Drop-away reveal** (6.53–6.87): the sticker field translates down with acceleration (pan_y 0.9 → 3 → 6 px/frame at 180 px analysis width ≈ 42 px/frame real), uncovering the next set already in place behind. Rebuild: `translateY` with `Easing.in(Easing.cubic)` over 5 frames, plus per-sticker random delays of 0–2 frames.
4. **Zoom-blur punch into footage → shrink to card** (10.00–10.67): 1–2 frames of radial zoom blur on the incoming footage at ~140% scale, then the footage scales down to an inset rounded card (≈ 76% W) over ~10 frames of ease-out while the paper background is revealed. Rebuild: `scale: interpolate(f, [0, 10], [1.0/0.76, 1], {easing: Easing.out(Easing.cubic)})` on a container with `border-radius` interpolated 0 → 30 px; add a radial blur (SVG feGaussianBlur on a scaled copy, or 3 stacked copies at 1.02/1.04/1.06 scale at 30% opacity) for the first 2 frames.
5. **Zoom-through with kaleidoscope blur** (14.87–15.20): the card zooms in (+1.65/frame) and blurs; one frame shows a **mirrored-tile radial blur** of the next scene; then the studio lands on a 10-frame decelerating push. Rebuild: frame 0–1 scale the outgoing card 1 → 1.6 with motion blur; frame 2 render the incoming scene in a 3×3 mirrored tile (CSS `transform: scaleX(-1)` on alternating tiles) with heavy radial blur; frames 3+ incoming at `scale 1.25 → 1` with `Easing.out(Easing.exp)` over 12 frames.
6. **Punch-in jump cut** (18.07): the same chalkboard scene cut to ≈ 1.3× scale and slightly re-framed, on the beat, as the caption completes "another gadget". Rebuild: switch the scale from 1 to 1.3 on one frame, then a 10-frame `pan_x` settle.
7. **Accelerating zoom-through** (19.13–19.53, 21.80–22.33): ease-in push for 6–8 frames (scale up ≈ 1.25×), cut on the fastest frame, and the next scene enters already moving and decays. Rebuild: outgoing `scale: interpolate(f, [0, 7], [1, 1.25], {easing: Easing.in(Easing.quad)})`; incoming `scale: interpolate(f, [0, 25], [1.2, 1], {easing: Easing.out(Easing.exp)})`.

## 7. What makes it high-end (the transferable lessons)
1. **Build a physical set for every sentence.** Wood desk, studio, toy theatre, grid paper. The viewer believes in a tabletop world, not a slideshow.
2. **Foreground blur props add depth for free.** Real hands, film cameras, a pen and a ruler blurred at 15–25 px along the frame edges turn a flat collage into 2.5D.
3. **Show user footage inside a card, never raw.** A rounded inset (≈ 76% W, 30 px radius) on textured paper makes phone or interview footage look curated and lets the paper set and grade wrap it.
4. **Doodle emphasis instead of scale emphasis.** Circle, X, bracket and underline the key phrase in marker. One accent colour (mustard) for labels plus one alert colour (red) for negation and emphasis.
5. **Handwriting locked to the voice.** Labels write on per letter within ~0.1 s of each spoken word, so the viewer reads and hears the same thing at the same time.
6. **Z-axis camera only, with velocity continuity.** Ease-in pushes cut at peak speed and the next set lands with an exponential decay. No pans needed.
7. **Cuts snap to the music grid (±0.1 s)** even with VO on top. The edit feels musical without the music being loud.
8. **One finishing stack over everything:** halftone + grain + oval vignette with soft edges + slight chromatic fringe. This unifies stock photos, AI images, product renders and phone footage.
9. **Quiet open → loud second shot.** A calm serif typewriter line for 1.4 s, then a dense collage wall. The contrast is the hook.

## 8. Remotion rebuild recipe
- **Composition:** `1920×1080`, `30 fps` (reference delivered at 15 fps; double all frame counts below), duration = VO + 1.2 s CTA hold (~28 s = 840 frames).
- **Scene stack (bottom → top):** `<SetBackground src="wood|paper|studio|theatre|grid" />` → `<HeroCutout src shadow={{offsetY: 0.3, blur: 30, opacity: 0.35}} />` → `<ForegroundBlurProps items blur={20} />` → `<MarkerLabel>` / `<TornPlate>` → `<DoodleStroke type="ellipse|x|arrow|bracket" />` → `<CameraZ>` wrapper (scale/translate for push, punch, zoom-through) → `<FinishStack halftone={0.12} grain={0.06} vignette={0.55} edgeBlur={6} chroma={1.5} />`.
- **Components & timings (30 fps):**
  - `TypewriterSerif`: font Lora Italic 500, size 0.12·H, colour `#3a3638`, words from VO timestamps (show at word.start + 4 frames), container `translateX` keeps the caret at x ≈ 0.62·W; `<Caret color="#d8bd2a" width={4} blink={16}>`.
  - `TornPlate`: mustard `#ccb024` rect with an SVG torn-edge mask; reveal via `clip-path: inset(0 ${100-p}% 0 0)` with p over 8 frames, `Easing.out(Easing.quad)`.
  - `MarkerLabel`: Gochi Hand / Patrick Hand, per-letter reveal paced so the word finishes at the spoken word's end (`chars / (word.end − word.start)`), no fade (pop).
  - `DoodleStroke`: SVG path with a rough (wobbly) ellipse, X as two brush strokes (`stroke-linecap: round`, width 0.035·H), `strokeDashoffset` from length → 0 over 12–16 frames (ellipse), 5 frames per X stroke, 8 frames for arrows. Red `#d32a2e`, white `#f2f0ee`, or yellow `#e6d36a` at 85% opacity.
  - `StickerBurst`: N=30 mouth PNGs with an 8 px white stroke (CSS `filter: drop-shadow(0 0 0 white)` ×4 or pre-outlined PNGs), `spring({damping: 14, stiffness: 260, mass: 0.5})` scale, delay = `distanceFromCentre * 0.04` frames; exit `translateY(0 → 120%)`, `Easing.in(Easing.cubic)`, 10 frames.
  - `FootageCard`: `<OffthreadVideo>` in a div with `border-radius: 30px; overflow: hidden; box-shadow: 0 10px 40px rgba(0,0,0,.25)`; entry scale 1.32 → 1 over 20 frames `Easing.out(Easing.cubic)`, radius 0 → 30.
  - `ZoomThrough`: outgoing `scale 1 → 1.25` over 14 frames `Easing.in(Easing.quad)` + `filter: blur(0 → 6px)`; incoming `scale 1.2 → 1` over 50 frames `Easing.out(Easing.exp)` (slow push tail).
  - `PunchIn`: scale step 1 → 1.3 on the beat frame, `translateX` residual of 3% W decaying with `spring({damping: 20, stiffness: 120})`.
  - `RadialWipeLogo`: SVG circle with `conic-gradient` mask angle 0 → 360° over 16 frames.
  - `ChalkboardCaption`: black `#1b1a1c` card with a 3 px gold `#b59a3c` border, pop from `translateY(30%) scale(.9)` with `spring({damping: 12, stiffness: 200})`, then per-letter text.
  - `CTAEndcard`: torn-photo card on grid paper; `EDITORS` per letter (Bungee, `#d0282c`, 3 px dark stroke); AE icons `spring({damping: 10, stiffness: 220, mass: .6})` with a 4-frame stagger; mustard URL plate slides from `x = −40%` over 6 frames, then per-letter type.
- **Tricky effects:** **halftone**: an SVG `<pattern>` of dots at 4–6 px pitch blended `multiply` at 10–15%, or a WebGL shader thresholding luminance into dots. **Edge defocus**: duplicate the frame, blur 8 px, mask it with a radial gradient (transparent centre → opaque corners). **Kaleidoscope zoom-blur frame**: render the incoming scene in a 3×3 grid with alternating `scale(-1,1)`/`scale(1,-1)`, then scale 0.5 → 1 with a radial blur for 2 frames. **Marionette**: rig the mannequin PNG in limb segments (upper arm/forearm pivots) and drive rotation with `Math.sin(frame/12)·8°`, adding strings as SVG lines from fixed top points to the hand/head anchors.

## 9. Variation ideas
