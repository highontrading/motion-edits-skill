The viewer gets the story and the proof of craft at the same time, and the edit doubles as an ad for the project file.

## 1. Concept & script structure
- **Topic / genre:** brand-origin micro-story (the Lamborghini–Ferrari insult legend), packaged as a behind-the-scenes "how this edit was built" promo for an editor marketplace.
- **Narrative beats:**
  - 0.00–4.42 **Hook:** "One insult created one of the greatest supercar brands in history." Title-like claim with a superlative.
  - 4.90–9.42 **Setup:** Ferruccio owned a Ferrari, and its clutch kept failing.
  - 9.72–15.78 **Conflict:** he went to Enzo Ferrari, and Enzo told him to "stick to making tractors".
  - 16.30–21.22 **Turn:** Lamborghini did the opposite and built a car to beat Ferrari.
  - 21.76–26.64 **Payoff (callback to the hook):** "that's how one insult started one of the biggest rivalries in automotive history."
  - 27.62–32.02 **CTA:** "Editors, grab the After Effects project file on earnedits.com."
- **Hook mechanics (first 1.5 s):** the frame is already busy at frame 0. There are two white UI labels ("Assets", "Animation"), a column of four bright green-screen thumbnails (#13c522), a preview window where a cut-out Ferruccio stands in front of a Lamborghini garage, and an AE timeline with coloured layer bars that is already scrolling. A comic speech bubble types "One" → "One Insult" (0.27–0.53 s) in time with the VO ("One" 0.00, "insult" 0.44). At 1.27–1.40 s the asset column and preview swap to a red supercar on a road. The green thumbnails and the moving timeline make the viewer look twice ("what am I looking at?").
- **Script → visual rule:** one preview segment per VO clause (12 segments). Each segment has (a) a literal comp (person, car, key, pedal, office, garage), (b) one typed keyword or a phrase from the VO, and (c) its own asset set shown at left. The assets always change 1–2 frames before the preview does.

## 2. Visual language
- **Art direction:** a meta UI collage. The background is a darkened (~25–30% brightness) movie-style poster for the edit ("TWO MEN. TWO VISIONS. ONE RIVALRY THAT CHANGED THE WORLD", Enzo in sunglasses, LAMBORGHINI lettering), so the context never leaves the frame. On top sit three zones:
  1. **Assets board:** x ≈ 6–37%, y ≈ 8–68%. Green-screen thumbnails and cut-outs in a loose 2-column masonry, 1–7 tiles at a time.
  2. **Animation preview:** x ≈ 41–91%, y ≈ 14–64%. A 9:16 window (358×637 px at 720 w, i.e. 50% of frame width) with a 1 px light border and soft shadow.
  3. **AE timeline:** y ≈ 70–100%. Dark grey rows (`#3b3b3b`/`#333`), a green work-area bar on top, a time ruler (8, 9, 10, 11 s), a red playhead and a mouse cursor. The layer bars are in AE label colours: lavender `#a3a3c6`, maroon `#9c3a4a`, pink/magenta `#c35ea9`, blue `#4a5fc0`, mustard `#c8b44a`. They form a **descending staircase** (each layer's in-point is later than the one above), so the timeline visibly "steps" with each segment.
- **Inside the preview (the edit itself):** archival B/W photos composited with colour cut-outs on paper-card backgrounds, plus AI-style cinematic stills (the two men facing off between red cars, a top-down desk flat-lay for the CTA). Recurring props: a red Lamborghini, the Ferrari shield, a car key, a clutch-pedal photo in a red frame, a comic speech bubble, an angry-vein emoji sticker, a roller-shutter garage, a V12 engine.
- **Colour:**

  - Preview accents: Lamborghini/Ferrari red `#c41e1e`, Ferrari yellow `#f2c200`, highlighter yellow `#e8cf3a`, and the CTA mustard `#ceaf20`/`#a88f1d`.

  - **UI labels:** "Assets" and "Animation" in white neo-grotesk (SF Pro / Helvetica Neue Regular), sentence case, ~17% and ~26% of frame width, no effects.
  - **Preview type (one style per segment):**
    - Comic bold-italic serif in a speech bubble ("One Insult").

- **Assets:** green-screen cut-outs (people, lamps, chairs, cars, hands), archival B/W photos (Ferruccio portrait, Ferruccio with model car, Ferruccio with V12), AI cinematic key art, a phone with the earnedits site, an AE "AEP" icon, headphones, a keyboard, a camera, waving-hand stickers and an angry-vein emoji.
- **Finishing / FX:** the darkened poster plate (~70% black overlay), a subtle drop shadow under the preview window. Inside the preview: paper grain, a vignette on B/W photos, rounded-corner photo cards (r ≈ 12% of card width) on black, and light flicker from a swinging lamp in the Enzo office comp (luma oscillates 54–57 over 13.6–14.4 s).

## 4. Motion & timing analysis
- **Pacing:** segments of 1.4–4.5 s, median ~2.9 s (≈ 0.37 changes/s). The frame never stops moving, though, because the AE timeline scrolls continuously and each segment has 2–4 internal reveals, so the effective "event" rate is ~1.2 per second.
- **The A→P stagger:** at all 11 segment changes the asset column swaps exactly **2 frames (133 ms) before** the preview (1.27/1.40, 4.73/4.87, 6.13/6.27, 10.20/10.33, 12.87/13.00, 16.00/16.13, 19.13/19.27, 21.67/21.80, 23.27/23.40, 27.73/27.87, 30.60/30.73). The eye reads "ingredients → result" on every beat.
- **Beat quantisation:** preview swaps land within ±0.1 s of a 152 BPM beat in 8 of 11 cases (19.27/19.27, 23.40/23.38, 30.73/30.72, 4.87/4.76, 6.27/6.36, 10.33/10.24, 13.00/13.10, 27.87/27.96), while staying within −0.17…+0.27 s of the VO clause start.
- **Easing signatures:** everything inside the preview is fast ease-out at 15 fps. Pops are 2–4 frames (133–270 ms) with a small overshoot (shield, bubble, sign). Slides are 4–5 frames (note, hand). Reveals that carry a camera feel decay over ~10 frames (the garage shutter: diff 24.8, 8.9, 7.8, 7.1, 6.5, 5.6 …, i.e. a quick ease-out). The only continuous motion is slow push-ins (zoom +0.2 to +1.3 per frame at 180 px analysis scale).
- **Entrance / exit vocabulary:** typewriter (20–24 chars/s, 1–2 chars per 15 fps frame), word-by-word speech bubble, word stack, hand-drawn ellipse / circle / underline write-ons (6–8 f), label box that grows from the left then types, badge that grows from the centre then types, photo card tilt-in (3 f), cut-out slide-in with a landing bounce (car), hand slam (5 f), shutter reveal (10 f), sticker pops (2–3 f).
- **Camera:** none on the outer frame (net pan 0). The preview has gentle push-ins on almost every segment and one pull-back (the walk, segment 5).
- **Secondary motion:** the AE timeline scrolls with the layer staircase stepping down, the playhead line and cursor move, the lamp swings and flickers, the car bounces, the hand bobs, and the ghost text drifts.
- **Sync:** typed text is word-locked to VO (Ferruccio 4.90 → typing starts 5.07; "clutch" 9.10 ↔ circle 9.07; "tractors" 14.98 ↔ bubble 14.47–14.67; "automotive history" 25.68–26.12 ↔ typing 25.53–26.40; ".com" 31.46 ↔ 31.73). Strong music onsets coincide with the hand slam (7.20), the bubble (14.70), the garage (19.78), the word stack (21.87) and the CTA (27.96), which points to SFX hits on reveals.

## 5. Transitions catalogue
1. **Staggered A→P hard switch** (all 11 segment changes): asset tiles swap on frame N, the preview content on frame N+2. No dissolve, no motion. Rebuild: two `<Sequence>`s per segment, with the assets `from = segStart - 2`.
2. **Assets-column clear** (21.67, 30.60): the asset board empties for the pure-type segment and the logo end card, which signals "this is just type" and focuses the eye. One frame, no fade.
3. **Inner reveals that act as transitions:** the sky plate dropping into the top half of the paper card (2.33–2.40, 2 f), the roller shutter rising (19.27–19.93), the photo-card tilt swap (17.27, 3 f), and the note sliding up over the key (7.93–8.27). Each one changes the scene without cutting the outer frame.
4. **Hard cut to a brand-colour end card** (30.73): the preview fills with mustard yellow, on the VO word "earnedits".

## 7. What makes it high-end (the transferable lessons)
1. **Show the ingredients next to the dish.** A raw-assets column + a live preview + a scrolling timeline turns a 30 s story into proof of craft. It suits any template, preset or course promo.
2. **Assets lead the result by 2 frames** at every change, a tiny cause → effect cue that makes the layout feel intentional rather than a screen recording.
3. **Keep the story legible inside a 50%-width window:** one subject per segment, ≤ 4 words of type, and B/W photos with one red/yellow accent so it reads at thumbnail size.
4. **A different type treatment per beat** (bubble, serif, typewriter, mono stack, badge, label) keeps a single-frame layout from feeling static. Colour discipline (black/white + red/yellow) holds it together.
5. **Hand-drawn marks on the noun** (an ellipse on "History", a circle on the clutch, an underline on the name), always landing on the spoken word.
6. **The background is the poster of the finished piece**, darkened ~70%, so the context and title stay present without competing.
7. **Cut on the beat, within the clause:** swaps snap to the 152 BPM grid inside a ±0.2 s window around the VO.
8. **Callback hook/payoff** ("one insult" in the first and last story lines) closes the loop before the CTA.

## 8. Remotion rebuild recipe
- **Composition:** `1080×1920`, `30 fps` (or 15 fps for the authentic stepped look; render the inner preview at 15 with `Math.floor(frame/2)`), duration ≈ VO + 0.4 s (~975 frames @30).
- **Layer stack:** `<PosterBackground src darken={0.7}>` → `<Label text="Assets" x=6% y=6%>` + `<Label text="Animation" x=53% y=10%>` → `<AssetBoard tiles={seg.assets} region={{x:6%,y:8%,w:31%,h:60%}} from={seg.start-4}>` (frames @30) → `<PreviewWindow x=41% y=14% w=50% aspect=9/16 border="1px rgba(255,255,255,.25)" shadow>` holding `<Series>` of segment comps → `<FakeTimeline y=70% h=30% layers={seg.layers} pxPerSec={240} playheadX=50%>`.
- **Components:**
  - `AssetBoard`: masonry of thumbnails with green-screen tiles (`background:#13c522`) holding PNG cut-outs, a hard swap with no animation; `from = segStart - 4` (@30 fps).
  - `FakeTimeline`: rows 18 px high, `#3b3b3b` with 1 px `#2c2c2c` separators. Bars use the AE palette `['#a3a3c6','#9c3a4a','#c35ea9','#4a5fc0','#c8b44a']`, laid out as a staircase (bar i starts at `segStart + i*3f`). Translate the whole strip `x = -frame*pxPerFrame`, add a red 2 px playhead and a cursor sprite wobbling ±6 px.
  - `TypeOn`: `text.slice(0, Math.floor((frame-start)*charsPerFrame))` with `charsPerFrame = 0.75` @30 (≈ 22 chars/s), start = word timestamp.
  - `DrawnEllipse` / `MarkerCircle` / `Underline`: an SVG path with `strokeDasharray = len`, `strokeDashoffset` interpolated len → 0 over 8 f @30 (`Easing.out(Easing.quad)`), a 6–8 px yellow `#f2d43a` or black stroke with round caps and slight path wobble.
  - `SpeechBubble`: a white bubble with a black 4 px outline; `spring({damping: 9, stiffness: 260, mass: 0.6})` scale 0 → 1 (~6 f with a ~10% overshoot), plus an anger-vein sticker that pops 2 f later.
  - `PhotoCardSwap`: a rounded rect `borderRadius: 24`, entering at `rotate(8deg) scale(.92)` → `rotate(0) scale(1)` over 6 f, ease-out.
  - `WordStack`: words in heavy mono at sizes `[1, 2, 2, 1]×base`; each appears at its word time −4 f with a 2 f scale 1.1 → 1.
  - `GrowBadge`: a red `#c41e1e` rounded rect with a 3 px white inset border; `scaleX` 0 → 1 from the centre over 6 f, then `TypeOn` for its label.
  - `ShutterReveal`: a corrugated-door PNG translating `y: 0 → -100%` over 20 f with `Easing.out(Easing.cubic)`.
  - `EndCard`: a radial gradient `#e8c832 → #a88f1d`, black logotype boxes; the ".COM" box appended on the word timestamp.
- **Tricky bits:**
  - Keep the preview window as its own `<AbsoluteFill>` with `overflow:hidden` so inner pushes never spill.
  - Drive both the board and the preview from one `segments[]` JSON (start, assets[], comp, captions[]) derived from the Whisper word timestamps, then snap each `start` to the nearest beat if it's within 0.12 s.

## 9. Variation ideas
1. **Template/preset marketplace ad** (any niche): keep the 3-zone layout and swap the story for a 25 s product demo. The assets column shows the user's raw clips, the preview the templated result, and the timeline sells "it's all editable".
2. **Course / tutorial teaser:** replace the AE timeline with a code editor or Figma layers panel scrolling in sync. The assets board becomes "files you get", and the preview plays the finished lesson output.
3. **Brand-history explainer for a client** (any founder-insult or rivalry story): drop the process UI, enlarge the preview to full frame, and keep the segment grammar (B/W archival + one red/yellow accent, a different type device per beat, hand-drawn marks on nouns).
