## 1. Concept & script structure
- **Genre:** opinion micro-essay / "industry exposé" with a template-sale CTA.
- **Beats:**
  - 0.0–3.3 **Hook (reframe question):** "What if aging was never the problem, but the fear of it was?"
  - 3.8–8.4 **History / accusation:** for decades the beauty industry turned wrinkles into flaws and aging into an enemy.
  - 9.1–12.2 **Mechanism:** once you fear getting older, there's always something to sell you.
  - 12.6–14.4 **List of three:** creams, serums, treatments (one shot each, 0.8–1.5 s).
  - 14.9–17.5 **Concession + turn:** skin care can improve skin but can't stop time.
  - 17.8–21.8 **Thesis:** the industry didn't invent aging, it learned to profit from the fear of it.
  - 21.8–27.1 **CTA:** "Editors, grab the After Effects project file on earnedits.com."
- **Hook mechanics (first 1.5 s):** frame 0 is already a full composition: a soft-focus grey-haired woman's face cut-out centred, the headline WHAT IF AGING (2 lines, ~65% FW) bottom-left, the small floating words WAS / NEVER / THE / PROBLEM around the face, and white serif "?" glyphs and dots drifting in the foreground. The camera immediately drifts down (pan_y +5 at f4), then pushes in on the face (zoom peak +7.35 at 1.50 s). At ~1.25 s a **red wavy ribbon wipes across the face** carrying a young engraved face: the old/young contrast lands before the first sentence ends.
- **Script → visual rule:** each noun becomes a cut-out object (decades → year counter, beauty industry → cosmetics pile, wrinkles → red surgical dash-lines on the face, fear → screaming halftone face, sell → coins and a dead piggy bank, creams/serums/treatments → tube, dropper, head full of tools, can't stop time → clock rings, profit → fan of dollar bills, fear → anatomical heart). The keyword of each clause is set big; the connecting words sit small around it (stacked-type layout).

## 2. Visual language
- **Art direction:** editorial **vintage-print collage** in one colour world. Every asset is grey-scale and treated as halftone, line-screen or stipple-engraving, sitting on a single deep-red velvet ground with heavy vignette. There is no full-colour photo except the faces of the women in shots 1 and 7, which stay desaturated. It reads like a dark fashion-magazine spread.
- **Backgrounds & textures:** a constant oxblood ground (`#360908` → `#691d1f` centre glow, `#1c0101` corners), a fine hex/dot halftone visible across the frame, film grain, and soft dust specks/"light flecks" (short white streaks drifting like embers). The ground never changes, so scene identity comes only from the hero object.
- **Colour:** 3-value palette plus one accent: deep red (`#380908`, `#4f0e0f`, `#691d1f`), warm grey/cream for objects and type (`#ada7a5`, `#bcb6b2`, `#e9e1d6`), near-black `#1c0101`. **Accent rules:** signal red `#ff3b3b` for the surgical dash marks and neon scratch crosses; dusty rose `#c25a5f` for "BUT"; pink `#e8a0a0` for the glowing heart; warm amber `#d98b3a` only for the microscope light cone. Inverted negative frames (cyan/white, luma ~172) are the only cool colour, and they last 1 frame.
- **Typography:**
  - **Primary: a high-contrast flared display serif** (wedge/flared serifs, tall x-height, ball terminals on J and G). It is the Roxborough CF / Gloock / Fraunces SuperSoft-Black / Bagnard class. ALL CAPS, off-white `#e9e1d6` with a **soft outer glow** (~8–12 px, 40% white) so it looks backlit. Scale ladder: hero word 60–85% FW (AGING, SELL, FILES), mid words 35–55% FW (WHAT IF AGING, DECADES, GETTING OLDER, PROJECT), whisper words 8–15% FW (WAS, NEVER, 2026, CREAMS, ALWAYS).
  - **Stacked/staircase layout:** phrases break into a zig-zag of short lines with varied sizes and indents (BUT / THE FEAR / OF IT / WAS; SOMETHING / TO / FIGHT; IT JUST / LEARNED; HOW TO / PROFIT).
  - **Tracked micro-labels:** "S E R U M S" with ~0.6em letter-spacing under the main word; mirrored or upside-down "ALWAYS" repeated along a circle.
  - **Lowercase script-ish serif accent:** "grap the" (sic, a typo for "grab") in a softer italic serif at ~15% FW, tucked under EDITORS.
  - **Handwritten chalk accents:** a white chalk circle round "SOMETHING TO FIGHT" with a curved arrow; grunge chalk tally-marks and crosses; red neon scribbles.
  - **Text-on-arc:** TREATMENTS letters burst out of the top of a head along an arc, each letter rotated.
- **Layout:** the hero object sits centred or slightly off-centre at 45–60% frame height. The hero word sits directly below or behind it, and small satellite words orbit at 15–25% distances. The camera often frames things partially (AGING cropped to "GING", DECADES to "ECADES") because it is *moving through* a larger canvas. That cropping is intentional and signals a world bigger than the frame.
- **Assets:** desaturated photo faces (woman with grey hair; dark-haired woman who morphs into her older self), stipple-engraved portrait cut-outs, skulls and half-skull split faces, cosmetics (petroleum-jelly tub, lipsticks, dropper bottle), a halftone cream tube with a swirl, a dropper with droplets and glossy bubbles, a halftone head with surgical tools, a piggy bank with X eyes, US coins, a fan of dollar bills, a halftone microscope, an anatomical heart, outlined clock rings, and Ae ".AEP" file icons.
- **Finishing / FX:** halftone and line-screen treatment on every object (dot size ~3–5 px at 720 w; the FEAR face uses a coarse square line-screen), a strong radial vignette (corners −60% luma), outer glow on type and key objects, **RGB split + horizontal ghosting on the FEAR shot**, floating ember flecks, light flicker on the end card (luma ±8 every 2–4 frames), a final fade to black.

## 4. Motion & timing analysis
- **Pacing:** slow by Shorts standards in *cuts* (8 transitions in 27 s, 0.3/s) but fast in *events*: a new word, object or camera move every 0.3–0.6 s. The list section (12.6–14.9) is the fastest: three objects in 2.3 s. The end card holds 6 s with a slow build.
- **Effective frame rate:** each 4th frame is a duplicate, so motion advances on a 3-on/1-hold cadence (~18 fps). This gives a faint stutter that suits the print look. When rebuilding at 24 fps, use `Math.floor(frame * 0.75) / 0.75` to reproduce it, or just render at 18 fps.
- **Easing signatures (per_frame.csv):**
  - **Camera pushes/pans inside shots are smooth ease-in-out with an out-bias:** push 1.29–1.92 rises 5 frames and decays 10; pan 19.75–20.4 rises 5 and decays ~12; the zoom-out 3.5–4.6 is near-symmetric (12 in, 14 out ≈ `easeInOutCubic` over 26 frames). Peaks are 5–9 px/frame at 180 px analysis width (≈20–36 px/frame at 720).
  - **Whips are snap-starts:** 14.42 starts at its peak (pan_y −13.7 ≈ 55 px/frame) and decays over 6 frames (pure ease-out). The exit whip at 7.0 is a 2–3 frame ease-in into the cut.
  - **Landings:** heart +1.6 → +0.2 over 12 frames; cream tube +2.1 → +1.0 over 6 frames. No overshoot anywhere (no sign flips): the camera is weighty and calm, matching the VO.
  - **Punch-ins as exits:** 2 frames of scale-up + blur on the last hero word (SELL, TREATMENTS) right before the cut.
- **Entrance / exit vocabulary:** wavy ribbon mask wipe; strip-slice peel (banner cut into a band that slides off); year counter ticker (~10 values over 1.5 s, roughly 1 change per 4 frames); slide-in object piles from the frame edge; dashed-line write-on (red surgical marks, ~12 frames); chalk circle + arrow write-on (~5 frames); rapid portrait substitution (2–6 frames per face); letter-by-letter typewriter (DIDN'T INVENT over ~18 frames, URL over ~18 frames); rise-from-below for heavy words (AGING, GETTING OLDER); text-on-arc letter burst (TREATMENTS); object assembly from parts (microscope); light-switch reveal (light cone on); face-age morph swap; icon pop (Ae tiles).
- **Camera:** almost every shot is a 2.5D "virtual camera over a giant collage" (net pan_x −51 and −70 in shots 1 and 8, net zoom +39 in shot 1). Moves chain one after another with ≤0.5 s holds, so the frame is rarely static. Rotation is used only for drama beats (roll twist into SELL, ±6.5°/frame; FEAR jitter).
- **Secondary motion:** ember flecks drifting upward, bubbles rising, coins orbiting, the piggy wobbling, end-card light flicker, a heartbeat-glow pulse on the heart.
- **Sync:**
  - **VO is the clock, not the beat.** Cuts land on the first syllable of the new noun: "aging" 7.12 vs cut 7.12 (0.00), "feared" 9.62 vs invert flash 9.58 (−0.04), "there" 10.76 vs 10.79 (+0.03), "Creams" 12.60 vs 12.58 (−0.02), "Skin" 14.92 vs 14.92 (0.00). "Serums" 13.28 vs 13.42 (+0.14) and "The industry" 17.84 vs 17.96 (+0.12) are slightly late.
  - Words appear 0–0.3 s after they are spoken: BEAUTY 4.5 vs 4.54; SELL 11.75 vs 11.76; YOU 12.0 vs 11.98; AGING (microscope) 19.0 vs 18.90; PROJECT 24.0 vs 23.88; FILES 24.25 vs 24.32. Connector words lag more (+0.25–0.4).
  - Only 4 of 8 transitions sit within ±0.1 s of a beat (7.12/7.08, 9.58/9.66, 14.92/15.02, 20.92/20.88). The music supports rather than drives the edit.
  - The two invert flashes bracket the "fear" idea: one on "feared" and one right before "fear of it". It is a semantic flash rule, not a rhythmic one.

## 5. Transitions catalogue
1. **Invert flash (negative frame)** at 9.58 and 20.92: 1 frame of the *incoming or outgoing* image colour-inverted (red → cyan, dark → white, luma ~172), then a hard cut. Rebuild: `filter: invert(1) hue-rotate(180deg)` on a single frame, optionally with a −8% scale jolt.
2. **Whip pan (directional)** at 7.0 (left), 10.67 (up) and 14.42 (down, in-shot): 2–3 frames of 40–55 px/frame motion with directional blur, then landing in the new area of the canvas or a hard cut. Rebuild: translate the whole canvas with `Easing.in(Easing.cubic)` over 3 frames plus an SVG directional blur (`stdDeviation="0 18"` for vertical).
3. **Punch-in exit** at 12.50 and 14.83: the last frame pair scales the hero word ~1.3× with blur, then cut. This makes even hard cuts feel propelled.
4. **Ribbon/strip mask wipe** at 1.25–1.75 and 3.75–4.25: a sinuous red band (SVG path with a wave) sweeps across, carrying new content inside it; later the band is reduced to a thin diagonal strip and pulled out. Rebuild: `clipPath` with an animated wave path (sine amplitude ~6% height) and translateX of the inner layer at a different speed (parallax inside the mask).
5. **Organic iris-close + texture gap** at 17.42–17.92: a dark, fuzzy-edged vignette closes over the face in ~8 frames, followed by 4 frames of dark grey grunge texture before the microscope builds. Rebuild: radial-gradient mask with a noise-displaced edge (SVG `feTurbulence` + `feDisplacementMap`), radius 70% → 0 over 8 frames, then a 4-frame `<TextureCard>`.
6. **Scale-up from a small cluster** at 14.92: the new scene starts as a small collage cluster (~35% FW) in the middle of the red void and expands to fill the frame in ~3 frames plus a second surge.
7. **Rapid substitution** at 7.12–9.5: same position, different portrait every 2–6 frames. It is a flicker cut that works as both transition and secondary motion.
8. **Fade to black** at 26.4–27.1 for the end, the only dissolve-type move in the piece.

## 7. What makes it high-end (the transferable lessons)
1. **One colour world, many objects.** Lock the ground (oxblood + vignette) and the treatment (grey halftone cut-outs + glowing cream serif). Then any object can appear and it still looks like one designed piece.
2. **Halftone/engraving everything:** photos, illustrations and 3D-ish props become one family once they share a dot or line screen.
3. **Move the camera, not the cut.** A 7-second opening built from chained eased pans and pushes over one large canvas feels more premium than 6 hard cuts.
4. **Editorial serif + staircase stacking** makes a social video read like a magazine cover. Vary sizes 8:1 between hero and whisper words.
5. **Objects as arguments:** a counter for "decades", a dead piggy bank for "sell", a microscope for "didn't invent". Every claim gets a prop.
6. **Semantic flashes:** reserve the harshest effect (invert frame, RGB split) for the emotional keyword ("fear"), and keep the rest calm.
7. **Exits with energy, landings without overshoot:** 2-frame punch-ins and snap whips out of shots, then smooth 10–14 frame ease-outs in. That fits a serious tone.
8. **Leave crops in.** Words cut off by the frame edge ("GING", "ECADES") imply a bigger world and keep the eye moving.
9. **Proofread the end card.** The "grap the" typo is the one amateur tell in an otherwise polished piece.

## 8. Remotion rebuild recipe
- **Composition:** `1080×1920`, `24 fps` (optionally hold every 4th frame for the 18 fps cadence), ~650 frames. Put all content on **one large world canvas** (e.g. 3000×5000 px) and animate a `<Camera>` transform (translate/scale/rotate) through keyframed "stations" synced to `words.json`.
- **Layer stack (bottom → top):** `<RedVelvetGround colors={['#691d1f','#380908','#1c0101']} radial>` → `<HexHalftoneTexture opacity={0.12}>` → world canvas: `<HalftoneCutout>` objects + `<SerifWord>` type → `<Embers count={25} drift="up">` → `<Grain opacity={0.07}>` → `<Vignette strength={0.6}>` → `<Flicker amp={0.06} every={[2,4]}>` (end card only).
- **Components:**
  - `Camera({stations})`: each station `{x, y, scale, rot, at}`; interpolate with `Easing.inOut(Easing.cubic)` over 20–26 frames for drifts and `Easing.bezier(0.4,0,0.2,1)` over 12–15 frames for pushes. Whip = 3 frames `Easing.in(Easing.cubic)` + directional blur.
  - `HalftoneCutout({src, screen:'dot'|'line', cell:4})`: an SVG filter or pre-processed PNG; desaturate + multiply the dot pattern; add `drop-shadow(0 6px 12px rgba(0,0,0,.5))`.
  - `SerifWord({text, size, glow:10})`: Roxborough/Gloock-class font, `color:#e9e1d6`, `textShadow:'0 0 10px rgba(255,240,230,.45)'`. Entrance: translateY 30%→0 + opacity over 8 frames, `Easing.out(Easing.cubic)`, at word start (+0–6 frames).
  - `StaircaseStack({lines:[{t:'BUT',size:1,color:'#c25a5f'},{t:'THE FEAR',indent:.3}…]})`.
  - `YearTicker({from:1009,to:1941,frames:36,steps:9})`: stepwise values, not continuous.
  - `DashedSurgeryLines`: SVG paths with `strokeDasharray="6 6"`, `#ff3b3b`, glow, drawn by `strokeDashoffset` over 12 frames, staggered 2 frames.
  - `ChalkCircle` + `ChalkArrow`: rough SVG path, a turbulence-displaced stroke, write-on over 5 frames.
  - `PortraitFlicker({srcs, holdFrames:[2,6]})`: same position, swaps images.
  - `RibbonMask`: an animated wave `clipPath`; inner content parallaxes at 0.6× camera speed.
  - `Typewriter({text, cps:12})` for DIDN'T INVENT and the URL.
  - `LightCone`: a trapezoid with a `linear-gradient(#d98b3a cc → transparent)`, opacity 0→1 in 3 frames with a 2-frame flicker.
  - `TextOnArc`: per-letter positions along an arc, scattered outward with a spring `{damping: 18, stiffness: 120}`.
  - `InvertFlash`: 1 frame of `filter: invert(1) hue-rotate(180deg) brightness(1.2)`.
  - `PunchExit`: last 2 frames scale 1 → 1.3 + `blur(6px)`.
  - `IrisClose({edge:'fur'})`: radial mask with `feDisplacementMap` noise, 8 frames.
- **FEAR chaos layer:** 10–15 copies of "FEAR" at random rotations, sizes and mirroring; RGB split (red/cyan channels offset ±6 px); `translate` jitter of ±4 px per frame; line-screen face via a `repeating-linear-gradient` mask.
