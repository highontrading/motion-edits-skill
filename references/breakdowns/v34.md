# v34 — "Before Amazon there was Sears": AI-caricature business-history short with kinetic stacked type

**Source creator handle:** earn.edits · **Format:** 720×1280, 9:16, 16 fps (delivered file; duplicate frames in the cut sheets point to a re-encode of a 24/30 fps master), 37.6 s · **Shots:** ~22 real (26 detected; detections at 7.44, 22.06, 22.69 and 26.31 s are the second frames of 2-frame flash transitions, and 26.69 s is a background tint ramp, not cuts) · **Avg shot:** ~1.7 s · **Music BPM:** ~86 (soft bed under the VO) · **VO:** yes, English, calm documentary-narrator delivery

**Style tags:** 3d-render, documentary-vo, kinetic-type, word-by-word-captions, stacked-type, giant-keyword, outline-type, glow, scanlines, vignette, motion-blur, depth-of-field, whip-pan, object-wipe, light-leak, counter-ticker, spring-overshoot, cta-endcard

**One-line essence:** AI-generated 3D "bobblehead" caricature scenes (colour = present, B/W = 1890s past) alternate with near-black textured title cards on which the narration builds itself as a centred stack of heavy caps, white connectors and huge red keywords. Every scene change is a short physical event (whip, thrown object, truck wipe, 2-frame invert flash, light sweep) that lands ~0.1 s before the VO phrase.

## 1. Concept & script structure
- **Genre:** business-history micro-documentary ("modern brand → forgotten predecessor → how it worked → scale → moral → CTA"). It uses the same narrative template as v05 (earn.edits house style), here with AI-character visuals.
- **Beats:**
  - 0.0–4.8 **Hook:** "Before Amazon delivered to your door, this man was already doing it with a piece of paper." A colour Amazon delivery man walks up to a door, then a B/W peephole POV shows the same man holding up a sheet of paper.
  - 5.0–11.2 **Setup:** "In 1896, Richard Sears discovered…" Sears stands on a floating island in the sky; people in small towns lacked access to products.
  - 11.6–18.6 **Turn / mechanism:** "So he created a simple solution, a mail-order catalog." Customers pick items (a red tick), send the order (the catalog flies into a mailbox) and wait (a vintage truck passes).
  - 18.96–25.8 **Scale:** "Soon Sears was selling almost everything: watches, clothes, tools, and even houses. It became a retail empire." A fast product montage with price tags, then a giant "EMPIRE".
  - 26.2–30.7 **Moral:** "The secret wasn't inventing a new product. It was finding a new way to reach customers."
  - 31.2–37.6 **CTA:** "Editors, learn faster inside a real project file. Grab yours on earnedits.com."
- **Hook mechanics (first 1.5 s):** the first frame is defocused and slightly zoomed. Over ~16 frames the image pulls back (zoom −2.4 → −0.75 per frame, an exponential ease-out) and racks into focus while the cartoon courier walks in from the left holding an Amazon box. A famous brand is spoken at word 2 ("Amazon" at 0.24 s) and the brand appears on the box at the same time. The 1.8 s shot ends with an accelerating camera roll (rot −1.0 → −2.4 °/frame over 6 frames), which throws the viewer into the peephole POV.
- **Script → visual rule:** a story clause gets a full AI-generated scene with a character or prop. An abstract or summary clause ("So he created a simple solution", "Soon Sears was selling almost everything", "It was finding a new way…") gets a **black title card with stacked kinetic type**. The ratio is ~50/50 and the two alternate. List items ("watches, clothes, tools, houses") get **one 0.6 s product card each**.

## 2. Visual language
- **Art direction:** mixed. (a) AI-generated 3D caricature characters with oversized heads (Pixar/bobblehead look) in photoreal AI environments: a suburban house, a sepia study, a floating rock island in the sky. (b) Kinetic-type cards on dark textured backgrounds. (c) Cut-out product props on glowing starburst badges. Colour vs. B/W encodes time: the present (Amazon courier, house) is in colour, and Sears and the 1890s are in **desaturated B/W on a saturated red** background.
- **Backgrounds & textures:**
  - dark cards: charcoal `#161516`/`#202020`/`#0d0d0d` with a woven-fabric/noise texture and a heavy radial vignette;
  - red cards: `#6c0e10` centre → `#430809`/`#2e0506` edges (vignetted velvet red);
  - sky cards (shot 3 and CTA): saturated cyan sky `#1cafd5`/`#2db4de` with white clouds `#abcad2`/`#aed1dd`;
  - sepia study for the catalog and product montage: `#150f0c`, `#2e231b`, paper `#d3ba9a`, with a strong DOF blur on the room behind;
  - **horizontal scanlines** (~3 px pitch at 1280 h, ~6–8% opacity) over every frame, which gives a CRT/VHS sheen.
- **Colour:** each card has one hue + neutrals. Rules: **red `#d0161b` = keyword** (SEARS, LIMITED ACCESS, TOWNS, WASN'T INVENTING, EDITORS, EARNEDITS.COM); **white = connector words**; **neon outline green `#22d33a`** for the positive "solution" phrase (A SIMPLE); **neon outline blue `#1e9bff`** for the moral (A NEW WAY); yellow `#f2c22c` for small labels (IN, RICHARD). Each product in the montage gets its own starburst colour: orange `#f5a623` (watch), magenta `#e85fe0` (underwear), lime `#a6f03a` (hammer), golden yellow `#f7b733` (house).
- **Typography:**
  - Primary: an ultra-heavy, slightly condensed grotesk with soft corners, ALL CAPS (looks like **Bricolage Grotesque ExtraBold/Condensed**, or an Archivo Black / Obviously Narrow Black class). Tight tracking (≈ −1%) and tight leading (≈ 0.85).
  - **Size hierarchy inside one stack:** connector words ≈ 20–30% of frame width (SOON 21%, "IT WAS FINDING" 50% at ~40 px cap height); keyword lines ≈ 50–70% width (SEARS 50% at ~95 px caps, WAS SELLING 67%); the CTA URL ≈ 95% width. Each stack is centred at y ≈ 40–55%, and lines are centre-justified with ragged widths, so the block reads as a poster.
  - Effects: a soft dark drop shadow on all type (offset ~3–4 px down, blur ~6 px, 50% black), a **white outer glow** on white words on dark cards (~10–14 px, 35%), and **outline-only neon words** (2 px stroke, glow 8–12 px) for the emphasis phrase. On the white card, a hollow "EMPIRE" (thin black stroke, ≈ 70% width, with vertical ghost/echo doubling on the stems) sits behind a solid black "A RETAIL".
  - Diegetic type: "AND WAIT FOR IT TO ARRIVE" is painted onto the road in perspective (light grey, ~70% opacity, bottom 20% of the frame, foreshortened to the ground plane). Price tags are kraft-paper luggage tags on a string with heavy black "2$", "0.5$", "0.2$", "1599$". A comic speech-bubble card ("Amazon delivery!") sits in the peephole scene.
- **Layout & composition:** the title-card stacks are dead-centre. On character cards the giant character occupies one vertical half (the B/W Sears is cropped at the chin/shoulder and fills ~50% of the width) and the type stack sits on the other half, starting at x ≈ 30%. The character swaps sides via a whip pan (8.7 s). Product cards place the object and starburst centred at y ≈ 45%, with the tag hanging off the upper right.
- **Assets:** AI-video characters (courier with an Amazon cap/box, Richard Sears in a 3-piece suit), a fisheye peephole POV with a black circular mask, a floating rock island with miniature houses, a catalog page ("amazon.com" mock catalog listing a book, a multitool, fish oil and "The Beatles – Abbey Road"), a red hand-drawn tick, a mailbox, a vintage blue delivery truck, a dark crate, cut-out products (watch, polka-dot underwear, hammer, house), starburst badges and price tags.
- **Finishing / FX:** scanlines, heavy vignette (corners at ~20% of the centre luma), DOF blur on backgrounds, motion blur on whips and throws, a white bloom on white type, 2-frame colour-invert flashes, a light-leak burst (orange/red) at 7.31 s, a white light-sweep at 26.19 s, and blob/turbulent-displacement text dissolves (words melt into blobs and red particles on exit, CTA section).

## 4. Motion & timing analysis
- **Pacing:** ~0.6 real cuts/s. Story scenes run 1.2–2.3 s and title cards 1.45–2.7 s. The **list montage drops to 0.6 s per item** (12–14), then the pace slows again for the moral (2.25 + 2.7 s) and the CTA (6.4 s). The motion curve shows peaks only at transitions. Inside shots the flow is low (0.03–0.7), so the energy comes from cuts and type builds, not camera moves.
- **Easing signatures (per_frame.csv):**
  - Opening pull-back: an exponential ease-out (zoom/frame decays ×~0.93 per frame from −2.44 to −0.75 over 16 frames), similar to `bezier(0.1, 0.6, 0.3, 1)` over ~1.6 s.
  - Shot 3 reveal: an ease-in over 11 frames (speed roughly doubles every 3 frames) → a 1-frame peak → an 8-frame exponential ease-out. This is the classic "smash" curve (`bezier(0.7, 0, 0.2, 1)`) with the hit at ~60% of the duration.
  - The whip at 8.4–9.1 s is ~6 frames in and ~6 frames out, with blur on the 2 middle frames and no overshoot.
  - The catalog throw (13.0–13.8 s) is the only **overshoot** in the piece: zoom and rotation change sign twice before settling in ~10 frames (≈ `spring({damping: 10, stiffness: 120, mass: 0.8})`).
  - The white flash-up (24.69–25.0 s) has luma steps 25 → 34 → 53 → 96 → 129 → 147 → 158: an S-curve over 6 frames.
- **Entrance / exit vocabulary:**
  - **Blur-blob word assemble** (each word appears from turbulent, smeared fragments to sharp in ~2 frames). It is used for every caption word.
  - **Fixed-cadence word stacking:** one word or short group every **0.375 s (6 frames at 16 fps ≈ 9 frames at 24 fps)**, measured in shots 11 and 19. This is a timed cadence, not strict word-sync, so on-screen words drift −0.25…+0.11 s against the VO.
  - Outlined emphasis letter enters from the left, larger, and settles (≈ 4 frames).
  - Pop-in prop on a starburst (2-frame pull-back).
  - Counter-roll price tag (~6 frames).
  - Write-on tick (~12 frames).
  - Truck-pass reveal of ground text (~8 frames).
  - Object drop-in (crate, ~4 frames).
  - Melt/particle dissolve exit (~4–6 frames) in the CTA.
  - Text pushed off by the next element (IT BECAME drops down as A RETAIL drops in).
- **Camera:** short decisive moves only (one reveal tilt, one whip, one snap scale, slow pushes in the peephole and CTA shots). No shake.
- **Secondary motion:** AI-video character idle motion (talking, blinking, breathing), floating debris around the islands, the tag swinging on the products, cloud drift in the CTA.
- **Sync:**
  - **Cuts lead the VO phrase by a median of ~0.08 s** (≈ 1–2 frames): 5.00 vs "In" 5.08; 11.56 vs "So" 11.64; 14.81 vs "Customers" 14.86; 16.50 vs "send" 16.60; 18.81 vs "Soon" 18.96; 21.31 vs "Watches" 21.44; 21.94 vs "clothes" 22.14; 22.56 vs "tools" 22.70; 24.31 vs "It" 24.34; 26.19 vs "And" 26.24.

  - The opening cut at 1.875 s falls in the VO gap (the word "door" ends 1.82 and the next phrase starts 2.08).
  - Physical transitions start before the phrase they introduce: the catalog throw starts 0.48 s before "a mail" so that it lands on the word; the truck starts 0.12 s before "and wait".
  - The music (86 BPM, period ~0.70 s) is a bed. Only 18.81 and 28.56 sit exactly on detected beats, so the edit is VO-driven, not beat-driven.

## 5. Transitions catalogue
1. **Roll-whip into cut** (1.875 s): the camera roll accelerates over the last 6 frames (−1.0 → −2.4 °/frame) and pan_x rises to +0.85. Cut at maximum speed into a new POV. Rebuild: rotate the outgoing scene 0 → −8° with `Easing.in(Easing.cubic)` over 6 frames plus a directional blur, then hard cut.
2. **Iris close + flash cut** (4.8–5.0 s): the peephole mask shrinks/darkens over 4 frames, then a hard cut to a bright sky (luma ×3.8). The dark-to-bright jump works as the hit.
3. **Light-leak burn** (7.31–7.44 s, 2 frames): frame 1 is the outgoing shot overlaid with an orange-red burn and a radial blur; frame 2 is an overexposed, cyan-tinted negative of the incoming shot. Then the new scene. Rebuild: `mix-blend-mode: screen` light-leak PNG at 100% for 1 frame, then `filter: invert(1) brightness(1.6)` on the incoming scene for 1 frame.
4. **Whip pan re-compose** (8.5–8.8 s): the same character is re-framed on the opposite side. ~12 frames total with 2 blurred middle frames.
5. **Object wipe / throw-in** (13.0–13.4 s): a paper prop tumbles across the frame and covers the cut while the background colour changes (charcoal → red over ~3 frames behind it), then settles with overshoot.
6. **Match-move across the cut** (16.25–16.75 s): the catalog shrinks away at the end of shot 9 and is seen flying into the mailbox at the start of shot 10.
7. **Vehicle wipe revealing text** (17.44 s): a motion-blurred truck crosses the full width in ~8 frames, and the road text exists only after it passes.
8. **Invert-spin flash** (21.94 and 22.56 s, 2 frames each): frame 1 shows the colour-negative of the incoming product rotated ~9° and slightly scaled up (flow 10–15); frame 2 is a negative, partly resolved; then a normal frame. It reads as a camera-flash "snap" per list item. Rebuild: `filter: invert(1)` + `rotate(-9deg) scale(1.08)` on frame 0, `invert(1) rotate(-3deg)` on frame 1, normal from frame 2.
9. **White flash-up / polarity flip** (24.69–25.0 s): the dark card ramps to white over 6 frames (S-curve) while the type swaps polarity (white on black → black on white).
10. **Light sweep** (26.19–26.31 s): a soft vertical white bar crosses L→R in 2 frames over the white card and leaves a dark card behind.
11. **Prop drop as a type wipe** (26.81 s): the falling crate pushes the previous line aside as the next line builds.

## 7. What makes it high-end (the transferable lessons)
1. **Alternate "scene" and "slogan":** a fully rendered story image, then a black type card, then a story image. The type cards act as chapter breaks and let the viewer absorb the line.
2. **Colour encodes time:** the past is B/W on red and the present is full colour. The viewer always knows which era they are in, with no captions needed.
3. **Two-colour type hierarchy in one stack:** small white connectors and huge red keywords, centre-justified. The size ratio is ~1:2.5 between connector and keyword lines.
4. **One special style per moral:** outline-only neon (green for "solution", blue for "new way") is used only for the thesis phrases. It must stay rare to keep its weight.
5. **List montage at 0.6 s per item**, with a 2-frame invert-spin flash between items and one starburst colour per item. This works for any "they sold X, Y, Z" beat.
6. **Physical transitions that carry story objects:** the catalog is thrown in, flies into the mailbox and is followed by a delivery truck. The object of the sentence carries the cut.
7. **Depth layering with type:** names and keywords sit behind the character's head or behind a prop, so the type feels part of the scene rather than a subtitle.
8. **Cut ~0.08 s before the phrase** and let the type build at a steady 0.375 s per word cadence.
9. **Uniform finishing** (scanlines + heavy vignette + soft glow) ties AI footage of mixed quality together.

## 8. Remotion rebuild recipe
- **Composition:** `1080×1920`, `30 fps` (the reference is 16 fps; render smoother), duration = VO + ~0.5 s ≈ 1130 frames.
- **Layer stack, title card:** `<TexturedBg color="#181818" texture="fabric" vignette={0.8}>` → `<StackedCaption lines=[...] cadence={11}>` (11 frames ≈ 0.375 s at 30 fps) → `<Scanlines pitch={4} opacity={0.07}>` → `<Vignette>`.
- **Layer stack, story scene:** `<OffthreadVideo src={aiClip} style={{filter: era==='past' ? 'grayscale(1) contrast(1.15)' : 'none'}}>` on a `<TexturedBg color="#6c0e10">` when the character is cut out → `<DepthText behind>` (type placed in a layer between the bg plate and a character matte, via a pre-keyed alpha clip or a second masked copy of the clip on top) → `<Scanlines>` → `<Vignette>`.
- **Components:**
  - `StackedCaption`: props `lines: {words: string[], color: 'white'|'red'|'outlineGreen'|'outlineBlue', scale: number}[]`, `cadence` (frames per word), `font: 'Bricolage Grotesque' 800, uppercase, letterSpacing: -0.01em, lineHeight: 0.85`. Per word: `opacity 0→1` and `filter: blur(12px)→0` over 3 frames, plus an SVG `feTurbulence` + `feDisplacementMap` scale of 30 → 0 for the blob-assemble look. White words get `text-shadow: 0 0 12px rgba(255,255,255,.35), 0 4px 6px rgba(0,0,0,.5)`, red words (`#d0161b`) get `0 4px 6px rgba(0,0,0,.55)`. Outline words use `-webkit-text-stroke: 3px #22d33a; color: transparent; filter: drop-shadow(0 0 10px #22d33a)`; the first letter enters from `translateX(-120px) scale(1.4)` with `spring({damping: 14, stiffness: 160})`.
  - `RevealTilt`: `translateY` from 0 to −40% of the height with `Easing.bezier(0.7, 0, 0.2, 1)` over 20 frames (30 fps), with a scale pull-back of 1.15 → 1 on the same curve.
  - `WhipRecompose`: `translateX` 0 → −110% (outgoing) and 110% → 0 (incoming) over 12 frames with `Easing.inOut(Easing.cubic)` and a horizontal blur of 0 → 24 px → 0.
  - `ThrowIn`: the prop enters from `x: 120%, rotate: 25deg`. `spring({damping: 10, stiffness: 120, mass: 0.8})` drives x and rotation (overshoot is intended); the bg colour interpolates `#181818 → #6c0e10` over 4 frames, starting when the prop crosses the centre.
  - `InvertSpinFlash`: a 2-frame overlay on the incoming scene: frame 0 `invert(1) rotate(-9deg) scale(1.08)`, frame 1 `invert(1) rotate(-3deg)`.
  - `StarburstCard`: an SVG 12-point star (outer/inner radius 1 : 0.55) with a fill colour, a 3 px white stroke and `drop-shadow(0 0 20px color)`; the cut-out PNG sits on top; a `PriceTag` (kraft `#d9b77a`, string, heavy black text) rotates ±6° with a sine swing (period 30 frames); entrance `scale 1.1 → 1` over 3 frames.
  - `CounterTag`: digits roll from a random value to the target over 8 frames (`Math.round(interpolate(...))`), with the final digits locking from left to right.
  - `GroundText`: a CSS 3D `perspective(800px) rotateX(70deg)`, `color: rgba(220,220,220,.7)`, `mix-blend-mode: overlay`, revealed by a mask that follows the truck's x position.
  - `VehicleWipe`: a truck PNG `translateX(-120% → 120%)` over 8 frames with a 20 px horizontal blur.
  - `PolarityFlip`: the background luma S-curve dark → white over 6 frames (`Easing.inOut(Easing.quad)`); swap the text colour at frame 3.
  - `LightSweep`: a vertical white gradient bar (`linear-gradient(90deg, transparent, #fff 40%, #fff 60%, transparent)`, width 60%) crossing L→R in 3 frames.
  - `MeltOut`: `feTurbulence` baseFrequency 0.02, `feDisplacementMap` scale 0 → 80 plus opacity 1 → 0 over 5 frames; optional red particle burst (20–40 small circles flying outward ~60 px).
  - `Scanlines`: a `repeating-linear-gradient(0deg, rgba(0,0,0,.08) 0 1px, transparent 1px 4px)` overlay.
  - `PeepholeMask`: a circular `clip-path` (radius 47% of the width) on a black bg plus a barrel-distortion feel (scale 1.1 with a radial blur at the edges); the iris close is a radius tween 47% → 40% over 4 frames.
