**Source creator handle:** earn.edits · **Format:** 720×1280, 9:16, 29.97 fps container with graphics animated at ~15 fps (motion updates every 2 frames), 46.6 s · **Shots:** 17 real (21 detected cuts; 15.48 and 15.82 are punch-in reframes inside the newspaper scene; 17.88, 18.42 and 18.95 are vertical blur-smear swaps inside the repurposed Gucci film clip) · **Avg shot:** ~2.7 s (median ~2.0 s) · **Music BPM:** ~96 (bed under VO) · **VO:** yes, English, measured storyteller read; no VO over the CTA

**One-line essence:** a fashion-business turnaround told as a run of "brand-coloured stages" (Gucci stripe, sky with flying luxury goods, Tom Ford bobblehead on a knit backdrop, tabloid clipping, school locker report card, macOS dialog, clipboard), cut together with repurposed official Gucci archive footage. Small sentence-case captions stay constant while each stage gets a giant, word-synced keyword block that becomes the poster of that sentence.

## 1. Concept & script structure
- **Genre:** brand turnaround case study ("brand at rock bottom → outsider arrives → doubters → bold cuts → result in numbers → lesson → antithesis restatement → CTA").
- **Beats:**
  - 0.0–5.4 **Hook / setting:** in the early 1990s Gucci sold cheap canvas bags in airport duty-free shops. A date plus a luxury brand doing something un-luxury.
  - 5.6–9.3 **Low point:** the brand lost its identity and the fashion world wrote it off.
  - 9.6–16.8 **Hero arrives + doubt:** a 33-year-old American named Tom Ford walks in, has never led a fashion house, and most expect him to fail.
  - 17.1–25.6 **The turn:** he cut cheap products, rebuilt from the ground up, and staged a landmark fashion show (8.7 s of real Gucci archive footage).
  - 25.6–29.5 **Payoff number:** sales grew from $230 million to $3 billion.
  - 30.2–34.7 **Lesson:** exclusivity isn't about price; it's about what you're willing to say no to.
  - 35.0–41.7 **Antithesis close:** Gucci almost died saying yes to everything; it came back because Tom Ford said no to almost all of it.
  - 41.9–46.6 **CTA:** a phone mock-up showing earnedits.com, with the title "EDITORS PROJECT FILE ON EARNEDITS.COM" (no VO).
- **Hook mechanics (first 1.5 s):** frame 0 is a white cloud bank. By 0.5 s the camera pushes down through the clouds (zoom +2.6 analysis/step), revealing a top-down aerial city. Giant white IN THE / EARLY (~83% of frame width) sits behind a **Gucci-monogram blimp** stamped "1990s" that crosses the type. It's a cloud fly-through, a giant date and a branded object within 0.7 s.
- **Script → visual rule:** each sentence is one stage. Its key phrase is set as a giant stacked keyword block that builds word by word with the VO. The noun becomes a literal Gucci-branded prop (blimp, Rimowa-style suitcase, GG handbag, glitter tie, report card), and the full VO runs in small sentence-case captions.

## 4. Motion & timing analysis
- **Pacing:** 0.37 real cuts/s. Collage stages run 1.8–4.8 s; the footage block (17.15–25.89) runs 0.5–2.3 s per shot plus three internal smear swaps, which makes it the fastest section. The lesson section slows to 4.8 / 3.1 / 3.7 s for reading.
- **Stepped animation:** collage motion updates every 2 frames (≈15 fps). Footage plays at native rate. That gives a slightly tactile feel, smoother than v13's 12 fps.
- **Easing signatures (from per_frame.csv):**
  - **Scene settle:** first-step zoom −3.2 to −4.5 decaying with ratio ~0.7–0.8 per 15 fps step; 90% settled in ~0.4–0.6 s and no overshoot. Then either a **constant linear drift** (tubes: exactly −1.2/step for 3.3 s, i.e. a linear scale-down) or a slow decaying drift. Rebuild: `Easing.out(Easing.exp)` over 18–24 frames from scale 1.12, then a linear `scale -= 0.0012/frame`.
  - **Directional settles** (Tom Ford tilt, newspaper slide, laptop slide): the peak velocity occurs on the first 1–2 steps after the cut, then decays geometrically at ~0.75 per step and is at rest in ~0.7–1.0 s. That is `Easing.bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` over ~24–30 frames.
  - **Newspaper swing rotate:** a symmetric velocity bell (1.6 → 6.4 → 0.6 over 1.0 s), i.e. `Easing.inOut(Easing.sine)` over 30 frames, ~15°.
  - **Paper slap:** scale settle plus a flutter rotation spike ~4 steps after landing, gone in 0.45 s. Use `spring({damping: 9, stiffness: 160, mass: 0.6})` on rotation (one visible swing).
  - **Cloud push-through:** ease-in-out bell (velocity peak at 0.4–0.55 s), ~24 frames.
  - **CTA phone:** a 1.6 s expo-out push with rotation −20° → 0°.
- **Entrance / exit vocabulary:**
  - Ghost-word build (opacity 0.3 → 1, ~4 steps, 2-step stagger).
  - Line slide-in from the side with a blur trail (~4 frames).
  - Prop rise from the bottom + pendulum sway.
  - Sticker slap (rotated, ~3-step pop).
  - Split-flap digit flip (~7 frames).
  - Bar slide-in (YEARS OLD).
  - Tab slide-out from behind the object (TO $3 BILLION).
  - Script write-on (~0.9 s for 8 letters).
  - Highlighter sweep L→R synced to words.
  - Typewriter in a UI dialog (~15 chars/s).
  - Stitched badge slam.
  - Grey → black word darken.
  - Exits are hard cuts or block replacement; nothing animates out.
- **Camera:** every collage stage has a settle plus drift (net zoom magnitudes 17–49). The largest true camera moves are the footage tilt-up (−96 net pan_y) and the newspaper swing. Reframe "jump cuts" inside a scene (15.48/15.82) emulate a handheld documentary zoom.
- **Secondary motion:** the suitcase sway, floating sky props, the **puppet jaw flap** on Tom Ford (the jaw opens ~10 px on stressed syllables), glitter shimmer on the tie, the phone screen recording scrolling.
- **Sync (measured):**
  - Cuts land on the first word of the new sentence: +0.03 ("The"), +0.03 ("and"), −0.01 ("Then"), −0.05 ("He had"), +0.01 ("Instead"), 0.00 ("fashion"), +0.05 ("The lesson"), +0.03 ("Gucci"), +0.02 ("It"). The median is ~+0.02 s, so the cut lands on the syllable.
  - Beat offsets are scattered (−0.33…+0.32 s at 96 BPM), so music is a bed again.
  - Keyword blocks: in shot 2 the whole block builds in ~0.5 s **ahead** of the VO (it leads "bags" by ~1 s, a poster-first approach). From shot 3 onward words appear 0–0.3 s **after** their spoken word.
  - Counter flip and script write-on start within 0.0–0.1 s of their spoken word ("33" 10.16 → flip 10.04–10.28; "Tom" 11.68 → write-on 11.68).

## 5. Transitions catalogue
1. **Hard cut on the sentence start** (most cuts); the new stage is always mid-settle on frame 1.
2. **Vertical blur-smear object swap** (17.82, 18.35, 18.89; from the source film): 3 frames of heavy vertical motion blur replace one bag with another on the same pedestal. Rebuild: translateY the old prop −100% and the new prop from +100% over 3 frames with an SVG `feGaussianBlur stdDeviation="0 40"`.
3. **Punch-in reframe jump** (15.48, 15.82): two hard cuts to tighter, differently angled crops of the same newspaper. It gives documentary "zoom-to-detail" energy with no animation cost.
4. **Cloud push-through reveal** (0.0–0.8): the opening transition from white into the scene. Rebuild: a cloud PNG layer scaled 1→2.5 with opacity 1→0 over 24 frames, `Easing.inOut(Easing.cubic)`, over a background scaling 1.15→1.
5. **Block replacement on a static card** (orange scene): no transition; the next 2-line block simply replaces the previous one as new words start building.
6. **Repurposed footage with source subtitles left in** (19.2–25.9): the official clip's own lower-third subtitles stay visible, layered under the creator's caption pill. This gives authenticity, but two subtitle lines compete. Crop or blur them in a rebuild if possible.

## 7. What makes it high-end (the transferable lessons)
1. **Build the backgrounds from the brand.** The Gucci web stripe, GG monogram props, green lockers and the red/green accent palette make a story about a brand look like that brand without its ads.
2. **One giant keyword block per sentence, word-synced, over a small neutral caption.** The poster type carries emotion and the caption carries the full text. Keep the caption style constant.
3. **Turn a person into a character:** bobblehead scale (+15%), a glitter tie and a flapping cut-out jaw make a static press photo "talk" for almost no effort.
4. **Show numbers as objects:** a split-flap counter for age, a school report card ("FINAL SALES" + A+) for revenue, and a tab sliding out for the end figure.
5. **Real footage at the turning point.** The pivot ("he cut… rebuilt…") switches from collage to cinematic archive footage, which signals that this is the real story.
6. **The lesson typed into a UI dialog** reads like a note to self, and the typing makes the viewer read along at speaking pace.
7. **Antithesis close on flat colour:** "said yes to everything" on a yellow warning badge vs. "said no to almost all of it" on a calm orange card, with the final clause flipped to white.
8. **Everything settles, nothing is still:** an expo-out settle on every cut, followed by linear drift.

## 8. Remotion rebuild recipe
- **Composition:** `1080×1920`, `30 fps`, duration = VO + ~4.5 s CTA. Quantise collage layers to 15 fps: `const f15 = Math.floor(frame / 2) * 2`.
- **Typical scene stack (bottom → top):** `<SurfaceBg src texture>` → `<Prop>` cut-outs with contact shadows → `<KeywordBlock words timings variant>` → `<SettleCamera from={1.12} drift={-0.0012}>` wrapping the above → `<CaptionPill>` (fixed, y 68%) → `<Grain opacity={0.07}>` + `<Vignette strength={0.25}>`.
- **Components & timings:**
  - `KeywordBlock`: Inter/Archivo Black, `letterSpacing: '-0.03em'`, `lineHeight: 0.85`, uppercase, width 70–85% of frame. Each word: opacity 0.3→1 and `blur(3px)`→0 over 8 frames (`Easing.out(Easing.quad)`) at its VO start (or a 4-frame stagger for poster-first blocks). Variants: `shadowHard` (`text-shadow: 4px 4px 0 #000`), `twoTone` (line colours), `multiply` (`mix-blend-mode: multiply` on a coloured card), `extrude` (stacked shadows).
  - `LineSlideIn({from: 'right'})`: translateX 40%→0 over 5 frames, `Easing.out(Easing.cubic)`, with a horizontal blur of 12px→0.
  - `PropRiseSway`: translateY 110%→0 over 30 frames (`Easing.bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1)`), then `rotate = 5 * Math.sin((frame - start) / fps * 2 * Math.PI / 1.2)` degrees around the top-centre origin.
  - `StickerSlap`: scale 1.6→1 over 6 frames with `spring({damping: 10, stiffness: 220, mass: 0.5})`, rotate −30°, white rounded rect with `box-shadow: 0 6px 12px rgba(0,0,0,.25)`.
  - `SplitFlapCounter({from: 30, to: 33, at})`: each digit flip = the top half rotating X 0→−90° (4 frames) then the bottom half 90→0° (3 frames). Stack the intermediate digits.
  - `PuppetJaw({img, jawMask, amplitudes})`: split the face PNG at the mouth line and translateY the jaw 0→10 px on VO word onsets (open 3 frames, close 3 frames). Drive it from word timestamps or an audio-amplitude envelope (`visualizeAudio`).
  - `ScriptWriteOn`: SVG single-stroke script path, `strokeDashoffset` over the word duration, gold `#c9a227`, 3px stroke.
  - `HighlighterSweep`: a rounded rect behind the text line, `scaleX` 0→1 from the left over the spoken duration of the highlighted words, `#f2a114` at 85% opacity, `mix-blend-mode: multiply`.
  - `SlideSettle({dir, rotate})`: initial offset 25% plus rotation 6°, decaying with `Easing.bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1)` over 24–30 frames.
  - `SwingRotate`: rotate 0→15° with `Easing.inOut(Easing.sine)` over 30 frames.
  - `PaperSlap`: scale 1.25→1 over 8 frames, plus a rotation spring (damping 9) kicked at landing.
  - `TabSlideOut`: a blue tab clipped by the paper edge, translateX from behind the paper over 8 frames ease-out.
  - `MacDialogTypewriter`: SF Pro Semibold, ~15 chars/s (2 frames/char) with a caret; the window height animates when wrapping (4 frames).
  - `StitchedBadge`: yellow `#f2c40f` rect with an inner `outline: 3px dashed #111; outline-offset: -8px`; slam = scale 1.3→1 over 6 frames, `Easing.out(Easing.back(1.2))`.
