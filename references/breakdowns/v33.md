## 1. Concept & script structure
- **Topic / genre:** a SaaS product promo / "how it works" teaser for an AI website builder that resolves to the Framer logo. It is a template/project-file showcase from earn.edits, since the end card sells the project file.
- **Narrative beats (no VO; the on-screen text carries the story):**
  - 0.00–1.80 **Hook / problem:** the line "Your website shouldn't take weeks" types itself word by word in small white type on black.
  - 1.85–3.38 **Action 1:** a "+ New Project" button. The cursor flies in and the camera snaps into a macro close-up of the button while the cursor clicks it.
  - 3.38–5.46 **Action 2 (prompt):** a glowing chat input box ("New Chat", "Opus 4.8" model picker) types "Create a AI website landing page". The camera cuts to a macro of the send-arrow button, which lights blue (the "send").
  - 5.46–12.55 **Payoff (generation):** a browser frame ("Desktop · 1200") pulls back from a corner close-up. The landing page "NeuraFlow: Supercharge Your Business with AI-Powered Intelligence" builds itself through blue pixel-block glitches. A second prompt ("Add features page") pops over it, the page scrolls down and a bento grid of feature cards assembles.
  - 12.55–16.02 **Feature list:** the button word swaps Design → Animate → Publish, a hand cursor clicks Publish and a ripple ring zooms through into "Real time / Collaboration" inside rotating broken neon rings.
  - 16.02–22.02 **Brand reveal:** a cluster of glowing app-icon tiles floats, then a dot-matrix dissolve reveals a blue neon Framer "F". The glow drains to a flat white mark and the "Framer" wordmark pops in with a blue bloom that cools to white.
  - 22.02–24.27 **CTA:** a hard cut to a white card reading "Get the project file at / earnedits.com".
- **Hook mechanics (first 1.5 s):** frames 0–5 flash a blue-grey **dot-matrix (LED pixel) field** on the left and right thirds of the frame (sat jumps to 124 at f4). By 0.25 s "Your" sits slightly left of centre and the words append at ~5-frame intervals with a **solid blue block caret** (≈ #2f6bff, the height of the cap line, ~1.5× the width of a word) leading the text. The problem statement is complete at 1.0 s. At ~1.5 s the last word is highlighted blue (selection) and the line clears to black at 1.75 s. The hook is quiet, small type on black that makes the viewer lean in, not a loud slam.
- **Script → visual rule:** one verb = one object in macro close-up (click = a button, type = an input box, send = an arrow button, generate = a website, publish = a button press). A feature noun gets one word inside one glowing container. There is no on-screen text the viewer has to read for more than ~4 words at once.

## 2. Visual language
- **Art direction:** a dark-mode UI mockup in "Apple keynote / Linear-style" neon minimalism. Every element is a dark glass panel (#0d0f14–#16181d fill, ~6–10% white top highlight) whose only light source is a **blue rim light / edge glow** along one or two edges. The rest of the frame falls off to pure black through heavy vignetting. There are no textures and no photos.
- **Backgrounds:** `#000000` throughout (mean luma 2–23 for every dark shot). The only "texture" is the **LED dot-matrix field** (round dots ~1.4% of frame width, on a ~2% pitch, varying brightness grey-blue `#57565a`/`#4a494c`). It is used twice as a transition material (0.0–0.25 s and 17.68–18.0 s). A faint code-text layer is visible at the right edge of the send-button macro (4.75–5.0 s). The end card is flat `#fefefe`.
- **Colour:** monochrome black + one electric-blue accent family:
  - glow core / rim: `#3e96c1` → `#31a3db` (cyan-ish highlight) and `#056ab2`/`#1b497c` (falloff).
  - navy fill tones: `#0a111d`, `#182739`, `#244769`.
  - CTA buttons ("Design/Animate/Publish"): saturated fill ~`#0a6cff`→`#1e90ff` with cyan text ~`#7fe3ff` (sat values hit 236–248, the most saturated moment in the video).
  - wordmark bloom: `#0a5cff` glow cooling to pure white.
  - end card: `#fefefe` bg, near-black `#111111` text, link blue ~`#1a8cf0`.
  - Rule: blue is light, never surface. It appears as glow, rim, caret, highlight or the pressed state. White is reserved for content text.
- **Typography:**
  - UI and headline type: a clean neo-grotesk, most likely **Archivo** (Regular/Medium; visible in "Opus 4.8", "Create a AI w…", "Real time" and the end card's "Get the project file at" with its spurred G and straight-legged j). The intro line looks like Helvetica/Arial Regular. All sentence case, no caps-lock anywhere.
  - Sizes (relative to frame width 1276): intro line ≈ 40% width total at ~36 px (cap height ≈ 3.5% of height), deliberately small. "New Project" button text ≈ 27% width in close-up. "Design/Animate/Publish" ≈ 18–22% width, ~70 px. "Real time" ≈ 27% width (350 px), white Regular. "Collaboration" ≈ 30% width, blue `#2f7dff` Medium, one line below. Framer lockup ≈ 22% width. End card: line 1 ≈ 31% width Regular black, "earnedits.com" ≈ 24% width **Bold** blue.
  - Effects: no strokes or shadows on type. Emphasis is done with **colour + glow** (cyan text on a glowing blue pill, a blue bloom on the wordmark that decays) or with a **blue caret/selection block**.
  - Model/UI labels in mid-grey `#75767b` ("Opus 4.8 ⌄", "New Chat ⌄", "Desktop 1200") for realism.
- **Layout & composition:** everything is centred on the frame's centre point, with no thirds. Hero objects are either small and floating in a vast black field (the button at 2.0 s ≈ 20% width, the logo ≈ 8% width) or **macro close-ups cropped off-frame** (the send-button corner fills the bottom-right quadrant, and the browser's top-left corner sits at 40% from the left). The alternation between tiny and huge is the main compositional rhythm.
- **Assets:** a mock AI-chat input component, a fake SaaS landing page "NeuraFlow" (nav, hero headline, dashboard with KPI cards +75% / +80% / +67% and sparklines), bento feature cards ("AI Workflow Automation", "Natural Language Processing (NLP) Engine", "Predictive Analytics", "Smart Data Visualization", "AI-Powered Decision Making"), glassy app-icon tiles with white glyphs, an arrow cursor with a blue gradient (a 3D-ish Framer-style pointer) and a white pointing-hand cursor, and the Framer logo.
- **Finishing / FX:** strong outer glow/bloom on blue edges (glow radius ≈ 4–8% of frame width), heavy radial vignette, soft depth-of-field blur on background layers (the website behind the 2nd prompt is blurred ~6–10 px and dimmed ~50%), a **pixel-block glitch-build** (solid `#0a4cff` squares that cover regions of the page and then reveal them), and light chromatic bloom. No grain, so the look stays clinically clean.

## 4. Motion & timing analysis
- **Pacing:** ~0.5 scene changes/s. Shots are short in the action section (0.9–1.6 s for click, type, send), long in the payoff (website 2.5 + 1.8 + 2.45 s) and longest on the brand (4.3 s). The motion-energy curve shows three dense clusters: 2.2–3.4 s (button clicks), 9.6–10.8 s (scroll-through and grid build) and 12.9–14.2 s (button swaps and ripple). Between them are nearly flat holds (flow < 0.2) at 4.0, 8.5–9.3, 11.0–12.0 and 18.5–22.0 s. This alternation of calm and burst is the edit's rhythm.
- **Easing signatures (per_frame.csv):**
  - Website pull-back (shot 5): zoom ramps for 8 frames to its peak (f155, −1.24), then decays over 15 frames with a long tail and no sign flip. This is an **asymmetric ease-in-out, ~1:2 in:out**, similar to `bezier(0.6, 0, 0.2, 1)` over ~23 frames.
  - Prompt-box rise (shot 6): 7 frames to the peak, 7 frames to settle, symmetric `bezier(0.45, 0, 0.2, 1)` over ~14 frames. The data shows no overshoot.
  - Scroll-through (shot 7): pure **exponential ease-in** (speed doubles every ~3 frames), cut at maximum speed, so the transition hides inside the blur.
  - Icon-cluster burst (shot 11): 4 frames up, 9 frames down, a fast ease-out.
  - Glow decays (logo, wordmark): long linear-ish fades of 33–40 frames. The ending is deliberately slow after a busy middle.
- **Entrance / exit vocabulary:**
  - typewriter by word with a block caret (5–6 frames/word);
  - typewriter by character in UI boxes (0.85–1.6 chars/frame);
  - snap zoom (1-frame scale jump from wide to macro);
  - button press (2-frame squash);
  - blue-fill sweep L→R on a selected dropdown (~6 frames);
  - pixel-block glitch build (blue squares cover then reveal, ~20–25 frames per region);
  - rise + focus-pull for overlay panels (~14 frames);
  - word swap inside a fixed container (hard swap + 1-frame pop, every 12 frames);
  - glow-bloom pop for the wordmark (instant on, ~33-frame cool-down);
  - glow drain from blue neon to flat white logo (~40 frames).
- **Camera:** a virtual camera only. Macro close-ups → pull-backs (shot 5), snap zooms (shot 2), continuous push-in through the rings (shot 10) and vertical scrolls (shots 7–8). Net rotation is small (±8–11°, measured on rotating ring and tile elements, not a camera roll). There are no whip pans and no shake.
- **Secondary motion:** cursor travel with eased arcs, rotating ring segments, floating tiles drifting at different depths, the glow "breathing" on rim lights, and caret blink.
- **Sync (music-only, 143.6 BPM, beat = 0.418 s; eighth = 0.209 s):**
  - Hard cuts sit on or just before beats: 5.464 vs beat 5.53 (−0.07 s), 12.554 vs 12.52 (+0.03), 22.022 vs 21.97 (+0.05), and the 2.29 snap zoom vs beat 2.23 (+0.06). The 3.378 click-cut leads beat 3.46 by 0.08 s, and the ripple into "Real time" at 14.14 lands on beat 14.16 (−0.02).
  - Other changes land on **off-beat eighths**: 4.546 (beat 4.27 + 0.21 + 0.06), 16.016 (15.81 + 0.21), 17.68 (17.44 + 0.21 + 0.03), 19.64 (19.48 + 0.21 − 0.05). Rule: the main scene changes sit on the beat, and secondary reveals sit on the "and".
  - The Design/Animate/Publish swaps run on a **12-frame (0.50 s) grid** rather than the beat, so they drift against the music. The rebuild should snap them to the beat (10 frames at 24 fps) or keep a fixed 12-frame cadence.
  - Strong onsets cluster at 12.10/12.52/12.91/13.33 (the button-swap section) and 19.06–21.13 (the logo/wordmark), which suggests SFX hits on the swaps and the wordmark.

## 5. Transitions catalogue
1. **Dot-matrix flash / dissolve** (0.0–0.25 s; 17.65–17.8 s). An LED-style grid of round dots appears with random brightness. The outgoing image is sampled into it for ~3 frames, and the dots fade over ~6 frames to reveal the next element. Rebuild: a `<canvas>` or SVG grid of circles (pitch ≈ 2% width) whose opacity = noise(x, y, t) × luminance of the outgoing frame; animate the opacity to 0 with a random per-dot delay of 0–4 frames.
2. **Snap zoom** (2.29 s): from a small object in the void to a macro of the same object in 1 frame, with no tween. Rebuild: change `scale` from 1 to 3.5 between two frames, with the anchor on the object's centre.
3. **Click-cut** (3.378 s): the cursor's click is the cut point, so the next shot shows the result of the click. Place the cut on the frame where the button squash bottoms out.
4. **Macro pull-back reveal** (5.46 s): open on an extreme close-up of a frame corner (rim light only), then zoom out ~3× over ~23 frames with an asymmetric ease. Content builds during the pull-back.
5. **Scroll-through** (9.4–10.1 s): the page scrolls with exponential acceleration until motion blur and a luma dip hide the swap to the next section. Rebuild: `translateY` with `Easing.in(Easing.exp)` over 12 frames plus a vertical blur that scales with speed (0 → 20 px) and a brightness dip to 40% on the last 2 frames.
6. **Click-ripple zoom-through** (13.9–14.14 s): on the click, 2–3 concentric rounded-rect outlines expand from the button (scale 1 → 1.6, opacity 1 → 0), the frame flashes brighter (+35% luma), and the next scene (the ring) is already zooming in. 6 frames total.
7. **Hard cuts on the beat** for all other macro changes. There are no crossfades anywhere except the glow decays.
8. **Light-to-paper hard cut** (22.02 s): black → pure white in 1 frame for the CTA card. This is the only bright frame of the video, so the CTA gets maximum contrast.

## 7. What makes it high-end (the transferable lessons)
1. **Light, not colour:** keep the surfaces black and dark-glass, and spend the accent colour only on emitted light (rims, glows, carets, pressed states). One hue family, ~5 tints.
2. **One object, one verb per shot.** Show the click, the typing, the send and the publish as separate macro moments instead of one wide screen recording.
3. **Alternate tiny and huge.** Small objects floating in black, then extreme macro crops that run off-frame, then pull back. The scale contrast creates the drama without any camera shake.
4. **Fake UI that reads as real:** a model picker ("Opus 4.8"), "Desktop 1200", plausible landing-page copy and KPI numbers. Grey secondary labels sell the realism.
5. **Generation shown as a build process** (pixel blocks, cards assembling), not as a cut to the finished page.
6. **Calm/burst rhythm:** dense 1–1.5 s bursts of motion separated by near-static holds. The ending decays slowly (40-frame glow fade) for a premium "breath".
7. **Main cuts on beats, secondary reveals on the off-beat eighths.**
8. **Brand reveal through drain:** the glowing hero logo loses its glow and colour until it becomes the flat brand mark. The visual language hands off to the real brand.
9. **Inverted end card:** the only white frame in the video makes the CTA impossible to miss.

## 8. Remotion rebuild recipe
- **Composition:** `1920×1080`, `30 fps` (or 24 to match the reference), `durationInFrames ≈ 730` at 30 fps (24.3 s). For a 9:16 variant use the same scenes centred with type ×1.4.
- **Layer stack per typical scene (bottom → top):** `<AbsoluteFill style={{background:'#000'}}>` → optional `<DotMatrixField>` → the scene object inside a `<CameraRig scale translateY>` → `<GlassPanel rim="bottom|top-left" glowColor="#2a7fff">` with content → `<Cursor variant="arrow-blue|hand" path>` → `<Vignette strength={0.85}>`.
- **Components:**
  - `GlassPanel`: `background: linear-gradient(180deg, rgba(255,255,255,0.06), rgba(255,255,255,0.01)) , #0d0f14`; `border-radius: 28px`; a rim built from a 1.5 px `border` in `#4aa8ff` masked with a `linear-gradient` so only one or two edges glow; plus `box-shadow: 0 0 40px 6px rgba(30,120,255,0.55), 0 0 120px 20px rgba(30,120,255,0.25)`. Animate the glow opacity ±10% with `Math.sin(frame/12)` for breathing.
  - `TypewriterCaret`: words appear every 5 frames (`Math.floor((frame - start) / 5)`); a blue block caret `#2f6bff`, cap-height tall, sits after the last word. The outro selects the last word (blue bg) for 6 frames, then clears.
  - `UITypewriter`: characters `= Math.floor((frame - start) * 1.2)`; the caret blinks every 8 frames.
  - `SnapZoom`: `scale = frame < cutFrame ? 1 : 3.5` on the scene wrapper (no interpolation).
  - `ButtonPress`: `scale = 1 - 0.04 * pulse(frame, clickFrame, 2)`, and the pressed fill is `#1e7bff` for 3 frames.
  - `PullBackReveal`: `scale = interpolate(frame, [0, 23], [3, 1], {easing: Easing.bezier(0.6, 0, 0.2, 1), extrapolateRight: 'clamp'})`, with the transform-origin at the hero corner.
  - `PixelBlockBuild`: an N×M grid over the target region; each cell is a `#0a4cff` square visible from `delay_i` to `delay_i + 3..6` frames with a random delay (0–18 frames). The content underneath fades in where its cell has finished.
  - `OverlayPrompt`: `spring({frame, fps, config: {damping: 20, stiffness: 140, mass: 0.8}})` mapped to translateY 60 → 0 px and opacity 0 → 1; background `filter: blur(8px) brightness(0.5)` interpolated over the same 14 frames.
  - `ScrollThrough`: `translateY = -H * Math.pow(t, 3)` over 12 frames, plus `filter: url(#vblur)` with stdDeviation ∝ speed and a brightness dip on the last 2 frames.
  - `WordSwapButton`: an array of words; `index = Math.floor((frame - start) / 12)`; on each swap a 1-frame `scale(1.05)`.
  - `ClickRipple`: 3 rounded-rect outlines, scale 1 → 1.6 and opacity 0.9 → 0, staggered 1 frame, over 6 frames, plus a full-frame `brightness(1.35)` flash.
  - `NeonRing`: an SVG circle with `strokeDasharray` defining 4 gaps; `rotate(frame * 0.6deg)`; glow via `feGaussianBlur` (stdDeviation 6) merged with the source; the scene scale grows 1 → 1.25 over the shot.
  - `FloatingTiles`: 6–7 `GlassPanel` squares with per-tile `z` and sin-based drift (amplitude 6–14 px, period 40–70 frames); the entrance burst is `spring({damping: 14, stiffness: 200})` from scale 0.85.
  - `LogoGlowDrain`: the logo SVG starts as a blue outline with `drop-shadow(0 0 24px #1e6bff)`; over 40 frames interpolate the stroke to a white fill, the shadow blur 24 → 0 and the scale 1.4 → 0.6.
  - `WordmarkBloom`: an instant-on wordmark with `text-shadow: 0 0 18px #0a5cff, 0 0 40px #0a5cff` and `color: #3b8cff`, interpolated to white with no shadow over 33 frames (ease-out).
  - `EndCard`: white background, Archivo Regular 44 px black + Archivo Bold 44 px `#1a8cf0`, centred, with no animation.
- **Tricky effects:**
  - The rim light that only lights one edge can be built from a conic- or linear-gradient border via `mask-composite`, or as an SVG `rect` stroke with a `linearGradient` stroke whose stops fade to transparent.
  - The dot matrix is best done on `<canvas>` with `useCurrentFrame()` for performance (≈ 50×28 dots).
  - The bloom on black needs additive look: layer two blurred copies with `mix-blend-mode: screen`.

## 9. Variation ideas
1. **AI/SaaS feature launch:** swap the chat box and website for the user's own product UI (dashboard, editor). Keep the black void, one-edge rim glow, macro-click-type-send sequence and the word-swap button for the feature list (e.g. "Import → Analyze → Share").
2. **Fintech / crypto card promo:** a glowing glass card instead of the prompt box, a gold or green accent instead of blue, a pixel-build of a portfolio chart, and the brand drain to a flat logo at the end.
3. **Developer tool / CLI teaser (9:16):** a terminal panel as the glass object, the typewriter by character with a block caret, and a scroll-through into a code diff. Use dot-matrix dissolves as section breaks and a white end card with the install command.
