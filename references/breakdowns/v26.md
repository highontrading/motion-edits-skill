## 1. Concept & script structure
- **Genre:** entertainment-news explainer / "what you need to know" recap (setup → hype → backlash → evidence → open question → CTA).
- **Beats:**
  - 0.0–4.4 **Hook:** "Before you watch Christopher Nolan's The Odyssey, here's what you need to know." A famous name, a film title and a promise of insider context, then a 1.4 s silent-looking paper card as a breath.
  - 4.4–6.6 **Hype:** the film broke records before release (Nolan raising an Oscar on a sepia stage, applause).
  - 6.6–8.6 **Turn:** but quickly sparked backlash (the same stage regraded cold blue, Nolan facepalming, fists in the crowd).
  - 8.6–18.0 **Evidence:** fans criticised modern dialogue and changes to Homer's story (60s cinema-front plate, reaction chat bubbles, giant "story."); many claim Odysseus no longer feels like the legendary mastermind (B/W war-room plate with the warrior in colour).
  - 18.0–21.1 **Open question:** did Nolan reinvent a classic or betray it? (Nolan holding one card, then two cards = a binary choice).
  - 21.1–27.3 **CTA:** project file at earnedits.com (tablet on a flat-lay desk).
- **Hook mechanics (first 1.5 s):** frame 0 is a noir still: a B/W cinema corridor, a man in a **brown jacket kept in colour** clutching his head in front of an "ODYSSEY" poster, and the caption "Before you" on a black label box. A **blurred fedora passer-by crosses the foreground** R→L (0.3–2.9 s) and the camera settles out of a zoom (−4.4 → 0 over ~0.9 s). Colour isolation, a poster and foreground parallax make it read as a film still in motion.
- **Script → visual rule:** one sentence = one staged AI plate of a "character" (Nolan, a fan crowd, Odysseus). Each 1–3-word VO chunk becomes an italic caption placed next to the subject's head or on its chest. Story reversals reuse the **same set with a new grade and pose** instead of a new location.

## 2. Visual language
- **Art direction:** footage-led, but the "footage" is photoreal AI stills (some lightly animated) composited like film frames: a subject cut-out over a plate, a blurred foreground layer (applauding hands, a passer-by, scrolls), and practical-looking light (spotlight beams, sparkles). The final scene switches to a top-down flat-lay desk with a tablet UI mockup.
- **Backgrounds & textures:** carpeted B/W corridor; crumpled white paper with a faint grid (the breath card); a theatre stage with an arch, curtains and volumetric beams; a 1960s "Galaxy Theatre" front with a marquee; a stone war room with maps; a dark wood-panelled library; a walnut desk.   - CTA desk: walnut `#482415`, `#5f392a`, `#825641`.
  - Accent: **lime-yellow** (`#d6d43a` by eye) for the hand-drawn loop, matching the yellow recolour.
- **Typography:**
  - **Primary caption:** a high-contrast **italic serif** (Instrument Serif Italic / Playfair Display Italic / Cormorant Italic class), regular weight, **lowercase sentence fragments**, white `#f4f1ec` with a soft dark shadow/glow (~8 px blur, 40%). Typical chunk width 30–40% of frame ("broke records" ≈ 37%, font ≈ 44 px at 720 w ≈ 6% of width).
  - **Hook label:** the same italic serif in white on a **solid black rectangle** (tight 4–6 px padding, no radius), "Christopher Nolans" ≈ 54% of width. It is used only for the first 3 s, like a film subtitle.
  - **Paper card:** the same italic in black, small (≈ 22% of width), centred.
  - **Emphasis by scale:** "story." jumps to **bold italic at ≈ 70% of width** in the top third. The CTA ("If you want / the project", "visit / earnedits") is a **bold/black italic serif** at ≈ 55–60% of width, two lines with tight leading (~0.85).
  - **Chat bubbles:** white rounded pills with a black bold grotesk (Inter Bold / Helvetica Bold), lowercase ("i hate it", "shit……", "disappointed"), ≈ 15–21% of width, floating above heads.
- **Layout:** captions sit on the subject: beside the head (y ≈ 25–35%) when the face is the point ("backlash", "Did Nolan"), on the chest (y ≈ 45–60%) otherwise. They alternate left/right of the subject so the face stays clear. The CTA keeps type at the vertical centre, beside the device.
- **Assets:** AI-generated stills of a Nolan look-alike (Oscar, facepalm, holding cards), a 60s cinema crowd, a Greek warrior, a noir corridor; foreground blur layers (hands, fists, fedora man); 4-point sparkle glints; the earnedits.com website screenshot in a tablet frame; desk props (headphones, glasses, paper clips, pen, pencil).
- **Finishing / FX:** halftone screen, grain, vignette, red/blue fringing on hard edges (tablet), shallow **depth of field** with animated **rack focus**, spotlight beams, sparkle glints (scale 0 → 1 → 0 over 4–6 frames, 3–5 on screen at a time).

## 4. Motion & timing analysis
- **Pacing:** ~0.4 cuts/s; shots 1.1–5.0 s, median ~2.2 s. Visual change comes every 0.3–0.6 s from caption chunk swaps, so a 5 s plate (Galaxy) never feels long. It also has four camera moves and three bubbles.
- **Frame rate:** motion updates on a 2-3-2-3 frame pattern (≈12 fps) inside a 30 fps file. The stepped look reads as "edited in AE at 12 fps" and gives the AI stills a stop-motion, slightly handmade cadence.
- **Easing signatures (measured on the 12 fps steps):**
  - **Post-cut settles** on almost every shot: a zoom-out of 2–6 (analysis units) on the first step that decays geometrically (ratio ~0.6–0.8 per step) over 0.35–1.0 s. This is ease-out without overshoot, as if each still "lands" into its frame.
  - **Reframing moves inside a plate:** symmetric ease-in-out, 0.6–0.9 s total, peak at mid-move (Galaxy pans peak 29–31 px/frame at 720 w).
  - **Pre-cut ramps:** push-ins or pans accelerate over 3–5 steps (0.2–0.4 s) into the cut (6.63, 23.30, 26.03), and the next shot continues the velocity (velocity-matched).
- **Entrance / exit vocabulary:** caption chunk **replace** (new chunk appears with a soft left-to-right wipe of ~4–6 frames, judging by the partial reveals "and maj", "to Hom"); the old chunk is removed instantly or carried off by the camera; chat-bubble pop above a head; a lime stroke write-on (~12 frames); a device drop-in with rotation settle; one-frame selective recolour; focus-pull in and out.
- **Camera:** a virtual camera on 2.5D plates: settle-zoom after each cut, slow push/pull (±0.1–0.8 per step), move-hold-move reframes to the person being described, and a roll settle (rot +4.2 at 13.70). A rack focus is used as a beat marker (14.0–14.53).
- **Captions are in scene space:** when the camera pushes or pans, the captions scale and travel with the plate ("the modern" grows and shifts between 9.87 and 10.13; "with many" shrinks with the 13.6 pull-back), and they defocus with it ("but quickly" at 6.67, "Odysseus." at 14.4). That makes the text feel photographed into the scene rather than overlaid.
- **Secondary motion:** sparkle glints (4–6 frames each), a passer-by in the foreground, a plane drifting across the Galaxy sky, applauding hands, the tablet screen cycling thumbnails (~0.8–1 s each).
- **Sync:**
  - **Cuts vs VO:** extremely tight, with cuts at or just before the first word of the new chunk: 3.00/"here's" 3.00 (0.00), 4.40/"The" 4.40 (0.00), 6.63/"but" 6.70 (−0.07), 8.60/"Fans" 8.64 (−0.04), 13.60/"with" 13.68 (−0.08), 18.03/"Did" 18.12 (−0.09), 21.10/"If" 21.12 (−0.02), 26.03/"download" 26.06 (−0.03). The yellow recolour lands on "mastermind" (−0.03). The whip pan at 23.30 fills the VO pause before "visit" (23.70).
  - **Captions vs VO:** each chunk appears on its first word (≈0 to +0.15 s) and holds until the next chunk; chunks are 1–3 words, 0.3–0.8 s each.
  - **Music:** cuts at 4.40, 8.60, 20.00 and 23.30 fall within ±0.05 s of the ~103 BPM beat grid; the others do not (mean |offset| 0.12 s vs 0.145 s by chance). The edit follows the VO; the VO itself sits loosely on the music.

## 5. Transitions catalogue
1. **Focus-pull cut** (6.63, 20.00): the incoming shot starts fully defocused (≈ 20–30 px blur at 720 w), sometimes with a vertical jolt of 2–3 steps, and resolves in 6–9 frames (0.2–0.3 s). At 6.63 the outgoing shot ramps a push-in first. Rebuild: `filter: blur(interpolate(f,[0,8],[28,0]))` with `Easing.out(Easing.cubic)` on shot B; on A, scale `1 → 1.06` over the last 5 frames with `Easing.in(Easing.quad)`.
2. **Mirrored-set reversal** (4.40 → 6.63): the same stage, camera and composition, with the grade swapped (sepia → cold blue), the pose swapped (trophy → facepalm) and the foreground crowd swapped (applause → fists). The viewer reads "the same moment, reversed". Rebuild: two plates aligned pixel-for-pixel, a focus-pull cut between them, and a LUT/colour-matrix change.
3. **Velocity-matched whip pan** (22.9–23.27 → 23.30–24.7): the pan accelerates over ~0.4 s, cuts at peak and continues at 54 px/frame, decaying over ~1.3 s. Rebuild: shared `translateX` driven by one curve across both sequences; the cut sits at the curve's peak; add horizontal motion blur `stdDeviation="12 0"` around the peak.
4. **Zoom-matched cut** (25.3–26.03 → 26.9): a zoom-out ramps into the cut and the wider framing continues zooming out. It is used to return from a close-up to the hero layout.
5. **Paper breath card** (3.00–4.40): a hard cut from a dense, dark cinematic still to near-white paper with tiny black italic, followed by a 2-frame dim before the hard cut out. The contrast and the stillness reset attention after the hook.
6. **Selective recolour as a beat** (16.87): no cut. One frame switches the foreground props from neutral to acid yellow on the keyword.

## 7. What makes it high-end (the transferable lessons)
1. **Grade = narrative.** Give each beat its own colour world, and show reversals by regrading the *same* set (sepia triumph → cold backlash) rather than cutting to a new place.
2. **Keep one subject in colour** on a B/W plate (jacket, warrior) to direct the eye and signal "this is the character".
3. **Captions live in the scene.** Parent them to the camera rig so they push, pan and defocus with the image; place them beside the head or on the chest and alternate sides.
4. **One typeface, scale for emphasis.** Regular italic serif for flow, bold italic at 60–70% width for the single climactic word ("story.") and the CTA.
5. **Settle every cut.** A 0.35–1.0 s zoom-out ease-out on each new still makes stills feel shot rather than placed.
6. **Move-hold-move inside long plates.** Reframe to whoever the VO is talking about (push → pan → pan → pull back), and pop a reaction bubble when the camera arrives.
7. **Rack focus and a single-frame recolour as punctuation** instead of more cuts.
8. **A deliberate 12 fps cadence** makes AI stills feel handcrafted and hides interpolation artefacts.
9. **Cuts on the first syllable** (median lead ~0.04 s); bridge VO pauses with camera moves, not holds.

## 8. Remotion rebuild recipe
- **Composition:** `1080×1920`, `30 fps`, but quantise all animation to 12 fps: `const f12 = Math.floor(frame / 2.5) * 2.5` (or render at 12 fps and let the platform upconvert). Duration = VO + 0.4 s.
- **Typical scene stack (bottom → top):** `<Plate src grade={LUT} selectiveMask?>` → `<SubjectCutout>` (for colour isolation and occlusion) → `<InSceneCaption>` → `<ForegroundBlurLayer src blur={18} drift>` (hands, passer-by) → `<Sparkles count={4}>` → all inside `<CameraRig scale x y rotate focusBlur>` → outside the rig: `<Halftone opacity={.15}>`, `<Grain opacity={.06}>`, `<Vignette strength={.3}>`.
- **Components & timings (30 fps frames):**
  - `CameraRig({keys})`: a keyframed scale/x/y/rotate with an **entry settle** of scale `1.12 → 1.0` over 24 frames (`Easing.out(Easing.exp)`); reframes of 18–27 frames with `Easing.inOut(Easing.sine)`; `focusBlur` interpolated per key (0 ↔ 14 px).
  - `InSceneCaption({chunks, anchor:'head'|'chest', side:'L'|'R'})`: Instrument Serif Italic 64 px, white, `text-shadow: 0 2px 10px rgba(0,0,0,.45)`. Each chunk enters with a gradient-mask wipe left→right over 6 frames (`mask-image: linear-gradient(90deg, #000 X%, transparent X+15%)`) and is replaced on the next chunk's first word. It is rendered *inside* `CameraRig`, so it inherits scale, pan and blur.
  - `HookLabel`: the same font on `background:#000; padding:4px 10px`, used only for the hook.
  - `EmphasisWord({text:'story.', widthPct:70})`: Instrument Serif / Playfair **Bold Italic**, pops with `spring({damping:16, stiffness:160})` scale 0.9 → 1, top third.
  - `ChatBubble({text, x, y})`: white pill, radius 999, padding `10px 22px`, Inter Bold 34 px black; pop scale 0 → 1 with `spring({damping:12, stiffness:260, mass:.5})`, timed to the camera's arrival on that person.
  - `WrapStroke({path, color:'#d6d43a', width:6, frames:12})`: an SVG path drawn with `strokeDashoffset`; split it into front/back segments and put the back segments *below* `<SubjectCutout>` so the loop passes behind the head (fake 3D wrap).
  - `SelectiveRecolour({mask, color:'#c9b40c', at})`: at `at`, swap the masked prop layer to a `feColorMatrix` tint (hard switch, 1 frame).
  - `FocusPullCut({from, to, blurFrames:8, jolt:[12,-18,6]})`: see §5.1.
  - `MirroredSetReversal({plateA, plateB, lutA:'sepia', lutB:'coldBlue'})` built on `FocusPullCut`.
  - `WhipPanCut({distance:1.4*width, rampIn:12, rampOut:36})`: one shared velocity curve across both scenes.
  - `DeviceDrop({src, from:{y:-200, rotate:-8, scale:1.1}, spring:{damping:14, stiffness:140}})` for the tablet, layered **over** the CTA text (text-behind-device).
  - `Sparkles`: 4-point star SVGs, each `scale 0 → 1 → 0` over 10–15 frames with a slight rotation, random positions near highlights.
- **Tricky effects:** Selective colour: duplicate the plate, desaturate the bottom copy (`saturate(0)`), and show the colour copy only through the subject's roto mask (an AI matte, or a pre-cut PNG). The halftone uses a pattern overlay in `multiply`. The rack focus needs separate plate/subject/caption layers so each can get its own `blur()`.

## 9. Variation ideas
1. **Sports drama recap** ("Before the Finals, here's what you need to know"): stadium plates graded gold for the win and cold for the loss using the mirrored-set reversal, in-scene italic captions beside the player, chat bubbles for fan reactions.
2. **Tech product controversy** ("Apple's new iPhone backlash"): keynote-stage plates with sparkles → cold regrade, a reviewer-crowd plate with bubbles, a flat-lay CTA with the product on the desk instead of a tablet.
3. **History/myth explainer** ("Who was the real King Arthur?"): B/W castle plates with the hero in colour, lime wrap-around strokes on key figures, a recolour of the map on the keyword, and a paper breath card for the central question.
