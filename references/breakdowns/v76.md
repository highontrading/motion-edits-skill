# v76 — "The Generation Effect": brand-psychology explainer built from nested UI and device reveals

**Source creator handle:** earnera.co · **Format:** 720×1280, 9:16, 18 fps (delivered file; probably a 30 fps master decimated on upload), 41.8 s · **Shots:** ~24 real (31 detected: 7 are white flashes, bar wipes, light leaks or twirl frames; 3 cuts were missed) · **Avg shot:** ~1.75 s (median ~1.5 s) · **Music BPM:** ~129 (soft bed under VO; cuts are not beat-locked) · **VO:** yes, English, calm, confident male narrator, ~2.6 words/s

**One-line essence:** each VO clause is staged inside a different familiar "container" (lock screen, Windows XP Notepad, iMessage thread, Instagram post, pinned note, magazine spread), and the edit keeps shrinking the last scene into the next container, so the viewer keeps doing a small "oh, it's inside a phone" decode. That is the generation effect the video is explaining.

## 1. Concept & script structure
- **Genre:** marketing-psychology micro-explainer (named principle → mechanism → two brand proofs → reframe → actionable rule → CTA). It is the same format as v05, from a different creator, with UI containers instead of print collage.
- **Beats:**
  - 0.0–4.6 **Hook:** "If you want people to actually remember your brand, you need them to do one thing: think." This is a conditional promise followed by a one-word payoff, and the word THINK gets its own full-frame poster at 4.39 s.
  - 5.2–6.4 **Named principle:** "This is the generation effect." It arrives as lock-screen notifications, so the principle is literally "delivered" to the viewer.
  - 6.6–11.1 **Mechanism:** effort to understand → the brain remembers longer (Notepad typing → system dialog → pinned BRAIN note).
  - 12.1–17.6 **Proof 1, Nike:** football footage, then the Jordan cut-out, then an iMessage thread about "inner battles".
  - 17.9–23.5 **Proof 2, Apple:** a minimal Apple-style title, a "Shot on iPhone" triptych collapsing into a phone, and a "Campaign" magazine spread, then memories and creativity on photo cards.
  - 24.4–30.7 **Reframe:** "You're not just watching the ad, you're finishing it in your head." Plain black on white, then an Instagram post: "That tiny bit of effort… makes a message unforgettable."
  - 31.5–36.9 **Rule:** "Don't give everything away. Use emotion, story or a twist…" One letterboxed card per noun.
  - 37.2–41.8 **CTA:** "Editors, comment 'project' and we'll send you access to the AEP file."
- **Hook mechanics (first 1.5 s):** a grid-paper desk with four blurry, glossy primitive shapes (red square, yellow square, green pentagon, green circle) and a tiny pixel-art character walking. Words type on beside the pentagon ("If you want people") over a green underline bar. At 0.67 s the camera whips up (pan_y −12 analysis px/frame ≈ 48 px/frame real) into a field of floating green notification pills with heavy depth-of-field, and the character is now climbing one of them. Three layers of motion (type, character, DOF pills) all land inside 1.3 s.
- **Script → visual rule:** every clause gets a recognisable **interface or object that would naturally carry that sentence**. A message becomes a notification or chat bubble, effort becomes typing in Notepad, understanding becomes a system dialog, memory becomes a pinned note or photo cards, and a principle becomes a lock-screen alert. The VO keyword is either typed into the UI or set huge on its own poster (THINK, EMOTION, STORY, TWIST, EDITORS, ACCESS).

## 4. Motion & timing analysis
- **Pacing:** ~0.57 cuts/s; shots are 0.56–3.1 s with a median ~1.5 s. The opening 7 s is fastest (shots of 0.6–1.7 s). The middle loosens to 2–3 s shots, but each contains 3–6 internal events (type-ons, slides, swaps), so the **event rate stays at ~1 event per 0.4–0.5 s** throughout.
- **Cut ↔ VO sync:** cuts are VO-driven, not beat-driven. Only 10/25 cuts fall within ±0.1 s of a beat (≈ chance at 129 BPM). Measured offsets from cut to the first word of the clause: 4.39 → "think" 4.40 (0.0 s), 13.44 → "They" 13.44 (0.0 s), 19.11 → "The" 19.12, 12.06 → "Nike" 12.14 (−0.08 s), 17.83 → "Apple" 17.90 (−0.07 s), 9.78 → "their" 9.86 (−0.08 s), 31.44 → "So" 31.52 (−0.08 s), 37.11 → "Editors" 37.22 (−0.11 s), 39.39 → "we'll" 39.48 (−0.09 s), 4.94 → "This" 5.18 (−0.24 s), 15.0 → "they" 15.30 (−0.30 s). **Typical: the cut leads the clause by 0–0.1 s**, occasionally 0.25–0.3 s when the new scene needs a beat to settle (a zoom-out reveal). Keyword swaps inside a shot are frame-exact (STORY 34.15 vs 34.18; TWIST 35.0 vs 35.06; "comment" 38.0 vs 38.0).
- **Word reveal timing:** UI text types on *with* or slightly *after* the VO (Notepad lags ~0.25 s, which feels like "the viewer typing"). Poster keywords appear on the word (±1 frame). Small emphasis marks (squiggle, selection box) land 0.0–0.4 s *before* their word.
- **Easing signatures (from per_frame.csv, 18 fps):**
  - **Post-cut camera settle** = exponential ease-out starting at max velocity on the cut frame. Examples: THINK tilt 8.5 → 5.4 → 3.7 → 2.7 → 2.0 → 1.3 (decay ≈ 0.68/frame, ~7 frames ≈ 0.4 s); BRAIN push 4.6 → 2.7 → 1.9 → 1.5 → 1.2 → 1.0 (~8 frames ≈ 0.45 s); CTA settle 3.0 → 1.2 → 0.8 → 0.5 (~5 frames). No overshoot anywhere (no sign flip in pan/zoom after a settle). This is an `Easing.out(Easing.exp)`-style feel, not a spring.
  - **Device zoom-out reveal** = ease-in-out over ~9 frames (0.5 s): velocity peaks on frame 4 (z −5.7) and is symmetrical. That is `Easing.bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1)`.
  - **Punch-zoom accent** = a single frame of +8.5 zoom (≈ +4% scale) then back to drift. Used once on "remembers".
  - **Accelerating push into a hard cut** (14 → 15): zoom 1.6 → 2.7 → 5.6 over the last 3 frames, i.e. an ease-in that is cut at peak speed.
- **Entrance / exit vocabulary:**
  - typewriter with block caret (Notepad, PC caption, IG caption): ~2 chars/frame at 18 fps
  - word-by-word pop on VO word (Apple, Nike, triptych)
  - blur → sharp per word: 2–3 frames, ~12 px → 0 blur, opacity 0.3 → 1
  - **per-letter blur reveal** (TWIST, ACCESS): ~1 letter/frame, letters left in light tint until settled
  - **out-of-order letter pop** (EDITORS), 4 frames total
  - notification row drop-in: slides down 1 row height, ~4 frames, slight spring
  - dialog grow from icon: 16 px → full, 4 frames
  - script write-on: ~1 letter per frame
  - card slide-in with rotation (AND, Ceativity): ~4 frames ease-out, ending at −5° to −10°
  - sticker pop with rotation (exclamation marks, Ae icon): scale 0 → 1.1 → 1, 3–4 frames
  - squiggle/underline/arrow draw-on: 3–6 frames
  - karaoke grey → black word darken
  - hard swap of keyword (EMOTION → STORY)
- **Camera:** mostly "virtual desk camera" moves. Slow continuous push-ins with roll (Notepad: +53 zoom with +32° roll over 3 s), tilts up (lock screen, iMessage), lateral pans (wood desk −48, campaign +37) and 3D orbits on the IG card. Every shot has *some* camera motion; nothing is locked off except the flat white type cards, which are the deliberate "breath" moments (mean flow 0.29–0.30).
- **Secondary motion:** pixel character walk cycle (on 2s), spinning film reel, a parallax paragraph drift behind the retro PC, floating Ae icon wobble ±15°, ghost-word horizontal scroll behind EMOTION/STORY, light-leak sweeps, and DOF pills drifting at different speeds.

## 5. Transitions catalogue
1. **Nested reveal zoom-out (signature)**: 4.94 s THINK → lock-screen wallpaper; 15.0 s Jordan scene → iMessage image; 19.94 s triptych → iPhone screen; 27.44 s white type card → Instagram post. The outgoing scene is *not* cut away: it keeps playing and is scaled down to become the content of a UI element in the next scene. Duration 2–9 frames. The faster versions hide the switch behind a 1–2 frame white flash. Rebuild: render scene A in a `<Sequence>` inside a container whose transform animates from `scale(1)` full-bleed to the device-screen rect (`scale(0.42) translate(...)`, `borderRadius` 0 → 48 px) over 9 frames with `bezier(0.65,0,0.35,1)`, while the device frame and background fade in at 0–2 frames.
2. **White flash / light burn**: 4.94 (2 frames), 11.89–12.06 (bloom 2 frames + pure white 2 frames), 30.11 (2 frames). An orange-white radial bloom grows from a corner and clips to white. Rebuild: an absolutely positioned radial-gradient layer, opacity 0 → 1 over 2 frames with `mix-blend-mode: screen`, then a solid white frame, then cut.
3. **Bar wipe** at 6.61 s: a light-grey vertical panel (~25% width) sweeps across L→R in 3 frames, with the new scene revealed behind its trailing edge. Rebuild: a `div` translating `-30% → 130%`, with the clip-path of the new scene tied to the panel's x.
4. **Light-leak carry** at 18.8–19.2 s: a pink/coral leak tints the outgoing type and is still on screen in the first 2 frames of the next shot, so the cut hides inside it. Rebuild: a leak video/PNG overlay across the cut point, `screen` blend, 10–14 frames.
5. **Slice glitch** at 19.89 s: 2 frames where the words break into horizontal slices offset ±10 px. Rebuild: 4–6 horizontal `clip-path` bands with random `translateX`.
6. **Twirl / swirl** at 34.78–34.94 s: the card twists into a vortex (rotation increasing toward the centre) over 3 frames, then the next card appears already settled. Rebuild: SVG `feDisplacementMap` driven by a pre-rendered swirl map, or a WebGL twirl shader in `@remotion/three`; angle 0 → 540° at the centre over 3–4 frames.
7. **Collapse to strip** at 31.44 s: the 3D-tilted IG card squashes to a thin horizontal band that *is* the next scene's iMessage band. Rebuild: `scaleY` 1 → 0.2 over 3 frames, crossfading content.
8. **Icon-led transition** at 39.39 s: the Ae icon flies toward camera and rotates, covering the change from full-frame to the letterboxed card. Rebuild: icon `scale 0.6 → 1.4`, `rotate -25° → 10°`, over 4 frames; it sits on top of both sequences.
9. **Hard cut out of accelerating push** at 22.11 s, plus plain hard cuts at 4.39, 9.78, 13.44, 24.5 s (all on a VO word).
10. **Defocus-out** at 17.78 s: Gaussian blur ramps to ~12 px over 3 frames before cutting to a clean white card.

## 6. Sound design
- A soft, mid-tempo (~129 BPM) bed sits well under the VO (rms mean 0.14). The VO is continuous with pauses ≤ 1.0 s (longest gap 11.14 → 12.14 s, covered by the white burn).
- Strong onsets line up with visual hits rather than the beat grid: 4.39 (THINK cut), 6.46/6.66 (bar wipe), 11.96 (white burn), 12.40 (Nike footage cut), 15.37 (zoom-out reveal), 17.90 (Apple), 24.61 (white card), 28.33 (caption), 30.70 (light burn), 31.77, 34.39 (STORY), 37.08/38.08/38.55 (EDITORS / comment / project), 39.45 (Ae icon). This suggests whoosh, pop or click SFX on every transition and keyword.
- UI sounds are implied: notification dings for the lock-screen rows, keyboard clicks under Notepad and caption typing, a Windows error "ding" for the dialog, and an iMessage send swoosh.

## 7. What makes it high-end (the transferable lessons)
1. **Put the sentence inside the interface that would carry it.** A notification for a named principle, a chat bubble for an aside, a dialog for "understand", a pinned note for "remember". Familiar UI is instantly decoded and feels native to a phone viewer.
2. **Nest scenes instead of cutting away.** Shrink the last scene into the next container (wallpaper, chat image, phone screen, IG post) 3–4 times per minute; each one is a small reveal that rewards attention.
3. **Alternate density.** Busy collage or UI scenes (5–8 layers, DOF, parallax) are followed by a flat off-white card with one line of SF-style type and almost no motion. The quiet card makes the next busy scene hit harder.
4. **One emphasis device per keyword, and never the same twice:** squiggle, selection box, glow underline, colour swap, letter-by-letter blur, out-of-order pop.
5. **Cut on the word.** Hard cuts land within 0–0.1 s before the clause, and keyword swaps land on the exact frame of the spoken word.
6. **Camera always eases out from the cut** (exponential decay over ~0.4 s, no overshoot), which makes each new scene feel like it was "landed on", not just switched to.
7. **Change the canvas shape to signal a new section.** Going from full 9:16 to letterboxed 16:9 cards at 27 s marks the "rules" segment without any title card.
8. **Brand colour as the thread.** One green runs from the hook pills to THINK, TWIST, the folder and ACCESS; red is reserved for tiny marks.

## 8. Remotion rebuild recipe
- **Composition:** `1080×1920`, `30 fps` (the reference is 18 fps; multiply its frame counts by 1.67), duration = VO + 0.4 s (~1260 frames for 42 s).
- **Timing source:** a whisper word-timestamp JSON drives everything. `cutAt(clauseStart) = clauseStart − 2 frames`; keyword swap `= wordStart`; UI type-on `= wordStart + 4 frames`.
- **Layer stack (collage scene):** `<PaperBackground variant="grid|blueprint|tiles|wood" lightShaft?>` → `<DepthLayer blur={14}>` (background props) → `<Cutout src stickerBorder={8} shadow>` hero → `<DepthLayer blur={0}>` type → `<DepthLayer blur={18} scale={1.3}>` foreground props → `<LightLeak>` → `<Grain opacity={0.07}>`, all inside `<DeskCamera>`.
- **Components:**
  - `DeskCamera`: wraps a scene and applies `scale/translate/rotate` from keyframes. Use `interpolate(f, [0, 8], [startScale, 1], {easing: Easing.out(Easing.exp)})` for the post-cut settle (start ~1.06 scale or a 40–60 px offset), plus a slow linear drift of 0.15%/frame for the rest of the shot.
  - `NestedReveal({from: <SceneA/>, toRect, device: 'iphone'|'imessage'|'igPost'|'polaroid'})`: animates SceneA from full-bleed to `toRect` over 9 frames with `Easing.bezier(0.65,0,0.35,1)`, rounding corners and fading the device chrome in.
  - `LockScreenNotifications({items: [{app, title, body, at}]})`: rows drop in using `spring({damping: 14, stiffness: 220, mass: 0.6})` on translateY (−110% → 0) and opacity.
  - `XPWindow` / `RetroDialog` (title bar `#0a246a → #3a6ea5` gradient, pixel font) plus `Typewriter({text, cps: 36, caret: 'block'})`.
  - `IMessageThread({bubbles: [{side, text, at}]})` with word-by-word fill and a "Read 12:30 PM" footer.
  - `InstagramPost({image, caption, likes})` inside a `<Card3D rotateX/rotateY>` (CSS `perspective: 1200px`) for the slow orbit (±8° over 1.2 s).
  - `BlurWordReveal({words, perWord: 3})`: each word `filter: blur(12px→0)`, `opacity 0.3→1`, `color` light tint → final, over 3 frames.
  - `LetterBlurReveal` (TWIST/ACCESS: 1 letter per 2 frames at 30 fps) and `ScatterLetterPop` (EDITORS: random order, all done in 7 frames, each letter `scale 0→1` with `spring({damping: 10, stiffness: 300})`).
  - `KaraokeDarken({words, from: '#7a7a7a', to: '#111'})`.
  - `GhostWordCard({word, subtitle})`: a letterboxed `1080×608` band; the ghost word at 2.5× in `#e3e3e3` scrolls `translateX` 0 → −8% over the shot; the keyword is in Anton with `letterSpacing: '0.15em'`.
  - `Squiggle`, `UnderlineDraw`, `ArrowDraw`: SVG paths with `strokeDashoffset` over 4–6 frames.
  - Transitions: `WhiteBurn` (radial bloom 2 frames + white 2 frames), `BarWipe` (5 frames at 30 fps), `SliceGlitch` (3 frames), `Twirl` (WebGL shader, 5 frames), `CollapseToStrip` (scaleY 1 → 0.2, 5 frames), `IconLedWipe`.
  - `LightLeak` (pre-rendered leak footage, `mixBlendMode: 'screen'`, 0.6 opacity, 15–20 frames).
- **Tricky bits:** depth-of-field is easiest as 3 discrete blur planes (0 / 8 / 18 px) rather than a real lens. The page-curl unroll can be a 6-frame sprite sequence or a CSS 3D `rotateX` on a folded corner triangle. The twirl needs WebGL; a cheap fallback is rotating 6 concentric clipped rings of the card by increasing angles.

## 9. Variation ideas
1. **SaaS feature explainer:** keep the "sentence inside its UI" rule. A feature name arrives as a Slack notification, a pain point as a Jira error dialog, the fix as a Notion page typing itself, and the proof inside a LinkedIn post. Swap the green for the product brand colour.
2. **Personal-finance tip:** containers become a banking-app push alert, a calculator, a receipt pinned to a corkboard, an iMessage from "Mom". Flat white cards carry the rule ("Pay yourself first"). The nested-reveal moves into a phone banking screen.
3. **Psychology or self-help series:** keep the letterboxed GhostWordCard for each principle (EMOTION / STORY / TWIST → e.g. CUE / ROUTINE / REWARD) with the twirl transition between them. Use a warmer palette (terracotta + cream) and replace the UI with journal pages and sticky notes.
