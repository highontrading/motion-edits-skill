**One-line essence:** a software tutorial with no raw screen recordings. Every After Effects panel is cut out as a rounded "sticker card" with a thick white border, laid on a green cutting mat, and filmed by one constantly drifting camera at 16 fps with grain and vignette, so a dry AE walkthrough feels like a hand-made documentary animation, the same look it is teaching.

## 1. Concept & script structure
- **Genre:** AE tutorial promo / lead magnet ("result → steps → comment-to-get-the-files").
- **Beats:**
  - 0.0–8.0 **Hook (result first):** photo cards of Robert Downey Jr. and Marvel fly onto a green cutting mat, then the Iron Man card takes over. "VOX / documentary animation" title, then "PARALLAX Effect" on a brick wall, and the camera flies into the card to show the finished parallax shot.
  - 8.0–12.5 **What it is:** the shot dissolves through a transparency checkerboard into its layered AE build (3D axis gizmos on clouds and buildings) while the VO explains "2D animation + 3D camera".
  - 12.5–23.5 **Step 1, assets:** pull-out into the AE UI → "ASSESTS" folder → a vertical scroll of five Pinterest search cards (IRON MAN PNG, FLAMES, CITY BUILDINGS, CLOUDS, FLARE EFFECT) → "for the energy in his hand" → chevron fold → Pinterest logo.
  - 23.5–49.0 **Steps 2–7 in AE:** open AE → sky-blue solid → 3D camera (with a perspective diagram) → import and 3D layers → puppet pins → null + parallax (the comp shatters) → 3D text.
  - 49.0–59.1 **Finishing:** adjustment layer, Noise 10%, Posterize Time 17 → "STYLIZED DOCUMENTARY LOOK" reveal.
  - 59.1–68.1 **CTA:** "How these animations are actually made?" → "Comment EDIT" → whip along a red marker line to "THE FULL TUTORIAL LIBRARY" → AEP file icon, "Project files TO STUDY AND RECREATE IT YOURSELF."
- **Hook mechanics:** frame 0 is already textured (a green cutting mat with grain and ruler numbers). Between 0.4 and 1.4 s, three photo cards slide in from the edges at different angles, one every ~0.3 s, each casting a soft shadow. The background desaturates to grey as the Iron Man card scales up to fill ~60% of the frame (1.7–2.6 s). Recognisable IP (Iron Man, RDJ, Marvel) plus a famous style name (Vox) appear inside 3 s.
- **Script → visual rule:** every noun in the VO becomes a physical object on the mat: a titled card, an icon sticker or a UI card. Every verb becomes a camera move or a UI state change (open = cursor click, create camera = a camera diagram, rig = pushpin stickers, null + parallax = a shatter into motion). Spoken nouns become huge outlined display titles.

## 2. Visual language
- **Art direction:** tactile desk collage over software UI. Real AE panels (Effect Controls, timeline, comp viewer, menus, colour picker) are cropped into rounded rectangles with a 6–10 px white border and a drop shadow, and placed on physical surfaces like printed stickers. Menus sit on torn-paper circles. Icons (AE, Pinterest, folder, pushpins, DSLR camera, hand cursor) are die-cut stickers.
- **Backgrounds & textures:**
  - Main set: a **green self-healing cutting mat** (`#1d513c` base, `#2c6852`/`#3d765c` mid, lighter grid lines `#6c9c81` at ~70 px pitch, ruler numbers on the edges) with a heavy vignette.
  - Variants: flat green construction paper `#4a9a72` with fibre texture (AE-icon and window scenes); a grey blurred wall in the hook; red brick wall plus clouds for the "Parallax" title; **grey graph paper with teal lines** (`#aaaaa9` paper, `#4f9e9c` lines, ~110 px cells) **under window-light gobo shadows** for the CTA.
  - The background changes at each chapter (hook → brick → mat → paper → mat → graph paper).
- **Colour:** the mat green sets the base mood. The accents come from the subject: sky blue `#399bd7`/`#4494cf`, Iron Man red `#992024`/`#782c24`, gold `#e9b61d`. Each section title has its own colourway (red/yellow for IRON MAN, orange for FLAMES, grey/white for CITY BUILDINGS and CLOUDS, icy cyan glow for FLARE EFFECT) so the asset list reads as distinct items. CTA: red marker `#d01c22`, navy `#1b1a6e`. Grading: slightly crushed and desaturated, warm-neutral, with a strong vignette (corners ~40% darker).
- **Typography:**
  - **Section titles:** heavy condensed display sans (Anton / Bebas Kai / Impact-like), ALL CAPS, with a **2–4 px contrasting outline plus a dark drop/extrude shadow**, set at 50–90% of frame width ("CITY BUILDINGS" ≈ 88%, "IRON MAN" ≈ 75%, "FLAMES" ≈ 50%). Each title has its own fill and outline: red fill + yellow outline (IRON MAN, PARALLAX), orange + cream outline (FLAMES), dark grey + white outline (CITY BUILDINGS), white inline/outline (CLOUDS), pale-cyan fill + outer glow (FLARE EFFECT), cream with dark shadow (ASSESTS, misspelled on screen).
  - **Brand-ish titles:** "VOX" in a heavy slab/soft serif (Rockwell Extra Bold / Clarendon Black-like) in mustard `#d9c94a` with a black outline and shadow, ~45% of width. "STYLIZED DOCUMENTARY" in a rounded ultra-black (Bowlby One / Luckiest Guy-like), white fill + red outline. "Comment" in a red rounded heavy face, underlined.
  - **Label badges:** small text on **tape strips**: "documentary animation" in white geometric sans (Poppins Medium-like) on a black tape bar; "Effect" in a small serif on a white paper tag; "LOOK", "EDIT" and "Pinterest" on red or white torn tape with curled corners.
  - **Narration snippets:** small white geometric sans, lowercase, top-left ("for the energy in his hand"), ~35% of width.
  - **CTA end line:** "Project files" in white medium sans with a soft shadow, then "TO STUDY AND RECREATE IT YOURSELF." in navy condensed caps with a white stroke, three lines, word-staggered.
  - **Emphasis:** the spoken noun = the title. Each title is set per letter, with letters dropping in staggered and bouncing.
- **Layout:** a single hero card centred, occupying 55–75% of width, with its title stacked directly above it (y ≈ 10–30%). During the asset scroll the items stack vertically like a feed: one in focus centre, the previous one blurred below. The AE "workspace" composition puts the comp card centre-top (y 10–60%) and the timeline card as a strip at y ≈ 65–80%.
- **Assets:** Iron Man cut-out PNGs, RDJ and Marvel posters, city skyline cut-outs (colour and B/W), cloud PNGs, flame/flare PNGs, Pinterest screenshots, AE UI crops, AE and Pinterest logos, a yellow folder icon, pushpin and DSLR line-icons, a hand cursor sticker, hand-drawn white arrows (one curved arrow and one loop arrow), red marker ellipses and lines.
- **Finishing / FX:** **posterize time ≈ 16–17 fps**, film grain/noise (~10%), heavy vignette, **depth of field** (background cards blurred at 8–15 px), drop shadows under every card (offset ~8 px, blur ~20 px, 50% black), torn-paper edges, a jittering "boiling" white outline on UI cards in the finishing section (51–57 s), and window-light gobo shadows on the CTA paper.

## 4. Motion & timing analysis
- **Pacing:** only ~6 hard cuts in 68 s (0.09/s), but a **new visual beat every ~1–1.5 s**: a card lands, a title drops, the camera reframes or the UI changes state. The perceived pace comes from the constantly moving camera (mean flow 1.2–4.0 px/f at 180 px, i.e. 5–16 px/f real) rather than from cutting. Fastest section: the asset feed (14.8–20.1), one item per ~1.1 s with the highest sustained flow (mean 4.0). Slowest: Effect Controls (51–57 s), where the camera only creeps.
- **Frame rate as style:** the whole video is **posterized to ~16 fps** (holds every ~2nd frame). Motion therefore reads as stepped and stop-motion-like, and per-drawn-frame displacement is doubled (e.g. card entrances land in ~6 drawn frames ≈ 12 real frames). This is exactly the "Posterize Time 16–17" the VO teaches: the video demonstrates its own lesson.
- **Easing signatures (measured on non-held frames):**
  - Camera pull-outs/push-ins are **long ease-in-out** (chevron reveal: zoom +0.5 → +5.8 → +0.4/f over ~32 f; Effect Controls push ~60 f).
  - Big chapter transitions **accelerate into the change** (12.0→13.0 pull-out: zoom −0.3 → −5.9/f; 31.8→31.97 punch: +1.1 → +7.9/f in 5 f; whip 62.8→63.23: px −2.2 → −10.9/f in ~12 f).
  - After a hard cut the new shot **starts at peak velocity and decays** (46.57: py 15.4 → 3.0 in 8 f; 14.77: 9.9 → 4.4 in 5 f; 65.23: zoom settle over ~20 f).
  - **Overshoot on objects and on the whip pan:** the whip-pan sign flip (+1 → +3.9/f for ~6 f), the Pinterest logo 1.3× pop, the AE icon hover 1.4×, and the CTA card's rotation settle (rot alternates ±1.4 over ~10 f, a damped spring).
- **Entrance / exit vocabulary:**
  - Card slide-in with rotation and drop shadow (~6 drawn frames).
  - **Per-letter drop-and-bounce titles** (letters fall from ~40 px above, ~2 letters per drawn frame, slight random baseline offsets that settle).
  - Tape-strip slide + type-on label.
  - Hand-drawn arrow and marker-ellipse write-ons (~8 f).
  - Sticker pop with ring burst.
  - Menus unfolding from crumpled paper.
  - Glass shatter exit (~15 f, shards with gravity and spin).
  - Cards blurring out as they leave the focal plane (DOF rack).
  - Word-staggered CTA lines.
- **Camera:** a constant **virtual multiplane camera** that never stops: slow push-ins on detail (to 2–2.5× for the puppet pins and Effect Controls), pull-outs for context (net zoom −95 in shot 5), vertical feed scroll, tilt into a perspective diagram, one whip pan. Background planes are blurred to simulate depth of field.
- **Secondary motion:** flame flicker on twos, pushpin bob, boiling outlines on UI cards (re-drawn every ~3 frames), grain at 16 fps, cursor travel.
- **Sync (VO):** titles land **0.1–0.6 s after their spoken noun** (VOX +0.5, PARALLAX +0.4, IRON +0.37, FLAMES +0.37, CITY BUILD +0.19, CLOUDS +0.11, FLARE +0.5, STYLIZED +0.6). The lag is larger than in v05/v59 because the letters take ~0.3 s to drop in, so the *completed* word lands ~0.6–0.9 s after it is spoken. Event-type hits are tight: Pinterest logo +0.03, the line wipe on "Finally" +0.01, the AE cut on "Start" −0.03. Chapter cuts lead the sentence by ~0.0–0.3 s (14.77 vs "An" 14.46 is the exception, cutting +0.3 s late into the list).
- **Sync (music):** 144 BPM bed (beat 0.42 s). Of the hard cuts, 59.17 lands exactly on a beat (59.164), and 25.07 (−0.08), 28.63 (−0.09) and 49.07 (−0.09) land just before beats. The edit follows the VO and only loosely respects the beat grid.

## 5. Transitions catalogue
1. **Cloud wipe** (4.6–5.3): cloud PNG layers rise from below the frame over ~12 f (py peak +10.9/f) and fully cover it for ~2 f; the new set (brick wall) is behind them as they part. Rebuild: 2–3 cloud PNGs at 120% width translating from y=110% to y=−40% with staggered 3 f offsets, and swap scenes at the midpoint.
2. **Zoom-through into a card** (6.9–7.7): the camera scales into the hero card until its contents fill the frame; the card border passes outside the frame. Rebuild: scale the card wrapper 1 → 1.9 with `Easing.inOut(Easing.cubic)` over ~24 f, then unmount the wrapper and show the contents full-frame.
3. **Transparency-checkerboard dissolve** (8.03–8.37, 10 f): the image fades to ~80% white over a PNG checkerboard and back into the "layered" version. It visually says "these are separate PNG layers". Rebuild: an opacity crossfade through a checkerboard CSS background (`repeating-conic-gradient(#ddd 0 25%, #fff 0 50%) 0/24px 24px`) with a white overlay peaking at 0.75 at the midpoint.
4. **Accelerating pull-out to reveal the UI** (12.0–13.3): the comp shrinks into the AE viewer, ease-in, ~35 f.
5. **Chevron paper-fold wipe** (21.2–22.3): two green paper panels with a yellow edge meet in a V that travels down the screen, uncovering the next card behind it. ~30 f, ease-in-out. Rebuild: two rotated rectangles (±35°) as an SVG mask/clip-path polygon whose apex y animates from −10% to 110%.
6. **White bar wipe** (23.5): a 15%-high white strip sweeps vertically in 4 f.
7. **Glass shatter** (41.6, ~15 f): the card breaks into 20–30 polygon shards that fly outward with rotation and fall, revealing the next layer underneath. Rebuild: a precomputed Voronoi polygon set, each shard a clip-path copy of the card, animated with a velocity from the centre + gravity (y += 0.5·g·t²) and rotation.
8. **Line wipe** (49.07–49.6): a thin white vertical line (~6 px) sweeps L→R, and the area it has passed shows the new scene (a hard mask edge with a white seam). ~15 f.
9. **Whip pan along a drawn line** (62.8–63.4): the camera follows a red marker stroke to the right, ~12 f ease-in, peak ~11 px/f at 180 px (≈44 px/f at 720 px), followed by a short counter-move overshoot.
10. **Slam-in with DOF** (65.23): a big icon enters oversized and settles while the background racks to a 15 px blur.
11. **State cut** (28.63, 57.23): the layout stays put and only a property changes (comp colour, comp contents), like a before/after.

## 6. Sound design
A light, upbeat bed at ~144 BPM under fast VO (rms 0.14). Strong onsets cluster at UI events and transitions (13.28 title drop, 14.98 list start, 17.1–18.2 list items, 28.70 colour cut, 39.15–39.94 pin placements, 47.0–47.3 text, 52.3–53.9 effect values, 61.02 CTA), which suggests pop and click SFX on title drops and UI actions and whooshes on the big camera moves. The VO never pauses more than ~0.5 s. There is no silence beat; the CTA runs straight on.

## 7. What makes it high-end (the transferable lessons)
1. **Never show a raw screen recording.** Crop each UI panel into a rounded card with a thick white border and a drop shadow, put it on a textured surface, and move a camera over it. Software becomes a tactile collage.
2. **One continuous camera instead of cuts.** Chain scenes with in-camera wipes (cloud, fold, shatter, line, whip). The viewer stays "in the world" for 68 s with only ~6 hard cuts.
3. **Make the video use the look it teaches** (posterize ~16 fps, 10% noise, vignette, DOF). The demonstration is the proof.
4. **Show the result first, then deconstruct it:** the finished parallax shot dissolves through a transparency checkerboard into its layer build.
5. **Turn every spoken noun into a big outlined title** with its own colourway, dropped in letter by letter. A list of assets then reads as a list of distinct "products".
6. **Literal sticker metaphors for tool names:** pushpins for the puppet pin, a DSLR for the 3D camera, a folder for assets, glass shattering for "null + motion".
7. **Depth-of-field and blur on everything not in focus,** so a busy UI collage still has one clear focal point.
8. **Hand-drawn marks** (white arrows, red marker loops, red underline lines) guide the eye and add a human layer over the digital UI.

## 8. Remotion rebuild recipe
- **Composition:** `1080×1920`, render at 30 fps but **posterize motion to 15 fps**: compute `const f = Math.floor(frame / 2) * 2` and drive every animation from `f` (or wrap scenes in a `<Posterize every={2}>` that freezes `useCurrentFrame`). Duration = VO + 0.3 s.
- **Layer stack per scene (bottom → top):** `<CuttingMatBg grid={70} color="#2c6852" lines="#6c9c81" rulers>` (or `<GraphPaperBg>` / `<ConstructionPaperBg>`) → `<Camera2D keyframes>` wrapping everything below → `<StickerCard src border={10} radius={28} shadow="0 12px 24px rgba(0,0,0,.5)" blur={depthBlur}>` ×N (UI crops / photos) → `<OutlinedTitle>` → `<TapeLabel>` → `<Doodle type="arrow|ellipse|line" drawOn={8}>` → `<GrainOverlay opacity={0.1} fps={15}>` → `<Vignette strength={0.45}>`.
- **Components:**
  - `Camera2D`: a single parent transform (`scale`, `translate`, optional `rotateX` for the perspective tilt) interpolated across keyframes with `Easing.inOut(Easing.cubic)`. Never fully at rest: add `sin(f/45)*4 px` drift.
  - `OutlinedTitle`: per-letter spans; each letter starts at `translateY(-40px) rotate(rand±8°) opacity 0` and springs to 0 with `spring({damping: 9, stiffness: 220, mass: 0.6})`, staggered 1.5 f per letter, starting at `wordStart + 3 f`. Style: `font-family: Anton; -webkit-text-stroke: 3px <outline>; paint-order: stroke fill; text-shadow: 0 6px 0 rgba(0,0,0,.55)`.
  - `StickerCard` pop: scale `spring({damping: 12, stiffness: 180})` 0.85 → 1 + rotate ±6° → 0; slide-ins over 12 f with `Easing.out(Easing.back(1.4))`.
  - `FeedScroll`: items stacked vertically 0.55·H apart; `translateY` steps to each item at its VO word time with a 14 f `Easing.inOut(Easing.cubic)`; items off-centre get `filter: blur(6px)`.
  - `ChevronWipe`: `clip-path: polygon(0 0, 100% 0, 100% Y%, 50% (Y+25)%, 0 Y%)` animating Y from −25 to 110 over 30 f, with a yellow 8 px stroke drawn along the same path in SVG.
  - `CloudWipe`: three cloud PNGs (`width: 140%`) rising with 3 f offsets, 14 f each, `Easing.inOut(Easing.quad)`.
  - `GlassShatter`: 24 precomputed Voronoi cells; each `<div style={{clipPath: polygon(cell)}}>` holds a full copy of the card, `translate = v·t + [0, 0.5·g·t²]`, `rotate = ω·t`, opacity fades after 10 f.
  - `CheckerDissolve`: as described in §5.3 (10 f).
  - `BoilingOutline`: an SVG rect stroke with `feTurbulence` + `feDisplacementMap` whose seed changes every 3 frames (scale 3).
  - `MarkerDoodle`: SVG path with `strokeDashoffset` write-on over 8 f, `stroke="#d01c22"`, width 10, round caps.
  - `WhipPan`: camera translateX −1·W over 12 f with `Easing.in(Easing.cubic)`, then a 6 f counter-move of +4% (`Easing.out(Easing.quad)`), with a directional blur (`feGaussianBlur stdDeviation="18 0"`) during the peak frames.
  - `SlamIcon`: scale 1.35 → 1 over 20 f `Easing.out(Easing.exp)`, background `blur(15px)`.
- **Tricky bits:** the perspective "3D camera diagram" is a `transform: perspective(1200px) rotateX(55deg)` on the comp card, with children counter-rotated (`rotateX(-55deg)`) so they stand up like cut-outs; the frustum is two SVG lines from a camera icon to the card corners. For the real parallax demo, put layers in a Three.js `<ThreeCanvas>` at z = −50 / −200 / −600 and move the camera on x, or fake it in 2D by multiplying `translateX` per layer by 1.0 / 0.6 / 0.3.
