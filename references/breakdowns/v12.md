**One-line essence:** a single concrete object (a popcorn bucket with a red ticket price tag) is restaged on 16 themed sets: theatre, spotlight, sky, tarot table, gallery wall. Big-move camera grammar ties them together: decaying push-ins, ease-in zoom exits, whip pans and frame-in-frame reveals. Every clause's keyword becomes heavy, textured type built word by word on the VO.

## 1. Concept & script structure
- **Genre:** pricing-psychology micro-explainer (second-person scenario → reveal → named principle → mechanism → 3 brand proofs → thesis → CTA).
- **Beats:**
  - 0.0–1.3 **Hook:** "You walk into a movie theater." Instant POV scenario.
  - 1.9–7.9 **Scenario:** small $6 / large $9 / medium $8.50. Three prices, three slide-pushed popcorn buckets in the same spotlight.
  - 8.3–10.8 **Turn:** "Suddenly, the large feels like the obvious choice."
  - 11.6–12.9 **Reveal:** "That was never an accident." Dark statement card.
  - 13.4–15.0 **Named principle:** "It's called the Decoy Effect." Space set, title with a sparkle. Arrives at 32% of runtime.
  - 16.0–22.6 **Mechanism:** a deliberately bad middle option exists to make the expensive one feel smarter.
  - 23.0–29.2 **Proof ×3:** Netflix tiers, Apple storage, movie theatres "for decades".
  - 29.6–35.9 **Thesis:** "The middle option exists for one reason (starburst '1') … to make you spend more while feeling like you made the smart decision."
  - 36.3–41.8 **CTA:** "Editors, project file on earnedits.com. Visit now and download it."
- **Hook mechanics (first 1.5 s):** the theatre screen's white **venetian-blind stripes retract upward** (0–0.9 s) to reveal a red "THEATRE" title card. At the same time the camera **pushes into the auditorium** (zoom +4.6/frame on frame 1, decaying ×0.85 per frame) and yellow words stack **upward** from the seat backs: YOU WALK → INTO A → MOVIE. Red seat rims glow in the foreground. It opens on motion from frame 1, with no static frame.
- **Script → visual rule:** the recurring hero prop (popcorn bucket + ticket price tag) carries all the numbers. Each clause gets a new themed set that literalises it: "suddenly" = flat alarm-yellow; "never an accident" = black CRT card; "it's called" = outer space; "deliberately bad option" = tarot cards ("THE FOOL" $8.00 vs "THE STAR" $9.50); "for decades" = rotating film strips; "smart decision" = a framed painting in a gallery.

## 4. Motion & timing analysis
- **Pacing:** 0.38 cuts/s (16 shots in 42 s), shots 1.7–5.3 s, plus in-shot events every 0.3–0.6 s (price typing, word pops, slams, prop swaps). The longest shots (12: film strips 5.3 s; 8: tarot table 4.8 s) are kept alive by big camera moves.
- **Easing signatures (measured per 18 fps frame):**
  - **Entrance / settle:** exponential ease-out, ×0.80–0.88 per frame. The hook decays from +4.64 to +0.23 zoom/frame in 22 frames (1.2 s); the bucket rise settles in ~15 frames (0.8 s).
  - **Exit into cut:** exponential ease-in, ×1.3–1.5 per frame over 7–10 frames (0.4–0.55 s). Seen in shots 1, 5, 7, 12, 14 and 15.
  - **Camera re-frames inside a shot:** asymmetric S-curves (tarot tilt: ~8 frames accelerating, ~12+ decelerating; film-strip whip: ~10 accelerating, ~16 decelerating).
  - **Slams:** text or badges start at ~1.3–1.5× with motion blur and shrink to 1.0 in 5–9 frames (0.3–0.5 s) with no overshoot.
  - **Pendulum:** the one true oscillation (tablet), ~0.6 s period, ~3 visible swings, which is roughly one swing per beat (beat interval 0.53 s).
- **Entrance / exit vocabulary:**
  - venetian-blind retract
  - words stacking upward
  - price-tag typewriter
  - letter-by-letter brand typing
  - word-by-word block build
  - slam-in from large with blur
  - rise-from-bottom prop
  - carousel slide (lateral object swap in a fixed spotlight)
  - prop replace (card → $100 bill, ticket → badge)
  - hand slides in with props
  - focus-pull entrance (1 blurred frame)
  - random-order letter assembly
  - stamp (starburst)
  - per-word boxed pop-on
- **Camera:** always moving. Decaying push-ins on entry; big moves in the long shots (tarot tilt ≈ +100 px analysis = ~400 px real; film strips net pan −151/+99, zoom −108, rot −31 over 5.3 s); a diagonal whip pan in the gallery.
- **Secondary motion:** blinds; spotlights; continuous film-strip rotation; drifting SUDDENLY; the tablet swinging; the site screen recording scrolling inside the tablet.
- **Sync:**
  - **Cuts are word-locked:** offsets from the cut to the clause's first word are −0.03 (Small), −0.03 (Large), +0.05 (It's), +0.02 (make), +0.04 (Netflix), +0.02 (Movie), +0.02 (make). Some cuts sit up to 0.15 s after the word (Suddenly −0.14, That −0.12, Apple +0.13, Editors −0.11, Visit −0.13).
  - **Beat lock:** many cuts also fall on a beat or onset within ±0.06 s (8.44, 11.72, 13.39, 20.56, 22.94, 25.11, 27.22, 32.50). The edit snaps to whichever of word or beat is nearer.
  - **Hero hits land on the exact word:** the bucket rises on "large" (+0.03 s); FOR DECADES slams on "for" (+0.01 s); the starburst "1" stamps on "one" (0.00 s); DECOY slams on "Decoy" (+0.01 s).
  - Body words appear 0–0.3 s after the spoken word. Statement cards are early by up to 0.2 s (WAS appears before it is spoken).

## 5. Transitions catalogue
1. **Zoom-punch cut** (1.89, 11.72, 32.50, 36.39 s): an exponential ease-in scale over 7–10 frames, a hard cut mid-velocity, and the next shot opens with a decaying settle. The same signature as v11, so it is a house style.
2. **Zoom-through into a white flash frame** (15.61–15.78 s): the push accelerates, then 1 frame of pure white, then the next scene fades out of white over 6 frames (0.33 s) with its own zoom-out settle. Rebuild: `opacity` of a white solid 1 → 0 over 6 frames after a 1-frame hold.
3. **Carousel slide** (~4.1 and ~6.0 s): the background and spotlight stay fixed; one bucket slides out left while the next slides in from the right, ~11 frames, ease-out. Reads as "next option on the menu".
4. **Vertical slide push** (26.8–27.2 s): the photo is pushed down, revealing the next panel above, followed by 1 near-black frame before the film-strip set (a film-gate feel).
5. **Whip pan inside a collage** (29.3–30.7 s film strips; 34.7–35.4 s gallery; 39.94–40.11 s exit): accelerate ~5–10 frames to 11–13 analysis px/frame (≈45–50 px/frame real) with heavy blur, then decelerate. Used both inside a scene (to a new area of the same board) and as a cut.
6. **Frame-in-frame zoom-out** (34.39 s): the previous scene (ticket on felt) becomes a painting in a gilt frame on a damask wall, revealed by a quick pull-back, and the camera then pans to the next frame. Rebuild: render scene A into a `<Sequence>` placed inside a frame PNG's window, scale the wrapper 1.0 → 0.45 in 6 frames, then pan.
7. **Prop replacement** (19.3 s card → $100 bill; 33.7 s ticket → badge): 1–2 frame swaps with a small scale punch, which act as "cuts" without changing the set.
8. **Focus-pull entrance** (25.11 s): the first frame of the new shot is blurred, then sharp. A cheap way to sell a real photo as "camera".

## 7. What makes it high-end (the transferable lessons)
1. **One hero prop, many worlds.** The same popcorn bucket and red price tag travel through 10 different sets, so the viewer tracks the idea, not the scenery.
2. **Literalise the abstract noun:** "decoy" in space, "bad option" as THE FOOL tarot card, "smart decision" as a framed painting. Each metaphor gets a fully dressed set.
3. **The camera never rests:** decaying push on entry, ease-in punch on exit, and S-curve re-frames or whip pans inside long shots. Measured exponents (×0.85 out, ×1.4 in) give it its "expensive" feel.
4. **Big numbers and key nouns land on the exact syllable** (bucket rise, FOR DECADES, starburst "1", DECOY at ≤ 0.03 s). Everything else can trail by 0.1–0.3 s.
5. **Change the type style with the set** (condensed theatre title, wide alarm red, CRT glow, gold 3D, techno, pixel CTA), but keep one heavy grotesk as the voice so it stays coherent.
6. **Physical transitions** (carousel slide, prop replacement, frame-in-frame, hand-delivered cards, pin-hung swinging tablet) beat dissolves.
7. **Statement cards on dark texture with bloom** reset the rhythm before each named idea.
8. **Brand the CTA in-world:** the site lives on a tablet pinned to a brick wall that swings on the beat, with a stamped URL badge.

## 8. Remotion rebuild recipe
- **Composition:** `1080×1920`, `fps={30}` (the reference is 18 fps; 30 keeps the smooth-on-ones feel), duration = VO + 0.3 s.
- **Typical scene stack:** `<SetPlate texture="felt|mat|wood|brick|damask">` → `<Spotlight cx cy rx ry color="#3b5bd6" feather={60}/>` (radial-gradient ellipse, `mix-blend-mode: screen`) → `<HeroProp src="popcorn.png" priceTag="$9.00">` → `<HeadlineStack lines words>` → `<Badge variant="stitched|rounded|pixel">` → `<Grain opacity={0.07}/>` → `<EdgeVignette/>`.
- **Camera wrapper `<Cam>`** (wraps each scene; drives scale/translate/rotate):
  - `entry`: `scale = 1 + A * Math.pow(0.85, frame)` with A ≈ 0.25 (decaying push), or `interpolate(frame, [0, 36], [1.15, 1], {easing: Easing.out(Easing.exp)})`.
  - `exit`: last 9 frames `scale *= 1 + 0.2 * Easing.in(Easing.exp)(t)`, plus a radial blur via a scaled, low-opacity duplicate layer.
  - `reframe`: `interpolate(frame, [a, b], [y0, y1], {easing: Easing.bezier(0.55, 0, 0.35, 1)})` for the tarot tilt and the gallery pan, with `filter: blur()` scaled by velocity.
- **Components:**
  - `BlindsReveal` (N=8 horizontal white bars, `scaleY` 1 → 0 with a 2-frame stagger from bottom, total 16 frames).
  - `StackUp` (lines enter from y+30 px with 4-frame ease-out; new lines are inserted above the previous one).
  - `PriceTagType` (characters of "$9.00" revealed every 2 frames).
  - `CarouselSlide` (outgoing prop `translateX` 0 → −110%, incoming 110% → 0, 18 frames, `Easing.out(Easing.cubic)`, slight 6° rotation lag).
  - `Slam` (`scale` 1.45 → 1, `blur` 12 → 0 px over 9 frames, `Easing.out(Easing.exp)`; add a 2-frame 6 px camera shake on landing).
  - `TypeLetters` (NETFLIX, 1 letter/frame).
  - `ScrambleAssemble` (EDITORS: each letter appears at a random frame within 12 frames, opacity + 4 px drop).
  - `TextOnArc` (SVG `textPath` on an arc of radius ~0.6 W, words revealed by `startOffset`; duplicate in green at +6 px offset, 70% opacity).
  - `MarkerStroke` (SVG path draw-on, 12 frames).
  - `Starburst` (12-point red polygon `scale` 0 → 1.1 → 1 via `spring({damping: 9, stiffness: 220, mass: 0.6})`, number inside).
  - `FlashFrame` (1 frame white + 6-frame fade).
  - `FrameInFrame` (scene → gilt frame window, scale 1 → 0.45 in 6 frames).
  - `PendulumHang` (`rotate = 9° * Math.exp(-frame/25) * Math.cos(2π*frame/18)` about the pin point; 18 frames ≈ 0.6 s period at 30 fps).
  - `WordBoxes` (pixel font such as "Press Start 2P"/"VT323", each word in a `#3d383c` box, pop on with no easing, 3 frames apart).
