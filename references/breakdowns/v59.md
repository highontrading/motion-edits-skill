## 1. Concept & script structure
- **Genre:** SaaS / AI-tool launch promo with a comment-bait CTA ("Comment INVIDEO and I'll send you the link").
- **Beats:**
  - 0.0–2.9 **Hook:** "Your AI doesn't just think anymore. Now it can edit too." This is a claim plus a twist, delivered as kinetic type with icons standing in for letters.
  - 2.9–7.8 **Pain:** a prompt bar types "It can edit too", then an AE workstation where a ChatGPT panel slides in full of step-by-step instructions, captioned "instead of telling you how?". The AI explains, but you still have to do the work yourself.
  - 7.8–14.4 **Product + setup:** "invideo Remote Agents" with agent cursors, then picks Claude → "Connect invideo" button → loading ring → "completely free."
  - 14.4–23.4 **Demo:** a flat NLE mock. Claude splits a clip, Cursor adds title clips, Codex adds music; "generates shots you never filmed" is shown as a pink flash into real footage; the footage morphs back into a clip on a timeline where "You" and "Claude" edit together.
  - 23.4–30.5 **Contrast / positioning:** macOS dock, Premiere icon → "to edit… manually." vs. "invideo was built for [you] and [Claude][ChatGPT] to edit together."
  - 30.5–34.6 **CTA:** "Comment INVIDEO" → logo endcard over a faded app homepage, subtitle "I'll send you the MCP link for free."
- **Hook mechanics (first 1.5 s):** frame 0 is a giant black "Y", cropped off-frame, inside a dashed **design-tool selection box with corner handles**. By f8 it has turned red and is zooming out ("Your"). By f16 (0.53 s) it has settled at ~42% of frame width, and "A" is followed by the **OpenAI logo standing in for the "I"**. "doesn't" streaks in with pink ghost trails and its "o" is a **3D red prohibition-sign emoji**. "just" has a cartoon white-glove cursor over the "u", and "think" grows a glowing lightbulb in place of its "i". Every word is a small visual joke, and the selection box tells you from frame 0 that this video is about editing.
- **Script → visual rule:** one VO sentence = one scene. When the sentence is abstract (claims, positioning), it is shown as **centred type on white, typed on to the word**. When it describes an action (connect, cut, add titles, generate), it becomes a **flat diagram acted out by labelled cursors**. Brand names become real logos.

## 2. Visual language
- **Art direction:** minimal Apple/Linear-style product film. Pure white or very light lavender-grey canvases; flat geometric UI (rounded rectangles, no gradients) for timeline clips; real product logos; one photographic desk set and one meadow wallpaper as "real world" anchors; one short stock-footage clip.
- **Backgrounds:** pure white `#fefefe` for every type-only scene. A light grid for the hook (≈200 px cells, lines `#e6e6e6`) and a fine 16 px grid for the agents scene (`#faf7fb` with a soft radial vignette). A dot grid (`#e5e2e6` with darker dots every ~107 px) for the NLE mock. A **red-orange gradient mesh** (`#ff2a10` hot spot top-centre → `#fd9a77` peach bottom-right → near-white `#fde3df` left) for the single "invideo was built…" line. Photographic: the pink/white minimalist desk with AE on a monitor, and an alpine meadow wallpaper.
- **Colour:** neutral white or grey, plus a **red family as the brand accent** (type red `#b5171e`–`#c0161c`, caption pill `#e8373f`, flash pink `#f09da2`, gradient `#ff2a10`). UI clip colours are fixed and saturated: titles pink `#ec94f4`, video blue `#4794fd` / `#3e83c8`, audio green `#19a563` / dark green `#3a7666`. Agent-cursor colours follow each agent's identity: Claude salmon `#f08a8a` with a red arrow, You green `#6fd27a`/`#35b04a`, Codex green, Cursor purple `#8b2cf5`, and the Claude "Connect" button in Claude orange `#e0703f`.
- **Typography:**
  - One family throughout: **neo-grotesk, Helvetica Neue / Neue Haas Grotesk Text, Regular–Medium**, sentence case with tight default tracking. It never goes bold or condensed; the heaviest type is the invideo wordmark.
  - Sizes are **deliberately small** for a promo. Hook line ≈ 42% of frame width (cap height ~15% of frame height). Sentence lines ("completely free.", "to edit manually.") ≈ 28–37% of width (cap height ~4% of height), centred. "invideo was built for" ≈ 40% of width, white on the gradient, left-third aligned.
  - **Colour emphasis rule:** the key or contrasting word is typed in red, then turns black once the next word arrives ("Your" red / "AI" black; "opera(te)" red → replaced by "manually."; "INVIDEO" red caps → black). Older words fade to grey `#a0a0a0` as the line scrolls (28.1–30.4).
  - **Glyph substitution:** letters are swapped for icons at the same cap height (I → OpenAI knot, o → 🚫, i → 💡; in the scroll line "you" → a person avatar tile, "your AI" → Claude and ChatGPT app tiles, each tilted ±8°).
  - Captions in UI: white Helvetica on a red rounded pill ("instead of telling you how?"); small labels in rounded pills attached to cursor arrows (Figma multiplayer style).
- **Layout:** type centred at y ≈ 50% (hook at y ≈ 25–55%). UI diagrams are centred, with the flat timeline occupying ~70% of the width. Lots of negative space: type scenes are >95% white pixels (palette share 0.97–0.99).
- **Assets:** OpenAI, Claude, Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, VS Code, Gemini CLI logos; 3D emoji (prohibition sign, lightbulb, glove cursor); After Effects UI screenshot on a monitor in a styled interior; ChatGPT dark chat panel; macOS dock icons (Finder, Mail, WhatsApp, Pr, Ae, Things); stock beach footage of people jumping; invideo homepage screenshot; invideo blob logo.
- **Finishing:** almost none, on purpose. Soft drop shadows on pills and the dock, a frosted-glass dock (blur + 40% white), a radial glow behind the lightbulb and the send button, and pink motion trails on the hook letters. No grain.

## 4. Motion & timing analysis
- **Pacing:** 0.49 cuts/s, shots 0.6–4.3 s. Fast in the hook (0.6–0.8 s shots), longer for the two "explain" scenes (4.1–4.3 s), and medium (1.1–2.3 s) for the type-only contrast section. Inside the long shots something new happens every ~0.5 s (cursor move, button pop, panel slide), so the screen is never idle even when the cut rate drops.
- **Easing signature (measured):** *momentum handoff*. Exits **accelerate into the cut**: ease-in, velocity growing ×1.3–1.5 per frame for 8–11 frames (24.6→24.93 py −0.2 → −6.8; 7.6→7.8 flow 2.4 → 6.8; 30.2→30.47 zoom −0.16 → −0.90). The next shot **starts at peak velocity and decays** with ease-out (14.40: flow 11.1 → <1 in 10 f; 23.37: pan peak at f3 then a 12 f decay; 31.77: zoom peak at f2, settles by f8). The eye never sees a dead stop at a cut. No overshoot on camera moves (no sign flips in pan/zoom). Overshoot is reserved for small objects (icon tiles, buttons, dock bounce).
- **Entrance / exit vocabulary:**
  - Giant zoom-out reveal of the first word (16 f, ease-out expo).
  - Per-letter **blur-fade typing**: each glyph arrives grey/blurred and turns black over ~3 f. Typing speed ≈ VO speed (~12–15 chars/s).
  - **Scramble typing** with geometric placeholder glyphs (▯ ◐ ◧) for ~4–6 f per letter before resolving (2.7–3.1).
  - **Scale-snap** for single words: 1.4× → 1× in 2 f ("Now").
  - Colour-swap emphasis (red → black when the next word arrives).
  - Pink ghost-trail streak on letters (hook).
  - Panel slide-in from off-frame (14 f, ease-in-out).
  - Pop-in buttons and icon tiles (~6 f, spring with overshoot).
  - Loading-ring draw-on (~15 f) then fade.
  - Cursor travel paths, 15–25 f per move with ease-in-out, and a click = 2 f scale dip.
- **Camera:** virtual 2D camera only. Push-ins to a detail (9.7–10.2), pull-outs to reveal context (14.4, 19.9), step zoom (15.63: instant ~0.85× reframe as a jump cut), and a shrink-the-whole-frame-into-a-layer move (17.07–18.1). No shakes, no whip pans.
- **Secondary motion:** idle drift of multiplayer cursors (±10 px sine, ~2 s period), lightbulb glow pulse, send-button ring pulse, dock icon magnify and launch bounce.
- **Sync (VO):** cuts sit within **±0.15 s of the first word of each sentence**: 2.13/"Now" 2.14 (−0.01), 7.83/"With" 7.88 (−0.05), 14.40/"It" 14.50 (−0.10), 19.87/"Live" 19.94 (−0.07), 23.37/"Adobe" 23.34 (+0.03), 27.00/"invideo" 27.00 (0.00), 30.47/"Comment" 30.42 (+0.05), 31.77/"I'll" 31.88 (−0.11). Type is **word-locked**: "completely" VO 12.84 → on screen from 13.0; "free" VO 13.42 → ~13.5; the avatar tile pops at 28.27 for "you" (VO 28.16, +0.11). The red emphasis word lands on its VO word.
- **Sync (music):** 136 BPM (beat 0.44 s). Strong onsets coincide with six cuts within ±0.07 s (2.90/2.902, 3.53/3.506, 19.87/19.876, 23.37/23.41, 27.00/27.07, 31.77/31.79). These are most likely whoosh or click SFX laid on the cuts rather than musical downbeats. The edit follows the VO, not the bar grid.

## 5. Transitions catalogue
1. **Colour-wash flash** (3.53, 18.37): over 3–4 f the frame is flooded with a saturated tint (lilac → magenta → hot pink; sat 59 → 211). The cut happens under the flash, and the new shot starts heavily tinted, then relaxes to its natural grade over 20–25 f. Rebuild: an `AbsoluteFill` with `mix-blend-mode: color` (or `screen`) pink layer, opacity 0→1 over 4 f; cut; on the new shot apply `filter: saturate(3) hue-rotate(-20deg)` interpolated to `saturate(1)` over 24 f with ease-out. It signals "AI magic / generation".
2. **Text match-cut into UI** (2.90): the same sentence continues, mid-scramble, inside a new container (prompt bar). Keep the text layer's content and timing continuous across the cut and change only the container and background.
3. **Background-only cut, text persists** (28.13): the gradient bg cuts to white and the text stays at its position, turns grey and starts scrolling. Rebuild: put the text in a parent sequence that spans both scenes, and swap only the background layer.
4. **Momentum cut** (7.83, 24.97, 27.0, 30.47): an accelerating ease-in exit (8–11 f) cuts directly to the next shot, which begins with an ease-out move or pure white. Rebuild with `Easing.in(Easing.cubic)` on exit and `Easing.out(Easing.exp)` on entry.
5. **Step zoom (jump reframe)** (15.63): an instant scale change of ~15% with no transition, used to "zoom out a level" in a diagram.
6. **Frame-becomes-layer** (17.07–18.37): the entire scene gets a dashed bounding box with corner handles and scales down to ~80% like an object selected in a design tool, which sets up "replace this with something generated".
7. **Footage → clip morph** (19.87): the footage takes on a blue tint in 2 f and becomes the flat blue clip rectangle, then the camera pulls out to show it sitting on a timeline. Rebuild: crossfade footage → solid `#4794fd` rect at the same bounds over 3 f, then animate the rect's bounds.
8. **Fade-to-empty then draw-on** (11.8–12.0): UI elements fade/shrink out over 5 f, leaving the bare background, and a spinner draws on. It works as a "processing" beat.

## 7. What makes it high-end (the transferable lessons)
1. **Small type on lots of white reads as confident.** Sentence-case Helvetica at ~30% of frame width, centred, with nothing else on screen: the restraint of an Apple keynote.
2. **Swap letters for icons** to make a line of type into a visual pun (logo as "I", prohibition sign as "o"). It costs nothing and is very memorable.
3. **Explain software with toy diagrams, not screen recordings.** Flat coloured bars for clips, a playhead, and named cursors acting as agents. Collaboration becomes legible in one glance.
4. **Multiplayer cursors as characters.** Each AI or person has a colour and a label, and their movements tell the story ("Claude splits, Cursor adds titles, Codex adds music").
5. **Hand momentum across cuts.** Accelerate out (ease-in, 8–11 f) and start the next shot already moving (ease-out). Every cut feels driven.
6. **One emphasis colour, used temporally:** the key word is red only while it's new, then turns black.
7. **A recurring tint flash means "AI magic"** and is reused for every generative moment, so the device becomes a semantic code.
8. **Cuts land on the first word of each sentence (±0.1 s)** and type locks to the word, so the picture and the voice always agree.

## 8. Remotion rebuild recipe
- **Composition:** `1920×1080` (or 1080×1920 with the type re-flowed), 30 fps, duration = VO + 0.5 s.
- **Typical type scene stack:** `<AbsoluteFill bg="#fff">` → optional `<GridBg cell={200} color="#e6e6e6">` → `<TypeLine words={vo.words} font="Helvetica Neue" weight={500} size={0.03*W}>` → `<GlyphSwap index={n} icon=… />` → `<MomentumExit type="scale|translateY" frames={10} easing={Easing.in(Easing.cubic)}>`.
- **Typical UI scene stack:** `<DotGridBg spacing={107} dot="#c9c6cb" bg="#e5e2e6">` → `<FlatTimeline tracks=[{color:'#ec94f4'},{color:'#4794fd'},{color:'#19a563'}] radius={6}>` → `<Playhead color="#2f8cff">` → `<AgentCursor name="Claude" color="#f08a8a" path=[…] clickAt=[…]>` ×N → `<Camera2D keyframes=[{f, scale, x, y}]>`.
- **Components:**
  - `BlurFadeTyper`: chars revealed at the VO rate. Each char goes opacity 0→1, `blur(6px)`→0 and colour `#9a9a9a`→`#000` over 3 f with `Easing.out(Easing.quad)`. Start the word at `wordStart − 2 f`.
  - `ScrambleTyper`: per char, cycle 3–4 random geometric glyphs (▯◐◧◑) on alternate frames for 4–6 f, then resolve.
  - `EmphasisWord`: colour `#c0161c` until the next word's start, then interpolate to `#111` over 4 f.
  - `GlyphSwap`: hide one letter, render an `<Img>` at cap height (`height: 0.72em`) with `spring({damping: 12, stiffness: 200, mass: 0.5})` scale 0→1 and a rotate of ±8°.
  - `HeroZoomOut`: scale 4→1 over 16 f with `Easing.bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)`, colour `#000`→`#c0161c` over the first 8 f, plus a dashed `<SelectionBox>` (1 px dashed `#888`, 6 px square handles) fading out at f12–16.
  - `AgentCursor`: an SVG arrow (filled, 2 px white stroke) plus a pill label (`border-radius: 999px; padding: 4px 12px`). Movement uses `interpolate` with `Easing.inOut(Easing.cubic)` over 15–25 f, idle `sin(frame/30)*8 px`, and a click is scale 1→0.85→1 over 4 f.
  - `ColorWashFlash`: pink overlay opacity 0→1 over 4 f, then on the next shot `saturate(3)`→`saturate(1)` over 24 f, `Easing.out(Easing.cubic)`.
  - `ScrollLine`: one flex row of words and icon tiles; translateX = −(width of everything left of the newest word − 0.55·W), interpolated with a spring `{damping: 30, stiffness: 120}` each time a word is added; words older than 2 turn `#a0a0a0`.
  - `LoadingRing`: SVG circle, `strokeDasharray` draw from 0 to 0.85·C over 15 f, stroke `#e0703f`, then fade to `#c9b2b0` and opacity 0 over 8 f.
  - `GlassDock`: `backdrop-filter: blur(20px)`, `background: rgba(255,255,255,0.35)`, `border-radius: 28px`. Enter translateX 40%→0 over 14 f `Easing.out(Easing.exp)`. Hovered icon scale 1→1.25; launch bounce translateY 0→−20→0 ×2 over 18 f.
  - `EndcardLogo`: scale 1.08→1 over 10 f ease-out over a `blur(8px) brightness(1.6) opacity(0.35)` screenshot; subtitle `BlurFadeTyper` at 14 px-equivalent (~2.2% of width).

## 9. Variation ideas
1. **Dev-tool / API launch:** the same white-type + flat-diagram grammar, with request/response packets as coloured pills moving between labelled boxes ("Your app", "API", "DB") and a cursor for each service.
2. **Fintech feature promo:** glyph swaps ($ for S, a chart arrow for an "l"); flat bar charts instead of timeline tracks; a green colour-wash flash for the "money moves automatically" moment.
3. **Team-collaboration SaaS (docs/whiteboards):** multiplayer cursors as the main characters across a flat doc mock. Keep the momentum cuts and the red-then-black emphasis word, and change the brand accent to the product colour.
