# v15 — "Nvidia made the PC obsolete" (tech-news collage explainer, animated on twos)

**Source creator handle:** earn.edits · **Format:** 720×1280, 9:16, 29.97 fps container but **every animated layer updates on twos (~15 fps effective)**: ~50% of frames have diff < 1.5 in every 5 s window, 48.9 s · **Shots:** 14 real (15 detected; 13.95 s is the phone rising into the same shot) · **Avg shot:** ~3.5 s (a new prop or text event every ~0.5 s inside each shot) · **Music BPM:** ~117.5 (bed under VO) · **VO:** yes, English, calm tech-news narrator

**Style tags:** collage-explainer, cutout-photo, device-mockup, ui-mockup, retro-computer-mockup, kinetic-type, giant-keyword, label-badge, typewriter, notification-ui, chat-ui, halftone, grain, vignette, slow-push, zoom-through, spring-overshoot, documentary-vo, cta-endcard, *velocity-matched-cut* (new), *animate-on-twos* (new), *flat-lay-desk* (new)

**One-line essence:** each sentence gets one tactile tabletop or sky "set" built from real photos (cards, stamps, devices, retro UI). The camera never stops breathing: it accelerates into every cut and decelerates out of it. All of it runs on twos, so the clean tech story feels hand-made, like stop-motion.

## 1. Concept & script structure
- **Genre:** tech-news micro-explainer ("old status quo → news event → proof → reframe → punchline → CTA").
- **Beats:**
  - 0.0–7.2 **Hook / status quo:** for 40 years the PC has been the same (mouse, keyboard, screen); same interaction on different hardware.
  - 7.3–8.9 **Turn:** Nvidia just changed that (black void, type only).
  - 8.96–19.0 **Event:** Computex 2026, Jensen Huang unveils RTX Spark, "30 years… packed into slim laptops".
  - 19.0–30.2 **Proof ×2:** it runs 120B-parameter models locally; Dell/Asus/MSI/HP/Lenovo are on board.
  - 30.4–37.8 **Reframe:** AI agents become the interface ("You talk, your PC thinks, then acts").
  - 38.1–44.0 **Punchline:** Nvidia didn't build a faster chip, it made the PC obsolete "and then replaced it".
  - 44.28–48.9 **CTA:** project file on earnedits.com.
- **Hook mechanics (first 1.5 s):** frame 1 is already moving. A beige Macintosh floats in a blue cloud sky and drifts up out of frame (0–0.5 s), the camera "falls" through clouds (0.5–1.3 s), and a grass hill rises from the bottom (1.33 s). There is no text until 1.40 s. The hook is pure motion plus the "40 years" number in the VO.
- **Script → visual rule:** one set per sentence. Inside the set, every listed noun becomes a physical prop that lands on its word (mouse/keyboard/screen → three playing cards; the five OEM names → five postage stamps). Short declarative sentences become type-only cards on a flat void (black, grey or orange).

## 2. Visual language
- **Art direction:** photo collage on literal sets. The sets are a sky with a grass knoll, a red casino felt, a cyan blueprint-grid sky, a navy desk with notebook pages, a green cutting mat, a desk in a room with a wall clock, and a red/green rug with a gingham napkin. Hero props are cut-out photos with soft contact shadows. UI is shown inside real devices: an iPhone, a tablet, a monitor and a framed phone.
- **Backgrounds & textures:** every frame carries a **halftone dot screen** (clearly visible on the sky and in the orange type), fine grain that also steps on twos, and a soft vignette. Flat-lay desks are dressed with *framing clutter* at the edges (crumpled paper balls, paperclips, wired earphones, a spiral notebook, a phone, a keyboard corner), which leaves the centre ~50% empty for the hero element.
- **Colour:** one dominant field per set, with the accent pulled from it. Measured palettes: sky `#8aa8bd`/`#abc1d1`; felt `#541717`; cyan grid `#1f87a2`/`#409aae` + red card `#b83031`; void `#090909`; navy desk `#192146`; green mat `#19431f`; orange prompt `#dd5c13`/`#bb4b0f`; grey void `#d5d5d5`→`#bababa`. The accents are **red `#d42a22` for title type**, **yellow `#f2b322`/`#f7c21b` for numbers and the turn-word "CHANGED"**, and **orange `#f07d1e`** for the final line.
- **Typography:**
  - Hero type: ultra-heavy **wide/extended grotesk** (Archivo Black / Helvetica Neue Black Extended / Druk Wide-like), ALL CAPS, tracking ≈ 0 to −2%, two or three stacked lines.
    - "PC HAS BEEN THE SAME": red `#d42a22` with a ~4 px darker-red offset shadow, ~64% of frame width.
    - "AT COMPUTEX": red `#e8232a`, 3–4 px black outline plus halftone fill, COMPUTEX = **88% width**.
    - "NVIDIA JUST / CHANGED / THAT": white with a soft dark outer glow, CHANGED in yellow, line ≈ 78% width.
    - "AND THEN / REPLACED IT": orange with a visible halftone dot fill, ~72% width.
    - "YOU TALK / YOUR PC THINKS / THEN ACTS": near-black `#141414` with slightly rough ink edges on a light radial gradient. Line 1 ≈ 80% width; lines 2–3 are set at ~0.6× size.
  - Number tiles: "2026" as four separate **white rounded tiles** (each ~15% width, ~12 px radius, soft grey drop shadow). Digits are a heavy rounded sans (Baloo/Fredoka Bold-like) in yellow `#f2b322` with a 2 px black stroke.
  - **Label cards:** a red grid card (`#c81f25`, white 8 px border, 12 px radius, faint lighter-red graph-paper lines, ~80% width) holding white heavy type split by a thin white rule. An **olive denim patch** (`#8a7a1f`) with a yellow dashed stitch and pinked (zig-zag) edges, sitting on top of the stamps.
  - **Script signature:** "Jensen Huang" in a thin monoline script, black, writing on under the 2026 tiles.
  - Diegetic UI type is native to each device: iOS SF Pro alert ("Reminder", blue "Love nvidia" / red "Dont mind"), Win98 MS Sans dialog with a blue title bar, ChatGPT-style greeting "What can I help with ?" in a light grotesk.
  - **Emphasis rule:** colour is used only on the pivot word (yellow CHANGED) or the whole line on the climax (orange). Everything else is white, black or the title red.
- **Layout:** the hero type block is centred at y ≈ 45–55%. Props sit either directly under the type (Mac, stamps) or on top of it, deliberately covering the title (the phone covers COMPUTEX, the Mac covers "THE SAME"). That layered occlusion is the collage signature.
- **Assets:** a Macintosh 128K cut-out; vintage playing cards re-illustrated so that the **Queen holds a mouse, the King holds a keyboard and the Jack holds a monitor**; a dirty beige CRT corner; an iPhone with the Nvidia newsroom; a tablet with ComfyUI and data viz; perforated postage stamps of laptops (Dell XPS 13, ASUS Zenbook 14, MSI Katana 15, HP Pavilion 15, Lenovo IdeaPad); an XP "Bliss" monitor; a Win98 dialog; a ChatGPT prompt box; the earnedits.com site in a wooden frame. There are also orange **4-point sparkle stars** as tiny accents.
- **Finishing:** halftone + grain + vignette everywhere, soft contact shadows (the Mac casts an elliptical blur shadow on the grass), motion blur on the fast entrances (the red card, the phone), and a slight film-gate darkening at the frame edges.

## 4. Motion & timing analysis
- **Pacing:** ~0.29 cuts/s, but the information rate is ~2 events/s. Each shot holds 1.5–6 s while props, cards, words and UI states change every 0.3–0.7 s. Type-only void cards are the short "breath" shots (1.5–1.7 s), apart from shot 11 (3.5 s).
- **Animate on twos:** every movement value is non-zero on alternate frames only (e.g. 0.07→0.47 s, 14.48→17.3 s). The edit was built at ~15 fps and delivered at 29.97. This is the main reason the collage feels tactile and stop-motion-like.
- **Easing signatures (from per_frame.csv):**
  - *Entrances* are ease-out with a long exponential tail: the red card decays from peak to <5% in 22 frames (~0.8 ratio per animated step) with an ~8% overshoot; the phone decays from 4.5 to 0.4 in 28 frames; the scene-settle in shot 10 takes ~35 frames.
  - *Into-cut ramps* are ease-in: zoom velocity grows exponentially for 8–26 frames and peaks on the last frame before the cut (2.94, 5.14, 19.09, 25.09, 28.83).
  - *Long camera moves* are symmetric ease-in-out (the 54-frame phone pull-back peaks exactly at its midpoint).
  - Real speed: flow ×4 (720/180). Typical peak 4–6 → **16–24 px/frame**; the snap zoom at 34.6 hits a flow of 16.5 → ~66 px/frame.
- **Entrance / exit vocabulary:** ghost-fade word reveal (~40% opacity + blur → solid in ~4 frames); card fly-in with rotation and blur; card-drop onto a stack (scale punch); tile pop (2026); script write-on (21 frames); device spring-up with a tilt swing; iOS alert pop; flat-lay stamp slide-in or drop-in on each name; patch slam; letter typewriter with auto-reflow; chat-box typing; dim-to-bright L→R fade (AND THEN). Exits are almost always hard cuts. Things leave the frame only in the hook (the Mac rises out).
- **Camera:** an always-on "breathing" virtual camera. Net zooms per shot run −53 to +0.3 (mostly slow pull-outs that make room for accumulating props); punch-ins only on reveals (the dialog, the chat box).
- **Secondary motion:** the tablet screen cycles UI states; row highlights cycle in the table; preview videos play inside the CTA phone; sparkle stars blink; the grain boils on twos.
- **Sync:**
  - **Cuts land on the first word of each sentence, within ±0.1 s:** 5.14 vs "Same" 5.22 (−0.08); 7.27 vs "Nvidia" 7.30 (−0.03); 8.94 vs "At" 8.96 (−0.02); 13.75 vs "30" 13.84 (−0.09); 19.09 vs "capable" 19.02 (+0.07); 28.83 vs "all" 28.80 (+0.03); 30.43 vs "Instead" 30.40 (+0.03); 34.60 vs "You" 34.58 (+0.02); 38.14 vs "Nvidia" 38.10 (+0.04); 44.28 vs "Editors" 44.28 (0.00).
  - The one exception is 25.09, a stamp scene that lags "Dell" by +0.35 s.
  - Big-type words appear 0.05–0.2 s after their spoken onset ("been" 1.84 → 1.87; "different" 6.28 → 6.31; "changed" 8.04 → 8.17).
  - Typewriter and chat type trail the VO by 0.4–0.9 s and finish before the next cut.
  - Prop drops are frame-accurate to spoken nouns (keyboard 3.76 → 3.74; screen 4.42 → 4.40; ASUS 25.68 → 25.69; HP 27.22 → 27.23).
  - Beat grid (117.5 BPM, 0.51 s): cuts at 7.27, 19.09 and 38.14 land on beats (±0.04); the others follow the VO, not the beat.

## 5. Transitions catalogue
1. **Velocity-matched zoom cut** (2.94, 5.14, 19.09, 25.09, 28.83). The outgoing shot's push-in (or pull-out) accelerates for 8–26 frames, a hard cut lands at peak velocity, and the incoming shot keeps zooming in the same direction while decaying over 10–20 frames. From the cut sheets: at 2.94 the Mac grows ~2.5%/frame for 3 frames, then the Queen card shrinks back in. The cut is invisible as a jolt because the velocity is continuous. Rebuild: `scale = 1 + k·t²` on the last N frames, then `scale = 1.06 − 0.06·(1 − e^(−t/5))` on the first frames of the next shot.
2. **Hard cut to a type-only void** (7.27 black, 34.60 grey gradient, 42.74 black): used at the turn, the reframe and the punchline. On the void the camera is locked.
3. **Hard cut to an empty set, then the hero rises in** (13.75): 2 frames of bare red felt before the phone enters from the bottom at extreme close-up with a +6° rotation kick. It reads as a "drop into frame".
4. **Patch / card slam over the previous layout** (28.83): a new label object lands over the old content while the set stays, so the scene continues and the topic resolves.
5. **Snap zoom into UI** (41.74): ~2× scale in 2 animated frames, −1.5° rot, 12-frame decay. Used to hand the viewer the key phrase.

- Strong onsets line up with physical events, so there are clearly SFX on each landing: Mac drop (1.30 / 1.95), King/Jack card drops (3.715 / 4.342), "DIFFERENT" (6.316), the cut to black (7.291), the 2026 tiles (9.358), the phone (13.886), the stamp drops (25.124 / 25.681), the patch slam (28.793), the scene cut (30.441), the dialog pop (32.067), the punchline cut (42.771) and the CTA (44.164).

- The VO never pauses longer than ~0.4 s. There is no silence beat.

## 7. What makes it high-end (the transferable lessons)
1. **Animate on twos** (15 fps in a 30 fps timeline) plus boiling grain and a halftone screen: digital collage instantly reads as handmade and premium.
2. **Never cut a static camera.** Ramp the zoom into the cut and decay it out the other side (a velocity-matched cut). It gives the seamless "one continuous move" feeling without any transition effect.
3. **Turn list items into props that land on their spoken word** (cards for mouse/keyboard/screen, stamps for brands). Frame-accurate sync on nouns is what makes it feel edited, not just animated.
4. **Occlude your own title.** Let the hero prop overlap the headline type (the phone over COMPUTEX, the Mac over THE SAME) to create depth and collage layering.
5. **Flat-lay desks with edge clutter** (paper balls, clips, earphones, phone, keyboard) frame an empty centre: a reusable "set" for any data or UI moment.
6. **Show tech inside period-appropriate devices** (Mac 128K, Win98 dialog, iOS alert, ChatGPT box). Diegetic UI does the explaining.
7. **Void cards for the turn and the punchline**, with one colour accent on the pivot word.
8. **Auto-fit typewriter blocks:** as lines are added, scale the block down and re-centre it so the composition always fills ~75–80% width.

## 8. Remotion rebuild recipe
- **Composition:** `1080×1920`, `fps={30}`; drive all animation with `const f = Math.floor(frame/2)*2` (on twos). Duration = VO + 0.6 s.
- **Layer stack (typical set):**
  1. `<SetBackground src="felt|grid-sky|navy-desk|cutting-mat" />` (photo, `object-fit: cover`)
  2. `<EdgeClutter items=[…] />` (PNG cut-outs pinned to the corners, soft `drop-shadow(0 12px 18px rgba(0,0,0,.45))`)
  3. `<HeroProp />` / `<DeviceMockup screen={<UIState …/>} />`
  4. `<KeywordStack />`
  5. `<HalftoneOverlay dot={3px} opacity={0.18} blend="multiply" />`
  6. `<GrainOverlay fps={15} opacity={0.08} />`
  7. `<Vignette strength={0.35} />`

  Wrap the whole stack in a `<BreathingCamera>` that applies `transform: scale()`.
- **Components & timings:**
  - `VelocityMatchedCut({dir:'in'|'out', rampFrames: 12–24, decayFrames: 15})`: outgoing `scale = 1 + 0.08·(t/ramp)^2.2`; incoming `scale = 1.06 → 1` via `spring({damping: 30, stiffness: 120, mass: 1})` (no overshoot).
  - `GhostWordReveal`: per word at `wordStart + 2f`: opacity 0.35→1 and blur 6→0 px over 4 frames (`Easing.out(Easing.cubic)`).
  - `CardFlyIn`: from translate(−40%, 30%), rotate −17°, blur 10 px → rest via `spring({damping: 14, stiffness: 140, mass: 0.9})` (gives the ~8% overshoot), ~22 frames.
  - `DeviceSpringUp`: translateY 60%→0, rotate 15°→0, `spring({damping: 16, stiffness: 110})` ≈ 30 frames.
  - `StackDrop`: card scale 1.15→1 + rot ±4° in 4 frames with an `Easing.out(Easing.back(1.4))` curve, plus SFX.
  - `StampGrid`: items keyed to word timestamps; parent `scale` interpolated so the grid always fits 90% width.
  - `DigitTiles`: stagger 2 animated frames, scale 0.6→1 with back-out.
  - `SignatureWriteOn`: SVG path `strokeDashoffset` over 21 frames, linear.
  - `AutoFitTypewriter`: reveal chars at 1 per 3 frames; measure the block and set `scale = min(1, 0.8·W / blockWidth)` with a 6-frame `spring` on scale changes; centre vertically.
  - `PunchIn({at, amount: 0.1})`: 8-frame ease-in / 14-frame ease-out.
  - `SnapZoomToUI({scale: 2, rotKick: -1.5})`: 2 animated frames in, 12-frame exponential decay.
- **Tricky bits:**
  - Halftone: an SVG `<pattern>` of circles, or a CSS `radial-gradient` repeat at 4 px multiplied over the frame.
  - Red outlined title: `-webkit-text-stroke: 4px #000` + `paint-order: stroke fill` + a halftone mask via `background-clip: text`.
  - Diegetic UIs: build them as real HTML (iOS alert, Win98 window, chat box) inside device frames so the text can type live.
