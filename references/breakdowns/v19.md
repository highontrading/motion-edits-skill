**Source creator handle:** earn.edits · **Format:** 720×1280, 9:16, 29.97 fps delivered, **animated on twos (15 drawings/s)**, 44.2 s · **Shots:** 13 real (14 detected; the 28.96→29.10 split is collage tiles popping in, not a cut) · **Avg shot:** ~3.4 s (median ~2.9 s) · **Music BPM:** ~117.5 (low bed under VO) · **VO:** yes, English, calm news-anchor delivery

**Style tags:** collage-explainer, cutout-photo, retro-computer-mockup, ui-mockup, paper-texture, grid-paper, halftone, grain, kinetic-type, typewriter, word-by-word-captions, label-badge, highlighter-marker, 3d-extrude-type, slow-push, documentary-vo, cta-endcard, on-twos-animation, prop-as-text-surface

**One-line essence:** each sentence is typed or set onto a different physical "writing surface" (CRT dialog, postcard, notebook page, corkboard sheet, Mac alert box), so the type always lives inside a real object, and every shot opens with the same expo-out scale settle that gives a 15 fps collage a camera-operated feel.

## 1. Concept & script structure
- **Genre:** tech-news micro-explainer ("bold claim → named product → what it promises → but → doubt → open question → CTA").
- **Beats:**
  - 0.00–4.87 **Hook:** today's most powerful computer "could be obsolete by 2029" (superlative, then a hard date).
  - 4.87–7.21 **Who:** Microsoft "says it knows why."
  - 7.21–9.01 **Name:** "It's called Majorana 2". The title card arrives at 16% of runtime.
  - 9.01–18.95 **Claim:** breakthrough → more reliable, more stable → path to a scalable quantum computer.
  - 18.95–24.36 **Payoff promise:** years of computing solved "in moments".
  - 24.36–32.57 **Turn:** "But there's a catch" → critics → Microsoft's past scrutiny.
  - 32.57–39.47 **Open question + sting:** future or biggest gamble? "By 2029, we'll know" (this echoes the hook's date: a bookend).
  - 39.47–44.18 **CTA:** project file on earnedits.com.
- **Hook mechanics:** frame 0 already moves. A beige Apple II-style computer **hangs from a noose rope off a tree branch** and swings in over a Windows-XP-style sky and meadow (zoom −13 and rot +3° in the first 0.87 s, then a counter-swing of −2.7° from 0.9–1.67 s). Heavy yellow type builds word by word over it. At 2.60 s it hard-cuts to a black void for the threat line. The visual pun ("computer hanged") lands before the viewer has parsed the sentence.
- **Script → visual rule:** one sentence = one "surface". Short punch lines (≤5 words: "COULD BE OBSOLETE BY 2029", "BUT THERE'S A CATCH", "BY 2029 WE'LL KNOW") get a **flat colour void with centred heavy type**. Long informational sentences get a **prop that the words are typed into** (dialog box, postcard, notebook, corkboard). Named entities (Microsoft) get real photos.

## 2. Visual language
- **Art direction:** retro-tech collage. Real photographed objects (Apple II, Macintosh Plus, beige IBM-style PC + CRT, Windows XP "Bliss" wallpaper, VHS tape, cinema tickets, film camera, spiral notebook, corkboard) are cut out and lit as tabletop still-lifes. The 1980s/90s hardware frames a 2020s quantum story, so "old computer vs new computer" becomes the visual subtext.
- **Backgrounds & textures:** XP sky and meadow → black grain void → photo sky → **teal grid paper** (#468289) → **green-painted wood desk** (#192927/#284647) → checkerboard floor with sky backdrop → **dark walnut desk** with newspaper clippings and crumpled paper → **mustard paper with a dark vignette** (#c38c39 centre → darker edges) → open sky → **green cutting-mat grid** → **red plaster wall** (#8b302d) with corkboard → black → **grey grid paper with a torn burlap patch** (CTA). No two consecutive shots share a background colour.
- **Halftone + grain everywhere:** a fine dot halftone screen (~3 px pitch at 720 w) is visible on every photo and on the teal grid, plus film grain and dust specks on the black voids. This one finishing pass ties mixed sources together.
- **Colour:** each scene = one dominant hue + neutral + one accent. Accents: **yellow #f2c230 with red #d8332a offset** (hero claims), **orange #e05d17** (dates/threats: BY 2029, WE'LL KNOW), **brick red #a63d2d** on mustard (the "catch"), **maroon badge #6c1f1e** (criticism), **red marker #c0282a** circle. Hue tracks mood: optimistic sky blue/teal during the claim, warm mustard/red during the doubt section.
- **Typography:**
  - **Hero display:** ultra-black wide grotesk, ALL CAPS, tight leading (~0.85), e.g. *Archivo Black / Montserrat Black / Helvetica Neue Black Extended*. Three treatments:
    1. **Yellow + red offset "print misregistration"**: fill #f2c230, a hard red #d8332a copy offset ~4 px down-right, plus a soft dark drop shadow (THE MOST POWERFUL COMPUTER; MICROSOFT FACED SCRUTINY). Set at 76–85% of frame width. Words in the same block vary in size: FACED is ~1.7× the cap height of MICROSOFT/SCRUTINY (size = emphasis).
    2. **Two-size stack on black**: a small white line (COULD BE OBSOLETE, ~55% width, ~3.5% frame height) over a large orange line (BY 2029, ~70% width, ~6% height).
    3. **Translucent knock-in**: IT KNOWS WHY in red #d81e1e at ~80% opacity with a multiply-like blend, so the sky texture shows through the letters.
  - **White on teal:** IT'S / CALLED / MAJORANA centred, MAJORANA at ~85% width, with a **yellow 12-point starburst sticker** holding a white "2" with a dark outline (≈18% width) stuck on the top-right corner.
  - **Typed-in-prop text:** heavy black grotesk caps inside UI (Win98 dialog "User answer required"; Mac "IMPORTANT MESSAGE" alert with PLAY / START buttons); bold black caps on notebook paper, centred and slightly rotated with the page (~−4°); **calligraphic script** (*Great Vibes / Alex Brush*-like, black) on a stamp-edged postcard; **thin handwritten print** (*Sacramento / Hello Honey*-like) on corkboard lined paper and the yellow sticky note.
  - **Badges:** "MORE RELIABLE" / "MORE STABLE" set white on **red cinema-ticket shapes** (with barcode stub); "OVER QUANTUM CLAIMS BEFORE" set white on a **maroon rectangle with a dashed inner stitch border** (≈90% width).
  - **Emphasis rules:** the newest word is the brightest. Words fade in from ~30% grey to full colour, so a line reads as "being said". Key payoff words get a **hand-drawn red marker ellipse** (SOLVED IN MOMENTS).
- **Layout:** voids and grids centre the type at y ≈ 40–55%. Prop scenes keep the hero object centred at 60–80% frame height with type inside it. In CTA and collage scenes the type sits low (y ≈ 55–75%). Nothing important goes in the bottom 15%.
- **Assets:** Apple II, Macintosh Plus, IBM PC + CRT, XP Bliss, Win98 dialog, Mac System alert, Microsoft HQ photos (4 different), a walking-presenter video clip (Microsoft keynote, keyed onto a white panel), VHS, cinema tickets, Fuji-style camera, spiral notebook, newspaper scraps, crumpled paper balls, corkboard + push-pin + sticky note, red marker, the earnedits.com website screenshot on stacked photo prints.
- **Finishing / FX:** halftone screen, grain, dust, soft contact shadows under every cut-out, a top-light hotspot (vignette brightening the centre of the notebook and mustard card), depth-of-field blur on foreground props (camera, crumpled paper), and heavy motion blur on all camera moves.

## 4. Motion & timing analysis
- **Frame rate:** all motion is **on twos**. In per_frame.csv every moving frame is followed by a near-zero-flow duplicate (flow alternates 4.2 / 0.1 / 3.2 / 0.05…), so the effective rate is 15 drawings/s inside a 29.97 container. This "stepped" feel is part of the collage look. Text fades, typing, camera moves and settles all step on twos.
- **Pacing:** 0.29 cuts/s, shots 1.3–5.7 s. Faster at the hook (2.3–2.6 s shots), slow in the information block (5.4–5.7 s shots carrying typing), and back to short punch cards (1.3 s "catch", 2.1 s "we'll know").
- **Easing signatures (measured on pair-summed flow):**
  - **Shot-open scale settle** (shots 3, 6, 7, 10, 11, 13): motion peaks on the first drawing after the cut (0 frames to peak), then decays exponentially: **50% of travel in ~8 frames, 90% in ~26 frames, done at ~40 frames (1.3 s)**. Scale ≈ 1.08–1.12 → 1.0. This is an expo-out: `Easing.bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` over 40 frames.
  - **In-shot camera moves** (pan to tickets, CRT push, notebook tilt, Mac push): ease-in-out with a longer tail, **18–22 frames to peak and 27–51 frames to settle** → `Easing.bezier(0.45, 0, 0.15, 1)` over 50–70 frames. The corkboard move is front-loaded the other way (46 up / 29 down), so it accelerates into the sticky note.
  - **Pendulum swing** (shot 1): 10 frames up / 15 down per half-swing, alternating rotation sign (+3.0° then −2.7°). That is a damped sine: `rot = 4° · e^(−t/0.6s) · sin(2π t / 1.6s)`.
  - No overshoot appears in camera data (no sign flips in zoom or pan within a move). Overshoot is reserved for stickers (the starburst "2").
- **Entrance / exit vocab:**
  - **Word blur-fade:** opacity 0.3→1, blur ~6 px→0, scale 1.05→1, over ~6 frames per word. The previous words stay; the newest word is visibly dimmer for 2–3 drawings.
  - **Typewriter at speech rate:** per-character typing timed so the last letter lands with the spoken word (postcard 9.11–11.68 for 56 characters ≈ 22 chars/s; dialog and notebook the same). No caret, except for the Mac alert, which reveals word by word.
  - **Slide-out-from-behind:** the ticket badges emerge from under the photo, L→R, ~6–8 frames with motion blur.
  - **Starburst pop:** scale 0→1.1→1 over ~6 frames.
  - **Tile pop-on:** collage photos appear one per drawing (2 frames apart) with no fade.
  - **Marker write-on:** ellipse drawn over ~10 frames.
  - **Torn-paper rise:** a photo with a torn top edge translates up ~60% of frame height in ~8 frames.
  - Exits are always **hard cuts**. Nothing animates out.
- **Camera:** every scene is a 2.5D still-life with a virtual camera: a settle at the start plus 0–1 decisive moves per shot (net zoom −8…−33 at cut-in, +17…+25 for pushes, pan up to −54).
- **Secondary motion:** the hanging computer's swing, the presenter video walking inside the collage, the template-preview video inside the phone on the CTA, and the floating Mac's slow rotation.
- **Sync:**
  - **Cuts ↔ VO:** cuts land on sentence starts within −0.1…+0.05 s (It's 7.26/cut 7.21; Microsoft 9.06/9.01; If 18.92/18.95; By 37.38/37.40; Editors 39.48/39.47). Exceptions: shot 2 cuts 0.34 s after "could" (2.26), so the hook picture holds a beat longer, and shot 10 cuts 0.38 s after "Microsoft" (28.58).
  - **Text ↔ VO:** words appear on the spoken word, 0 to +0.1 s (IT'S/CALLED/MAJORANA, the "2" starburst at 8.35 vs 8.32, typing in sync with speech). Badges driven by a camera move lag by 0.3–0.5 s (MORE RELIABLE about 0.4 s after the words, because the pan comes first).
  - **Beats:** 6 of 12 cuts are within ±0.06 s of a 117.5 BPM beat (4.87, 14.75, 18.95, 24.36, 28.96, 32.57); the mean |offset| is 0.09 s against 0.13 s for chance. Editing is VO-led, and the cut is nudged onto the nearest beat when one is within ~2 frames.

## 5. Transitions catalogue
1. **Hard cut + expo scale settle** (the default, 9 of 12 cuts): the new shot appears at ~110% and eases to 100% over ~40 frames. Rebuild: wrap each scene in `<SettleIn from={1.1} frames={40} easing={bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1)}>`.
2. **Hard cut to black void** (2.60, 37.40): no settle; the type itself does the animating (fade grey→white). Used for the threat and the answer, as bookends.
3. **Torn-paper rise** (4.87–5.2): the cut goes to an empty sky, then the building photo with a ragged white torn edge rises from the bottom over ~8 frames as the frame zooms out. Rebuild: an image with an SVG torn-edge mask translating `y: 60% → 0` over 8 frames with `bezier(0.2,0.9,0.3,1)`.
4. **Empty-stage then collage pop** (28.96–29.2): the cut lands on just the background + keyword; collage tiles snap in one per drawing within ~4 drawings (~0.25 s) during a zoom-out. Rebuild: a stagger of 2 frames per tile, opacity 0→1 instantly, inside a 45-frame zoom-out.
5. **Motion-blur camera move inside a shot** (12.1, 16.4, 34.6): not a transition between scenes but a re-framing within one, used instead of a cut to reveal the next phrase's prop (tickets, sticky note).

## 7. What makes it high-end (the transferable lessons)
1. **Put the words inside an object.** Typing into a dialog, a postcard or a notebook makes plain captions feel like props, and each object adds era or meaning (retro computers for a "future of computing" story).
2. **Two text registers:** short punch lines go on a flat colour void, centred and huge; long informational lines get typed into a prop at speech rate.
3. **Same entrance every shot:** a 40-frame expo-out scale settle (~1.1→1.0) after every cut. It is cheap, consistent, and makes still images feel shot by a camera.
4. **Animate on twos** across everything (type, camera, props). The stepped 15 fps cadence reads as handmade collage, and it halves render cost.
5. **Halftone + grain over all sources** unifies stock photos, UI screenshots and video clips into one printed world.
6. **Bookend the hook:** the date in the hook ("by 2029") returns as the final line on the same black void with the same orange.
7. **Size = emphasis** within a block (FACED ~1.7× its neighbours; BY 2029 ~2× COULD BE OBSOLETE).
8. **Mood through background hue:** cool blues and teal for the promise, mustard and red for the doubt.
9. **Reveal by moving, not by cutting:** a motion-blurred pan or push to a new prop inside the same set keeps continuity during long sentences.

## 8. Remotion rebuild recipe
- **Composition:** `1080×1920`, `30 fps`, duration = VO + 0.3 s. Wrap every animated value in `const f2 = Math.floor(frame/2)*2` so it steps on twos.
- **Layer stack per scene (bottom → top):** `<Backdrop texture="grid|wood|plaster|void" tint>` → `<Prop src shadow>` (cut-out PNGs with `drop-shadow(0 12px 18px rgba(0,0,0,.45))`) → `<PropScreen>` (dialog/postcard/paper area with the text inside, transformed with the prop) → `<HeroType>` → `<Sticker>` → `<HalftoneOverlay pitch={4} opacity={0.18}>` → `<GrainOverlay opacity={0.08}>`. Everything sits inside `<SettleIn>` and `<CameraMove>`.
- **Components:**
  - `SettleIn` — `scale: interpolate(f2, [0, 40], [1.1, 1], {easing: Easing.bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1), extrapolateRight:'clamp'})`.
  - `CameraMove` — keyframed `{x, y, scale, rotate}` with `Easing.bezier(0.45,0,0.15,1)` over 50–70 frames, plus directional blur proportional to velocity (SVG `feGaussianBlur stdDeviation="${v*0.6} 0"` rotated to the motion vector).
  - `WordFadeIn` — words keyed to whisper timestamps; each word `opacity 0.3→1, filter blur(6px)→0, scale 1.05→1` over 6 frames, starting at `wordStart*fps`.
  - `SpeechTypewriter` — `charsVisible = interpolate(frame, [sentenceStart, sentenceEnd], [0, text.length])`, or per-word character spans mapped to each word's start/end so typing finishes with the word.
  - `RetroDialog` (`variant="win98"|"macSystem"`) — title bar `#0a0a8a`→`#1084d0` gradient for win98, 1 px bevel borders, body in a heavy grotesk.
  - `MarkerEllipse` — SVG path, `strokeDashoffset` from length to 0 over 10 frames, stroke `#c0282a`, width ~6 px, slightly rough (two overlapping passes).
  - `TornRise` — image masked with a jagged-top SVG clipPath, `translateY 60%→0` over 8 frames.
  - `TicketBadge` — red rounded rect + barcode stub, slides from `x: -40%` (behind the prop, lower z-index) to 0 over 8 frames with `bezier(0.2,0.9,0.3,1)`.
  - `StarburstSticker` — 12-point polygon, `spring({damping: 9, stiffness: 220, mass: 0.5})` scale.
  - `Pendulum` — `rotate = 4*Math.exp(-t/0.6)*Math.sin(2*Math.PI*t/1.6)` around the rope's top anchor.
- **Type CSS:**
  - Yellow misregistered title: `color:#f2c230; text-shadow: 4px 4px 0 #d8332a, 6px 10px 12px rgba(0,0,0,.35)`.
  - Translucent red: `color: rgba(216,30,30,.8); mix-blend-mode: multiply`.
  - Halftone: a repeating radial-gradient dot pattern at `mix-blend-mode: soft-light`.
