# v22 — "One insult": Lamborghini vs. Ferrari origin story (halftone cut-out collage)

**Source creator handle:** earn.edits · **Format:** 720×1280, 9:16, 15 fps (true 15 fps, only 2 duplicate frames; low-frame-rate "collage" cadence), 32.5 s · **Shots:** 14 real (13 detected cuts; one missed card-swap cut at 17.2 s; the 32.4 s "shot" is the 1-frame black tail) · **Avg shot:** ~2.3 s (median 2.5 s) · **Music BPM:** ~152 (driving bed under VO) · **VO:** yes, English, storyteller narrator

**One-line essence:** archival B/W photos and glossy product cut-outs are printed into one halftone world, and every shot opens with a decelerating push-in "breath". The type style switches per scene (editorial serif, film-subtitle yellow serif, mono typewriter, heavy mono, badge), so a simple anecdote feels like a premium documentary.

## 1. Concept & script structure
- **Genre:** brand-history anecdote ("one-line hook → protagonist → inciting problem → insult → revenge → moral → CTA").
- **Beats:**
  - 0.0–4.4 **Hook:** "One insult created one of the greatest supercar brands in history." (a number + conflict word + superlative)
  - 4.9–9.4 **Setup:** Ferruccio Lamborghini owned a Ferrari but kept having clutch problems.
  - 9.96–12.0 **Action:** he went straight to Enzo Ferrari.
  - 12.9–15.8 **Insult (turn):** Enzo's reply: stick to making tractors (a speech bubble, the only "dialogue" on screen).
  - 16.3–21.2 **Revenge:** he did the opposite and built a car to beat Ferrari.
  - 21.8–26.6 **Moral:** that's how one insult started one of the biggest rivalries in automotive history (the hook phrase repeats as a bookend).
  - 27.7–32.4 **CTA:** grab the After Effects project file on earnedits.com.
- **Hook mechanics:**
  - Frame 0 is already busy: a sepia factory photo with the Lamborghini shield, a small B/W man, and a faint handwritten "You Dumb" script drifting at the bottom.
  - By 0.27 s a sports car and a tractor (cut-outs with thick tan paper outlines) have popped in around him, and a torn-paper label "One" appears. "One Insult" is complete by 0.53 s with a hand-drawn yellow scribble ellipse wrapped around it.
  - The camera pushes in (ease-out, 0.73 s), then accelerates upward into the cut at 1.4 s.
  - The tractor in the hook plants the punchline before it is spoken.
- **Script → visual rule:** each clause gets one scene. Proper nouns become portraits with their typed name (Ferruccio) or brand badges (Ferrari shield + "Fararri" ribbon label). The problem noun becomes an evidence photo ("Clutch" pedals pinned to notebook paper). Verbs become actions (walking, a fist bang, a garage door rolling up).

## 4. Motion & timing analysis
- **Pacing:** 0.4 cuts/s, with shots 1.0–4.5 s (median 2.5 s), which is slower than most earn.edits pieces. The density comes from **in-shot builds** (3–6 events per shot: props popping, text typing, doodles drawing) rather than cuts. The fastest stretch is the hook (1.0–1.4 s shots). The longest holds are the notebook "evidence" (4.07 s) and the moral render (4.47 s).
- **15 fps cadence:** all motion is stepped at 15 fps, which gives a tactile stop-motion collage feel. Walk cycles and glove hands animate on 2s (7.5 fps).
- **Easing signatures (measured):**
  - *Signature "push-in breath" on almost every cut:* the zoom velocity is highest on the first frame after the cut and decays exponentially.
    - Ferruccio: 4.9, 2.4, 1.8, 1.5, 1.3, 1.1, 1.0, 0.9, 0.7, 0.6, 0.5
    - Enzo: 3.5 → 0.3 in 9 frames
    - racetrack: 3.1 → 0.6 in 6 frames
    - phone: 2.7 → 0.4 in 8 frames
    - garage: 9.4 → 0.5 in 16 frames
    - The cut lands mid-move and the camera settles over 0.5–1.1 s. That is `Easing.out(Easing.exp)` with no overshoot.
  - *Exit accelerations:* hook tilt pan_y −0.3, −0.5, −0.7, −0.9, −1.1, −1.3, −1.8, −3.0 (ease-in over 8 frames into the cut).
  - *Mid-shot punch:* the Enzo push is a symmetric ease-in-out bell (rises over 6 frames, peak 4.0, decays over ~12 frames). It is timed so the peak lands ~0.1 s before the punchline word.
  - Zoom-out reveal (History): a ~5-frame rise and ~5-frame fall bell, peak −2.3.
- **Entrance / exit vocabulary:**
  - pop-in cut-outs (1–2 frames, no overshoot)
  - letter-by-letter typing (~1 char/frame at 15 fps ≈ 15 cps)
  - underline that grows with the typing
  - hand-drawn ellipse write-on (6–8 frames)
  - paper sheet slide-up (4–5 frames)
  - torn-ribbon reveal
  - shield pop-up from behind a label
  - fist slam (2 frames down, hold, up)
  - card pop from small+blurred (2 frames)
  - tilted card swap (4 frames to straighten)
  - roller-door lift (4 frames)
  - word blur-in (3 frames)
  - badge-then-text (empty box first, then type)
  - spring-out file icons with rotation
  - glow pop on the end card
- **Camera:** push-ins dominate: net_zoom +18 (hook), +26 (portrait), +41 (Enzo), +59 (garage zoom-through). Zoom-outs are used once for a reveal (History, −8). One lateral parallax scroll (walk, −70) and one upward tilt (hook exit).
- **Secondary motion:** the swinging lamp light, the pulsing anger mark, glove hands on 2s, the walk cycle, "You Dumb" ghost script drifting, and handwriting on the notebook page.
- **Sync (cut / reveal vs. VO word start):**
  - Ferruccio cut 4.87 vs "Ferruccio" 4.90 (−0.03)

## 5. Transitions catalogue
1. **Hard cut into a decelerating push** (most cuts): the incoming shot already moves at peak zoom speed and settles exponentially over 8–16 frames. Rebuild: `scale = interpolate(f, [0, 20], [1.0, 1.08], {easing: Easing.out(Easing.exp)})` at 30 fps.
2. **Accelerating tilt-out** at 1.4 s: pan_y ramps over 8 frames and the cut lands at peak speed.
3. **Layer wipe-over** at 2.27–2.4 s: the sky/verge layer slides down over the paper scene; same prop (car) on both sides, so it reads as one continuous set.
4. **Paper slide-up overlay** at 8.0 s: a notebook page with the evidence photo covers the previous layout inside the same shot (4–5 frames, ease-out, slight rotation).
5. **White flash frame + pop-card** at 16.07–16.2 s: 1 frame brightened (+25 luma), cut to black with a small blurred card that scales to full in 2 frames.
6. **Tilted card swap** at 17.2 s: the new card enters from below at ~8° and straightens in 4 frames.
7. **Door-reveal zoom-through** at 19.27 s: the roller shutter lifts and the camera dives through the doorway (zoom impulse 9.4, decays over 16 frames).
8. **Badge-first reveal** at 25.6 s (and 29.33 s): an empty coloured box appears, then the text types into it.
9. **Glow-pop end card** at 30.73 s: the logo box scales from ~0.95 to 1 with an outer white glow that fades over 3 frames.

## 7. What makes it high-end (the transferable lessons)
1. **One print treatment unifies mismatched sources.** A halftone screen + grain + vignette over archival B/W, glossy product cut-outs and AI renders makes them feel like one printed world.
2. **Every cut lands on a moving camera.** A decaying push-in (exp ease-out, 0.5–1 s) on each new shot makes static photos feel filmed.
3. **Build inside the shot instead of cutting.** 3–6 small events per shot (pop, type, doodle, slide-over) keep density high at only 0.4 cuts/s.
4. **Match the type voice to the source:** editorial italic serif for "history", yellow subtitle serif on archival cards, typewriter mono for facts, comic lettering for the quote, rounded heavy badge for the moral.
5. **Mark evidence physically:** a highlighter tape label, red photo frame and hand-drawn ellipse turn a detail (the clutch) into a clue.
6. **Puppet-animate cut-outs** (walk cycle + parallax set, fist slam, lamp swing) so collage characters act out verbs.
7. **Punch the camera into the punchline.** A bell-shaped push peaking ~0.1 s before the insult word, then the speech bubble.
8. **Plant the payoff in the hook** (a tractor in frame 1, before "tractors" is ever said).
9. **One accent colour (yellow) threads every scene**, against B/W and a single brand red.

## 8. Remotion rebuild recipe
- **Composition:** `1080×1920`. Use `30 fps` but step animation to 15 fps (`Math.floor(frame/2)*2`) for the collage feel. Duration = VO + 0.5 s (~990 frames).
- **Scene stack (bottom → top):**
  1. `<SetBackground src|color texture="paper|grid|lined" />`
  2. `<Cutout src outline={{color:'#b8946a', width:8}} shadow />` (props, characters)
  3. `<PhotoCard src radius={60} />` for archival scenes
  4. `<Caption variant="serifItalic|subtitleSerif|typewriterMono|heavyMono|badge|comic" />`
  5. `<Doodle type="ellipse|scribble|underline" color="#e4c21e|#111" drawOn={7} />`
  6. `<HalftoneOverlay dot={5} opacity={0.18} blend="multiply" />` → `<Grain opacity={0.06} />` → `<Vignette strength={0.35} />` → `<InsetFrame />`
- **Key components & timings (30 fps values):**
  - `PushBreath`: wraps every scene; `scale = 1 + 0.08*(1-Math.exp(-f/6))`; for the zoom-through use 0.25 over 32 frames.
  - `TiltOut`: last 16 frames, `translateY = -220*Math.pow(t,2.2)`.
  - `TypeOn`: 1 char per 2 frames, with an optional `<GrowUnderline>` whose width = measured text width at the current char.
  - `PopIn`: 2-frame scale 0.9 → 1 + opacity 0 → 1, no spring (stop-motion feel).
  - `SlideUpSheet`: translateY 100% → 0 over 8 frames, `Easing.out(Easing.cubic)`, rotate 4° → 0.
  - `FistSlam`: y −60% → 0 in 2 frames, hold 4, back up in 4.
  - `WalkCycle`: a 4-pose sprite on 2s + background `translateX = -14*frame` and foreground `-28*frame` (parallax).
  - `LampSwing`: a rotating cone gradient `rotate = 12*Math.sin(frame/9)` with multiply blend.
  - `PunchPush`: `scale` bell `interpolate(f,[0,12,36],[1,1.06,1.08],{easing:Easing.inOut(Easing.cubic)})` timed so f=12 is 3 frames before the punchline word.
  - `SpeechBubble`: `spring({damping: 12, stiffness: 200})` scale 0 → 1 from the tail point, plus `AngerMark` scale pulse 1 ↔ 1.15 every 8 frames.
  - `PhotoCardPop`: scale 0.85 → 1 + blur 10 → 0 over 4 frames.
  - `CardSwap`: incoming translateY 30% → 0 and rotate 8° → 0 over 8 frames.
  - `ShutterReveal`: translateY of the door 0 → −100% over 8 frames, then `PushBreath` (strong).
  - `WordBlurIn`: blur 10 → 0 over 6 frames with scale 1.05 → 1.
  - `Badge`: box `PopIn`, then `TypeOn` after 4 frames.
  - `GlowPop`: `filter: drop-shadow(0 0 24px #fff)` fading over 6 frames.
- **Halftone:** an SVG pattern of circles with `mix-blend-mode: multiply` at 15–20% opacity, or a fragment shader in `@remotion/three` for true luminance-driven dot size.
- **Text along VO:** drive every `TypeOn`/`WordBlurIn` from Whisper word timestamps (start at `word.s − 2f`). Doodles fire on the keyword's start frame.

## 9. Variation ideas
1. **Tech-founder rivalry** (e.g. a famous email feud): archival press photos on halftone, the insulting email as a speech bubble, a garage-door reveal of the first prototype, and the moral on a heavy-mono type card.
2. **Sports origin story:** cut-out athlete walk/run cycle across a stadium set with parallax, the coach's insult in a comic bubble, a photo-card montage with yellow subtitle serif, and a "Championship history" badge on the final render.
