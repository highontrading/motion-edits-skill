## 1. Concept & script structure
- **Genre:** entrepreneur/business-mindset micro-lesson ("pain → diagnosis → reframe → solution → moral → CTA").
- **Beats:**
  - 0.0–2.9 **Hook:** "Here's why most business owners end up hating their business." (Kinetic type, confetti stars, then an angry-sun emoji on manga speed lines.)
  - 2.96–4.6 **Diagnosis:** the business depends on them (Tony Stark poster collage, a "deal with it" sunglasses gag).
  - 4.98–11.8 **Reframe:** if you can't step away and every decision needs you, you don't own a business, you own a stressful job (iMessage thread → "YOU!" → AI chat typing and reply).
  - 12.2–16.4 **Cause:** founders build everything around themselves instead of systems (desktop of Jobs / Zuckerberg / Gates photo windows → Notes app).
  - 16.48–20.96 **Solution:** "The solution is simple": build systems, hire professionals (grid-paper type → lime pill button → hiring cards with Hire buttons).
  - 21.06–26.4 **Moral:** asset, not a trap; if it can't run without you, it owns you (white minimal type → overhead desk footage).
  - 26.9–32.2 **CTA:** "Just for editors… full open AEP file… earnedits.com… comment Link".
- **Hook mechanics (first 1.5 s):** frame 0 is white with three blurred confetti stars (lime `#b5c77e`, coral `#cf6350`, sky blue) already flying toward camera, which gives depth and motion before any word. "HERE'S" slams in at 0.27 s (VO 0.00), and each following word slides in from the right as the camera **pans along a single line of type** (HERE'S → WHY → MOST → BUSINESS OWNERS), with the stars passing in the foreground. The camera then pulls back to reveal the full two-line lockup.
- **Script → visual rule:** **each sentence picks an interface that would naturally contain it**: a personal message ("if you can't step away") → iMessage; a blunt verdict → an AI chatbot reply; "founders" → a desktop of founder photos; "instead of building systems" → a note being written; "hire professionals" → a hiring UI. The interface then **performs** the words: typing, sending, highlighting or clicking them on the beat of the VO.

## 4. Motion & timing analysis
- **Pacing:** ~16 real shots in 32.3 s (≈0.5 cuts/s). The hook is the fastest part (four looks in 3.7 s: type, emoji, poster, punch-in), then shots stretch to 2–3.3 s as the UI does the "talking". There is no montage burst; the energy comes from **in-shot UI activity** (typing, popping, clicking) rather than cutting.
- **Cut ↔ VO sync:** cuts sit on the **first word of each sentence**: Because −0.03, If +0.05, you −0.01, The −0.01, That's +0.04, If −0.03, without +0.03, hating −0.03. Median ≈0 s, with two deliberate leads (Most −0.17, "of" −0.17) and one lag on the CTA (+0.27). The beat grid (103 BPM) is ignored: offsets to the nearest beat scatter from −0.30 to +0.28 s.
- **Word reveals:** UI-internal words land **0.1–0.2 s after** the spoken word (bubble words +0.13–0.2, sticky labels ≈+0.07, "Simple" ≈0, Hire click ≈0). Live typing runs **0.7–1 char/frame (21–30 cps)** and is timed to span the spoken phrase exactly (chat typing 8.83–9.87 vs VO 8.78–9.86).
- **Easing signatures (per_frame.csv):**
  - **Letterbox close-in** (14.47): luma 199 → 172 → 163 → 158 → 155 → 152 → 150, i.e. **55% / 73% / 84% / 90% / 96% / 100% of the travel by frames 1–6**. Exponential ease-out over 6 frames, the same curve as v78.
  - **Punch-in** (3.73): an instant scale jump, then the zoom rate decays 5.8 → 3.0 → 1.7 → ≈0 over 4 frames (ease-out settle).
  - **Hook zoom** (2.03–2.30): 8 frames at a near-constant high zoom rate (6–10/frame), then a dead stop (0.69). An almost **linear crash-zoom with a hard stop**, followed by a 3-frame whip-tilt (pan_y −4.9 → −1.5 → −0.75, ease-out).
  - **Glitch** (8.73–8.80): 3 frames of flow 33 → 13 → 29 with luma collapsing 147 → 81 → 14, a two-step blackout through pixel blocks.
  - **Footage section:** zero camera motion (diff ≈0.1–0.3). The calm is deliberate, and only the overlays animate.
- **Entrance / exit vocabulary:** slide-in word with motion blur + camera pan along the line; colour-wipe fill inside a word (WHY, "build everything" label); badge pop with a 2-frame overshoot; stacked staggered label slides; object drop with a bounce (sunglasses); bubble grow + words filling inside the bubble; giant sent-bubble pop; live keyboard typing with key pop-ups; send-arrow fly-up; streamed AI reply; window pop/stack; cursor-dragged sticky labels; serif typing in a doc window; text-selection highlight with handles; typewriter + coloured keyword; square → pill stretch; progress-fill pill; cursor click on a Hire button; stroke draw-on swoosh; ghost-caption fade over footage; notification stack rising from the phone; orbiting glowing icons.
- **Camera:** a virtual camera that behaves like a phone/screen recording being filmed: slow pushes (zoom +8…+43 net per shot), tilts that follow newly appearing UI (desktop pan_y +47), 3D perspective tilt on the Notes page, crash-zoom + whip-tilt in the hook, and a lateral glide in the CTA gallery (pan_x −36). - **Secondary motion:** confetti stars drifting through DoF, speed-line flicker, blinking carets, Gboard suggestion bar updates, the AI "thinking" dot, floating out-of-focus Adobe tiles, CTA dust particles and icon glow pulses.

## 5. Transitions catalogue
1. **Hard cut on the sentence's first word**: the default. Each cut also switches interface, palette and type system.
2. **Crash-zoom + whip-tilt** (2.03–2.40): the camera dives 8 frames into the next keyword area, then whips up 3 frames, inside a single canvas. Rebuild: one large `<AbsoluteFill>` canvas, animate `scale` 1 → 1.8 linearly over 8 frames, then `translateY` +18% over 3 frames with `Easing.out(Easing.cubic)` and 10 px vertical blur.
3. **Punch-in** (3.73, 7.87): instant 110–120% scale jump on a word, settling over 4 frames. Rebuild: `scale = 1.12 - 0.12*Easing.out(Easing.cubic)(min(f/4,1))` from the cut frame.
4. **Pixel-block glitch** (8.73–8.80): 3 frames. The white frame breaks into rectangular blocks and the dark scene shows through in blocks, then fully. Rebuild: a mask of 12–20 random rectangles revealing the next scene over 3 frames + 1 frame of RGB offset (±6 px).
5. **Letterbox close-in** (14.47): see v78. Bars 0 → 255 px over 6 frames with exponential ease-out; the scene inside is a UI.
6. **Black strobe** (14.93–15.07): two single black frames separated by a picture frame, during which the page switches from flat to a 3D-tilted, zoomed framing. It hides a framing jump like a camera "reset".
7. **Zoom-out dissolve** (18.85, 29.35): ≈6-frame crossfade while the outgoing scene scales down, so the new scene feels like a wider view of the old one.
8. **Invert flash into footage** (23.57): 1 frame of the outgoing type colour-inverted (white on black, lime → violet `#6d5cff`), 1 frame of the incoming footage inverted (bluish negative, luma 204), then the normal footage. Rebuild: `<Sequence durationInFrames={1}>` with `filter: invert(1)` on each side of the cut.
9. **Stroke draw-on camera follow** (21.9–22.7): the swoosh line draws across the frame and the camera tracks its head, which turns a static type card into motion without moving the text.

## 7. What makes it high-end (the transferable lessons)
1. **Let the interface speak the line.** Put the sentence inside the UI where people would actually read it (text thread, AI chat, notes, notifications) and make the UI perform it in sync with the VO: typing, sending, highlighting, clicking.
2. **Time live typing to the spoken phrase span** (0.7–1 char/frame), so the line finishes typing as the voice finishes it.
3. **Loud hook, quiet body.** Huge ALL-CAPS kinetic type, confetti and a comic emoji for the first 3 s, then a switch to tiny native UI type with one lime keyword. The contrast makes the later lines feel intimate and credible.
4. **One brand accent across all worlds:** lime for buttons, keywords, highlights and the CTA border. Everything else stays in the native colours of each UI.
5. **Visual gags on the diagnosis line** (sunglasses dropping on Tony Stark exactly on "depends") give a memorable beat at almost no cost.
6. **Ground the moral in real footage.** After 20 s of graphics, a locked-off cinematic overhead shot with ghosted captions placed in the scene's negative space (not as subtitles) makes the conclusion feel "real life".
7. **Use cheap, hard transitions** (punch-in, glitch, black strobe, invert flash) and save smooth moves for the camera inside a scene.
8. **Recap inside a UI:** the Notes page body already contains the whole script as body text. The viewer subconsciously sees the argument as "one coherent note".

## 8. Remotion rebuild recipe
- **Composition:** 1080×1920, 30 fps, ≈970 frames (VO length). Scale every px value here by 1.5. Letterbox bars become 383 px each.
- **Data:** VO words JSON → `scenes[]` keyed to sentence starts; each scene receives its `words[]` with frame times for internal sync.
- **Typical scene stack:** `<SceneBG>` (white / grid / UI screenshot / footage) → `<UIMock>` (component-built, not a flat screenshot, so it can animate) → `<Cursor>` → `<Accent>` (pill/highlight) → `<Camera>` wrapper (scale/translate/rotate3d) → `<Letterbox>` → `<Grain opacity={0.05}>` → `<FlashLayer>`.
- **Components:**
  - `<KineticLineHook words={[...]} enterFrames={4} blurPx={14} cameraPan="followActiveWord" font="Gilroy Black" color="#1d4335" />` + `<ConfettiStars count={6} depthBlur={[0,12]} colors={["#b5c77e","#cf6350","#9fd3f0"]} />`.
  - `<SpeedLines rays={48} flickerEvery={2} />` (SVG lines from the centre, random length, re-seeded every 2 frames).
  - `<LabelStack items={["BECAUSE","THE","BUSINESS"]} box="#f39a1e" staggerFrames={3} from="left" />`.
